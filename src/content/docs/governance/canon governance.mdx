## Purpose

This document defines how **Canon** functions within the MOQ Values Matter project.

It covers:
- what Canon is and is not
- the three content status labels and their rules
- scope boundaries for the Atlas, Essays, and Methods
- how Canon is promoted, revised, and deprecated

Canon exists to stabilize **high-quality intellectual patterns** once they have proven coherent, grounded, and explanatorily superior under current conditions.

Canon is not truth, doctrine, or ideology.
It is a mechanism for **preserving intellectual excellence without freezing inquiry**.

<a id="what-canon-is"></a>
## 1. What Canon Is

Canon is a collection of **stabilized intellectual-level static patterns**.

A Canon entry represents:
- a coherent explanation
- grounded in observable reality
- aligned across MOQ levels
- with known limits and failure modes
- judged superior to available alternatives under current conditions

Canon entries persist because they have **earned stability**, not because they are final.

In MOQ terms, Canon entries are **static intellectual patterns**. They are subordinate to physical, biological, and social realities, must respect cross-level constraints, and remain corrigible when higher-quality static patterns emerge.

Canon does not claim metaphysical primacy. It claims **functional excellence under present conditions**.

---

<a id="what-canon-is-not"></a>
## 2. What Canon Is Not

Canon is explicitly **not**:

- Ultimate truth
- Moral approval
- Consensus or popularity
- Institutional authority
- A method or framework
- A container for Dynamic Quality

Dynamic Quality cannot be defined, operationalized, or institutionalized. Canon records *what remains* after Dynamic Quality has already passed and stabilized. For the full Dynamic Quality constraint, see `ai-instructions.mdx` Section 6.

---

<a id="canon-authority"></a>
## 3. Canon Authority

Canon authority derives from:
- coherence
- grounding
- explanatory power
- comparative superiority

It does **not** derive from authorship, institutional backing, ideological alignment, or technical sophistication.

Disagreement with Canon is permitted and may be valuable.
Replacement of Canon requires **higher-quality stabilization**, not argument alone.

<a id="status-labels"></a>
## 4. Content Status Labels

Every text, diagram, or artifact in this project carries **exactly one** of the following status labels. Labels must be declared explicitly at the top of each file:

```mdx
<!-- STATUS: CANON -->
<!-- STATUS: EXPLORATORY -->
<!-- STATUS: APPLICATION -->
```

Without clear labels, exploratory ideas harden prematurely, Canon erodes silently, and disagreements become semantic rather than substantive.

---

<a id="label-canon"></a>
### 4.1 🟥 CANON

Canon artifacts define **binding concepts, terms, structures, and governance rules** for the project.

Canon is normative. Canon terms must be used consistently everywhere else. Canon conflicts override all other content.

**Canon answers:** *"What does this project mean?"*

**Includes:** fundamental laws, Atlas entries that have passed promotion, governance documents, the Canon glossary.

**Rules:**
- Canon cannot be casually edited
- Changes require explicit revision intent and documented rationale
- Canon terms must not be redefined outside Canon governance

---

<a id="label-exploratory"></a>
### 4.2 🟧 EXPLORATORY

Exploratory artifacts are spaces for testing ideas, extending the model, probing edge cases, integrating external perspectives, and speculative application.

Exploratory work is **not binding**.

**Exploratory answers:** *"What might be possible?"*

**Includes:** draft essays, speculative diagrams, cross-framework experiments, early notes, thought experiments.

**Rules:**
- Must not redefine Canon terms
- Must not silently contradict Canon
- If an idea stabilizes, it must be explicitly promoted to Canon — nothing becomes Canon by accident

---

<a id="label-application"></a>
### 4.3 🟦 APPLICATION

Application artifacts adapt Canon for specific audiences or domains — coaching, leadership, education, public communication — by simplifying language **without changing structure**.

**Application answers:** *"How is this used here?"*

**Includes:** executive guides, coaching tools, workshop materials, public-facing summaries.

**Rules:**
- Must conform to Canon
- Simplification is permitted; reinterpretation is not
- If Application reveals ambiguity in Canon, Canon must be clarified — the ambiguity is Canon's problem, not Application's license

---

<a id="conflict-resolution"></a>
### 4.4 Conflict Resolution Between Labels

When two documents conflict:
- Canon overrides Exploratory
- Canon overrides Application
- Exploratory conflicts must be resolved before promotion to Canon
- Application conflicts indicate misuse of Application, not an error in Canon

<a id="scope-boundaries"></a>
## 5. Scope Boundaries

The project distinguishes between stabilized knowledge, exploratory thought, and analytical tooling. These serve different functions and must not be mixed.

---

<a id="scope-atlas"></a>
### 5.1 The Value Atlas

The Value Atlas contains **Canonical Value Patterns**: stabilized intellectual descriptions of recurring value patterns, analyzed across MOQ levels, grounded in observable reality, and governed by Canon rules.

The Atlas exists to answer: *"What value patterns reliably exist, and how do they function across levels?"*

**Belongs in the Atlas:**
- value patterns (not opinions) that recur across contexts, cultures, or systems
- entries suitable for stabilization and comparison
- entries that can plausibly satisfy the Canon Promotion Checklist

Examples of Atlas-eligible patterns: Justice, Loyalty, Trust, Power, Legitimacy, Corruption, Authority.

**Does not belong in the Atlas:**
- speculative theories
- personal reflections
- political advocacy
- methodological debates
- narratives or storytelling
- emerging ideas without stabilization
- unresolved ideological positions

If an entry requires persuasion to be accepted, it does not belong in the Atlas.

> Nothing may enter the Value Atlas unless it can plausibly satisfy the Canon Promotion Checklist.

---

<a id="scope-essays"></a>
### 5.2 Essays

Essays are the **exploratory space** of the project. They exist to think aloud, test interpretations, connect patterns, challenge Canon, synthesize insights, and explore implications.

Essays may be provisional, personal, speculative, argumentative, and incomplete.

**Essays answer:** *"What might this mean? How does this connect? Where could this be wrong?"*

**Belongs in Essays:**
- interpretation of Atlas entries
- critique of Canon patterns
- synthesis across multiple values
- historical or contemporary analysis
- boundary-pushing ideas and unresolved tensions

Essays may reference Canon. They may challenge Canon. They may never redefine Canon.

---

<a id="scope-methods"></a>
### 5.3 Methods

Methods are **intellectual tools**, not truths. They include analytical lenses, scoring heuristics, comparison techniques, diagnostic questions, and structured ways of reasoning.

Methods are static intellectual patterns. They are not Dynamic Quality.

**Methods answer:** *"How are we looking? What does this lens reveal? What does it obscure?"*

Methods may inform Essays and support Atlas analysis. They must not override Canon verdicts, redefine Atlas structure, or claim authority by themselves. Usefulness is not Canon status.

---

<a id="scope-nowhere"></a>
### 5.4 What Belongs Nowhere Yet

Some things do not belong anywhere in the project yet:
- raw intuition without articulation
- unresolved emotional reactions
- slogans or memes
- purely rhetorical positions
- premature abstractions
- ideas that cannot yet be examined

These belong in notebooks, drafts, and conversations — not in the repository. Not everything needs to be published.

---

<a id="scope-enforcement"></a>
### 5.5 Boundary Enforcement Principle

When in doubt:
- Prefer **Essays** over Atlas
- Prefer **not publishing** over misplacing
- Prefer **restraint** over expansion

Moving something into the Atlas is always harder than leaving it outside. This is intentional. Clarity of boundaries is a form of quality.

---

<a id="canon-promotion"></a>
## 6. Canon Promotion

Promotion to Canon is a **deliberate act**, not an automatic consequence of writing.

An entry may be promoted to Canon only if:
- it follows the Canonical Value Atlas Entry structure (10 sections)
- it satisfies all criteria in the Canon Promotion Checklist
- its promotion is explicitly declared and documented

Canon promotion always requires responsibility, justification, and traceability. There are no silent promotions.

For the full promotion criteria, see `canon-promotion-checklist.mdx`.

---

<a id="canon-revision"></a>
## 7. Canon Revision

Canon is **stable, not frozen**.

Revisions to Canon entries are permitted when:
- new evidence emerges
- better explanatory patterns appear
- internal inconsistencies are discovered
- conditions materially change

All Canon revisions must:
- be documented in revision notes
- justify why stability is being disturbed
- preserve traceability of prior versions

Unjustified volatility is treated as quality degradation.

---

<a id="canon-deprecation"></a>
## 8. Canon Deprecation

When a Canon concept is replaced by a superior pattern:
- it must be explicitly marked **Deprecated**
- all references to it must be updated or annotated
- the rationale for deprecation must be documented

Silent removal is not permitted. Deprecated entries remain in the repository as historical record until explicitly archived.

---

<a id="governance-principle"></a>
## 9. Governance Principle

> Canon exists to protect the project from premature certainty, ideological capture, and intellectual drift — not to prevent revision.

The burden of change lies with those who wish to disturb stability.
The burden of justification rises with the level of stability claimed.

Canon is what we stand on.
Exploratory is where we step forward.
Both are necessary. Confusing them is not.
