## Purpose

This checklist defines the criteria required for a Value Atlas entry to be promoted to **Canon** status.

Canon status denotes **intellectual stabilization**, not final truth. An entry is Canon when it has achieved sufficient coherence, grounding, and explanatory power to persist as a high-quality static intellectual pattern under current conditions.

**Status progression:** Draft → Reviewed → Canon

## Governance Context

This checklist is part of the Canon governance system and must be read in conjunction with:

- `canon-governance.mdx` — definition, scope, and authority of Canon
- `canon-value-construction-method.mdx` — how each section must be built
- `ai-instructions.mdx` — rules for AI-assisted drafting and evaluation

Checklist compliance is mandatory for Canon promotion but does not override Canon governance principles. **All criteria below must pass without exception.**

---

## A. Structural Integrity

<a id="a1"></a>
### ☐ A1. Canonical Structure Complete

All **10 canonical sections** are present, ordered correctly, and use exact canonical headings:

1. Neutral Definition
2. Multi-Level Evaluation (0–2 per level)
3. Multi-Level Coherence Analysis
4. Relational Impact Map
5. Persistence Logic
6. Empirical Evidence
7. MOQ Coherence Verdict
8. Summary Schema
9. Explicit Boundary Rejections
10. Canon Status & Revision Notes

No section may be omitted, merged, reordered, or renamed.

**Fail condition:** Missing, altered, or reordered sections.

---

<a id="a2"></a>
### ☐ A2. Internal Coherence

- Definitions, evaluations, analyses, and verdict do not contradict each other.
- Multi-level scores are justified by the coherence analysis.
- The verdict follows logically from all preceding sections.
- No section performs another section's analytical function.

**Fail condition:** Internal inconsistency, unresolved contradiction, or section content leakage.

---

## B. MOQ Integrity

<a id="b1"></a>
### ☐ B1. Cross-Level Respect

- No MOQ level is reduced to another.
- Physical, biological, and social constraints are acknowledged where relevant.
- Intellectual analysis does not override lower-level realities.
- Cross-level causation follows MOQ directionality: lower levels enable higher ones; higher levels may stabilize or distort lower ones but may not generate lower-level recognition.

**Fail condition:** Reductionism, intellectual overreach, or inverted directionality.

---

<a id="b2"></a>
### ☐ B2. Primary Level Declared and Stable

- Exactly one MOQ level is designated as primary (score = 2).
- The primary level is where the value originates, is first stabilized, and performs its core function.
- Level scores (0–2) reflect dominant tendencies, not edge cases.
- Scoring logic is consistent with other Canon entries.
- Context-dependence is explicitly stated if present.

**Fail condition:** Multiple primary levels, arbitrary scoring, or unstable level placement.

---

## C. Reality Anchoring

<a id="c1"></a>
### ☐ C1. Empirical Grounding

- Evidence is drawn from observable domains (history, institutions, behavior, biology, etc.).
- Evidence is framed as observation → pattern → interpretation.
- Examples are representative rather than cherry-picked.
- Speculative claims are clearly marked as such.
- Evidence does not justify moral claims or invoke authority as validation.

**Fail condition:** Purely abstract reasoning, unfalsifiable claims, or evidence used to moralize.

---

<a id="c2"></a>
### ☐ C2. Persistence Logic Plausible

- Clear functional mechanisms explain why the pattern recurs across time or cultures.
- Persistence is not equated with moral legitimacy or tradition.
- Conditions under which the pattern weakens or disappears are identified.

**Fail condition:** Teleological claims, inevitability framing, or persistence confused with popularity.

---

## D. Intellectual Excellence

<a id="d1"></a>
### ☐ D1. Explanatory Gain

- The entry increases orientation, clarity, or understanding beyond what was previously available.
- It explains something non-trivial.
- It integrates multiple observations into a coherent pattern.

**Fail condition:** Redescription without added insight.

---

<a id="d2"></a>
### ☐ D2. Comparative Superiority

- Competing explanations or framings are acknowledged.
- It is clear why this pattern is retained over available alternatives.

**Fail condition:** Unexamined exclusivity or framework capture.

---

<a id="d3"></a>
### ☐ D3. Conceptual Humility

- Boundary conditions are explicitly stated.
- Known failure modes are identified and non-moralized.
- The pattern is, in principle, falsifiable or corrigible.

**Fail condition:** Absolutism, hidden dogma, or absence of failure modes.

---

## E. Boundary Discipline

<a id="e1"></a>
### ☐ E1. Boundary Rejections Present

- Common incorrect framings are explicitly rejected.
- Competing explanations the entry does not adopt are named and explained.
- Rejections are structural, not rhetorical.
- No new positive claims are introduced in this section.

**Fail condition:** Missing boundary rejections, strawmanning, or rhetorical dismissal.

---

## F. Verdict and Readiness

<a id="f1"></a>
### ☐ F1. MOQ Coherence Verdict Settled

- The verdict is clear and conditionally framed where appropriate.
- The verdict states when the value increases coherence and when it degrades it.
- No unresolved internal objections remain.
- The verdict does not introduce new analysis or depend on speculative future developments.

**Fail condition:** Oscillating, provisional, or analytically overloaded verdict.

---

<a id="f2"></a>
### ☐ F2. Summary Schema Complete

- All required schema fields are present: primary level, typical score profile, coherence type, persistence drivers, common failure mode, related patterns.
- Schema elements are comparable across other Canon entries.
- No metaphorical or poetic phrasing.

**Fail condition:** Incomplete, ambiguous, or non-standard schema.

---

<a id="f3"></a>
### ☐ F3. Revision Transparency

- Revision notes are complete and up to date.
- All significant changes leading to Canon status are documented.
- Rationale for Canon promotion is explicitly stated.

**Fail condition:** Silent edits or undocumented promotion path.

---

## Canon Promotion Rule

An entry may be promoted to **Canon** only if **all checklist items pass without exception**.

If any item fails:
- Status remains **Reviewed**
- Failed criteria must be addressed explicitly before re-evaluation
- Partial Canon designation is not permitted

---

## Required Canon Declaration

When an entry is promoted to Canon, append the following text verbatim to its Canon Status & Revision Notes section (Section 10):

> **Canon Declaration:**
> This entry has been evaluated against the Canon Promotion Checklist and is designated Canon on the basis of intellectual coherence, cross-level integrity, empirical grounding, and explanatory stability under current conditions.

---

## Scope Note

This checklist evaluates **intellectual stabilization**, not moral approval, consensus, or institutional endorsement.

Canon entries persist because they remain the best stabilized intellectual patterns available under current conditions — not because they are final or authoritative beyond their demonstrated explanatory power.
