## L-P-02: Entropy

**Statement:**
In isolated systems, energy distributes toward states of greater disorder over time; the total entropy of an isolated system does not decrease.

---

### Function

Entropy describes the directional tendency of isolated physical systems toward states of lower available energy and higher disorder. It is not the absence of pattern but the redistribution of energy across a greater number of equivalent microstates — the system moves toward the configuration that can be achieved in the greatest number of ways.

A precise distinction matters here: isolated systems (no exchange of energy or matter with surroundings) always increase in entropy. Closed systems (energy exchange allowed, no matter exchange) and open systems (both allowed) can locally decrease in entropy by exporting entropy elsewhere. This distinction is critical for understanding higher levels: biological and social systems are open systems that maintain local order precisely by increasing entropy in their environment.

In MOQ terms, entropy is the physical level's mechanism for preventing static patterns from becoming permanently frozen. It ensures that no physical configuration endures indefinitely without active maintenance — all forms eventually redistribute their energy toward equilibrium.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Entropy operates as a baseline condition that all higher levels must actively work against. Biological systems are defined precisely by their capacity to locally resist entropy — maintaining ordered structure by consuming energy from their environment and expelling disorder. This is not a violation of the second law but its consequence: local order is purchased at the cost of greater disorder elsewhere.

Social and intellectual patterns similarly require active maintenance to persist. Institutions, norms, and knowledge frameworks do not hold themselves — they require ongoing effort to sustain against the general tendency toward disorganization. The second law does not cause social or intellectual decay, but it establishes the physical baseline condition that makes active maintenance necessary at every level.

---

### Failure Mode

Within isolated physical systems, entropy increase is not a failure — it is the direction of physical process. The failure mode at higher levels occurs when biological, social, or intellectual systems cease the active maintenance required to sustain their patterns. The result is not entropy in the physical sense, but dissolution of the higher-level structure through the same underlying logic: without energy input to maintain order, patterns degrade. Each level has its own mechanisms of degradation, which should not be described as entropy but as level-appropriate dissolution.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Heat dissipation from hot to cold bodies (thermal equilibration)
- Dissolution of ordered chemical structures over time in isolated conditions
- Radioactive decay toward more stable nuclear configurations
- Increase of disorder in closed mechanical systems over time

---

### MOQ Placement

Entropy is the physical level's complement to conservation. Where conservation establishes that quantity is preserved across transformation, entropy establishes the direction those transformations take: toward greater distribution, greater disorder, greater equilibration. Together they define the physical constraint space within which all higher-level patterns must operate — resources are conserved in quantity but their availability for organized work decreases over time without active renewal.
