## L-P-01: Conservation

**Statement:**
Within closed systems and under conditions of stable spacetime symmetry, energy and matter are neither created nor destroyed — they transform.

---

### Function

Conservation is the foundational physical value pattern: the tendency of reality to preserve what exists rather than allow it to disappear. Physical systems maintain the total quantity of energy and matter across transformations. Nothing is lost; form changes while the underlying quantity persists.

Conservation laws are not independent axioms — they are consequences of symmetry. By Noether's theorem, each conservation law corresponds to a specific symmetry of physical law: conservation of energy follows from time-translation symmetry, conservation of momentum from translational symmetry, conservation of charge from gauge symmetry. This means conservation holds precisely where the relevant symmetries hold, and becomes more complex in contexts — such as cosmological scales in an expanding universe — where those symmetries do not straightforwardly apply.

Within the scales relevant to biological, social, and intellectual patterns, conservation holds reliably and functions as a stable constraint on all higher-level processes.

In MOQ terms, conservation is the physical level's primary expression of static quality: pattern is held not because it is commanded to hold, but because persistence under symmetry is what physical reality selects for at this level.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Conservation operates as a constraint on all higher-level patterns. Biological systems exploit conservation — metabolic cycles store and release energy within conserved quantities. Social and intellectual systems depend on the physical stability that conservation produces without contributing to it.

Conservation does not generate higher-level patterns. It establishes the physical accounting within which higher-level patterns must operate.

---

### Failure Mode

Conservation does not fail within the physical level under the conditions it applies to. What can fail is the higher-level acknowledgment of conservation as a constraint on the substrate. When biological or social patterns operate as if physical resources were unlimited, the result is scarcity, depletion, and eventual degradation of the higher-level pattern itself — but this is a failure of higher-level patterns to honour their substrate, not a failure of conservation. The physical law continues operating; what dissolves is the higher-level structure that depended on a substrate it did not respect.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Conservation of energy in thermodynamic systems (First Law of Thermodynamics)
- Conservation of mass in chemical reactions at classical scales
- Conservation of momentum in mechanical collisions
- Conservation of electric charge in electromagnetic interactions

---

### MOQ Placement

Conservation is the primary physical-level static pattern, grounded in the symmetry structure of physical law rather than asserted as a brute fact. It expresses the first condition of value at the physical level: to exist is to hold pattern within the conditions that symmetry makes possible.
