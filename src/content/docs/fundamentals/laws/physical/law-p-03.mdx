## L-P-03: Relativity

**Statement:**
There is no absolute frame of reference; physical quantities such as position, velocity, simultaneity, and the passage of time are relational — they depend on the reference frame of measurement, not on intrinsic properties of objects in isolation.

---

### Function

Relativity establishes that physical quantities are not absolute properties of isolated objects but relational properties that depend on the reference frame of measurement. The physical world does not consist of objects with fixed intrinsic properties that exist independently of their relations — what is measured depends on the conditions of measurement. The laws governing those relationships are objective and consistent, but the quantities they describe are frame-dependent.

This has a significant implication: the same physical event can be measured differently by observers in different reference frames, and both measurements are equally valid within their frames. Physical law is invariant; physical quantities are not absolute.

A philosophical note: the interpretation that "relation is more fundamental than substance" follows from relativity on a relationist reading of spacetime — the position that spacetime intervals, not substances, are the fundamental physical entities. This is a well-supported but contested position in philosophy of physics. Substantivalism — treating spacetime itself as a substance within which relations occur — remains a live alternative. The entry reflects the relationist reading as the interpretation most consistent with MOQ's relational ontology, without claiming it as the only valid reading.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Relativity is a property of physical reality that higher levels inherit as a constraint. All higher-level patterns exist within spacetime and are subject to relativistic conditions at extreme velocities or gravitational fields — conditions that do not typically affect biological, social, or intellectual processes at ordinary scales, but which establish the physical framework within which those processes occur.

Relativity does not generate higher-level relativism. The observation that physical measurement is frame-dependent does not imply that biological survival, social coordination, or intellectual truth are merely perspectival. Each level has its own standards of correctness that are not undermined by physical frame-dependence.

---

### Failure Mode

Relativity does not fail within the physical level. The failure mode is a philosophical one: misapplying the frame-dependence of physical measurement to argue that all judgments — moral, social, intellectual — are equally valid from their respective perspectives. This is a category error. Physical frame-dependence is a precise mathematical relationship between reference frames under well-defined transformation rules. It does not license general relativism at higher levels, where the relevant standards of correctness are level-appropriate rather than frame-dependent in the physical sense.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Time dilation at high velocities (special relativity)
- Gravitational time dilation near massive objects (general relativity)
- Frame-dependent simultaneity of spatially separated events
- Equivalence of inertial and gravitational mass
- Gravitational lensing of light around massive bodies

---

### MOQ Placement

Relativity establishes at the physical level that measurement is irreducibly relational — a result that is consistent with, though not proof of, MOQ's broader claim that Quality is relational rather than a property of isolated substances. This alignment is treated here as a structural resonance between a physical result and a philosophical framework, not as a derivation of one from the other.
