## L-P-06: Quantum Potential

**Statement:**
At sub-atomic scales, physical systems do not have fully determinate properties prior to interaction — they exist across a range of possible states described by probability distributions, and a specific outcome becomes determinate through measurement or interaction with another system.

---

### Function

Quantum potential describes the fundamental indeterminacy of physical reality at sub-atomic scales. Prior to measurement or interaction, physical quantities such as position and momentum are not hidden facts waiting to be discovered — they are genuinely indeterminate, described by a wave function that encodes the probabilities of different outcomes. A specific value becomes actual through the process of measurement or interaction.

This is not a limitation of current knowledge or measurement precision. Bell's theorem, confirmed by successive experimental tests from Aspect (1982) onward, rules out local hidden variable theories — meaning that quantum indeterminacy cannot be explained away as ignorance of underlying determinate values, at least within local realistic frameworks. The dominant interpretation within physics is that indeterminacy is a feature of physical reality itself, though debate continues across alternative interpretations (many-worlds, pilot wave, relational quantum mechanics, and others). This entry reflects the dominant realist interpretation while acknowledging that the interpretation question is not settled.

In MOQ terms, quantum potential establishes that even at the most foundational physical level, the distinction between potential and actual is real. Static physical patterns — determinate, stable, measurable — emerge from a substrate that retains genuine openness at its base. This is consistent with MOQ's insistence that Dynamic Quality cannot be entirely eliminated by static patterns at any level, without implying that quantum mechanics is the mechanism through which Dynamic Quality operates.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Quantum effects operate at sub-atomic scales and do not directly produce indeterminacy at biological, social, or intellectual levels through any established physical mechanism. Higher-level openness — biological variation, social negotiation, intellectual creativity — has its own level-appropriate sources and must be explained at those levels, not derived from quantum indeterminacy.

The significance of quantum potential for the MOQ framework is structural and philosophical rather than mechanistic: it establishes that physical reality itself is not fully deterministic at its base, which is consistent with a world in which Dynamic Quality genuinely operates rather than being entirely suppressed by static pattern. This is a compatibility claim, not a causal derivation.

---

### Failure Mode

Quantum potential does not fail within its domain. The failure mode is philosophical: misappropriating quantum indeterminacy to justify indeterminacy, free will, or relativism at higher levels. The argument typically runs: "even physics admits uncertainty, therefore social or moral judgments cannot be certain either." This conflates the precise probabilistic structure of quantum mechanics with vague claims about higher-level uncertainty. Quantum effects do not scale to produce social or intellectual indeterminacy — each level has its own sources of openness and its own standards of rigour.

A further failure mode: treating quantum potential as the physical mechanism through which Dynamic Quality operates. Dynamic Quality is not a quantum phenomenon. The compatibility between quantum indeterminacy and MOQ's openness to Dynamic Quality is a structural resonance, not a mechanistic identification.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Wave-particle duality in light and matter (double-slit experiment)
- Quantum superposition prior to measurement
- Probabilistic distribution of particle positions and momenta (Heisenberg uncertainty)
- Quantum entanglement and non-local correlation between particles
- Radioactive decay as a genuinely probabilistic process with no hidden trigger

---

### MOQ Placement

Quantum potential is the sixth physical law and the one most directly relevant to understanding the relationship between static and dynamic at the physical level. It establishes that potential and actuality coexist at the physical base — determinate patterns emerge from a substrate that is not itself fully determinate. This is consistent with MOQ's framework without requiring that framework to make claims about quantum mechanics specifically.

The relationship between L-P-05 and L-P-06 should be read together: lawlike regularity describes the consistent behaviour of physical systems at the scales relevant to higher-level patterns; quantum potential establishes that this regularity is probabilistic rather than deterministic at its base. Neither undermines the other — they describe physical reality at different scales and levels of resolution.
