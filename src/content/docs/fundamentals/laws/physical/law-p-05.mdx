## L-P-05: Lawlike Regularity

**Statement:**
Physical systems behave consistently under identical conditions — the same configuration of physical quantities, governed by the same physical laws, produces the same outcomes. This regularity is not a metaphysical guarantee of causation but an observable structural feature of physical reality.

---

### Function

Lawlike regularity describes the consistent, repeatable behaviour of physical systems: where the same conditions obtain, the same outcomes follow. This is what makes physical reality navigable — prediction, engineering, and systematic knowledge of the physical world all depend on regularity holding across instances.

This entry deliberately avoids the term "causality" as a primary framing. Causality as a concept is philosophically contested at the level of fundamental physics. Bertrand Russell argued in 1912 that the word "cause" does not appear in fundamental physics, which deals instead in functional relationships and differential equations describing how physical quantities evolve. More recently, debates about quantum non-locality, retrocausality in certain quantum interpretations, and the block universe implied by relativity have made causal language in fundamental physics increasingly problematic.

What physical law actually delivers is not causation in the intuitive sense — a producing force that makes effects happen — but lawlike regularity: a consistent functional relationship between physical states across time and space. This is a weaker but more defensible claim, and it is sufficient for the MOQ purpose of establishing the physical level as a reliable substrate for higher-level patterns.

In MOQ terms, lawlike regularity is the physical level's expression of sequential coherence — the pattern holds not just in the present but across the sequence of physical states. It is what makes the physical world a stable substrate rather than an unpredictable flux.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Lawlike regularity at the physical level is a necessary condition for all higher-level patterns. Biological systems depend on physical regularity to function — metabolic processes, neural signalling, and physical development all rely on consistent physical behaviour. Social and intellectual patterns similarly depend on physical regularity as their substrate.

Higher levels exhibit their own forms of sequential coherence — biological consequence, social precedent and outcome, intellectual inference — but these are not reducible to physical regularity. They are level-appropriate expressions of consistent pattern across time, each with their own logic and failure modes.

Crucially, lawlike regularity at the physical level does not determine what higher-level patterns will do. It constrains the physical conditions within which they operate; it does not generate their content or outcomes.

---

### Failure Mode

Lawlike regularity does not fail within its domain at the scales relevant to higher-level patterns. At quantum scales, regularity takes a probabilistic form — the same conditions produce a distribution of outcomes rather than a single determinate result — which is addressed in L-P-06.

The failure mode relevant to this entry is cross-level: applying physical lawlike regularity as a deterministic model to biological, social, or intellectual phenomena. These levels are not mechanically determined by their prior physical states. Biological adaptation, social negotiation, and intellectual discovery involve forms of openness to new pattern that cannot be captured by physical regularity alone. Treating higher levels as if they were closed deterministic systems is a category error — it is intellectual reductionism dressed as rigour.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Consistent prediction of planetary orbits under gravitational equations
- Reproducible outcomes of chemical reactions under controlled conditions
- Consistent behaviour of electromagnetic fields under Maxwell's equations
- Statistical regularity of quantum outcomes across large numbers of measurements

Note: the first three examples are classical approximations that hold at scales relevant to biological and social phenomena. At quantum scales, regularity is probabilistic rather than deterministic — see L-P-06.

---

### MOQ Placement

Lawlike regularity establishes at the physical level that sequential consistency — the reliable relationship between physical states across time — is a fundamental physical value pattern. It is the condition that makes the physical substrate dependable enough to support higher-level patterns without determining what those patterns will do.

The avoidance of "causality" here is not evasion but precision. MOQ itself faces a version of this problem: the claim that higher levels have moral precedence over lower ones is not a causal claim in the physical sense. It is a claim about the ordering of value patterns — which is precisely the kind of claim that lawlike regularity, rather than efficient causation, is capable of grounding.
