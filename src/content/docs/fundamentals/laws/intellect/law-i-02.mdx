## L-I-02: Coherence

**Statement:**
Intellectual quality requires that patterns hold together internally — that within any unified intellectual structure, propositions, inferences, and conclusions do not contradict each other. Coherence operates locally within domains and across domains in tension, not as a single global requirement of unbroken consistency.

---

### Structural Function

Coherence is the condition under which intellectual patterns hold their quality across operations. An intellectual pattern that contains internal contradiction collapses as a pattern of value — anything can be derived from contradiction, which means the pattern no longer constrains thought, which means it has lost the capacity to do intellectual work. Coherence is what allows intellectual patterns to remain stable enough to be used, transmitted, tested, and refined.

The coherence requirement operates at multiple scales. Within a unified argument or theoretical structure, contradiction is not tolerated — the structure must hold together or it falls apart as a pattern. Across domains, however, intellectual patterns routinely operate in productive tension with each other: classical mechanics and quantum mechanics produce different predictions in different domains; mathematical foundations contain known unresolved tensions (Gödel results, set-theoretic paradoxes) that have not destroyed mathematics. The structural requirement is not unbroken global consistency but local coherence within domains and managed tension between them.

In MOQ terms, coherence is what makes intellectual patterns capable of holding quality. A pattern that has lost coherence has not just become incoherent — it has lost intellectual quality. The quality of an intellectual pattern is partly constituted by its coherence; the two are not separate properties.

---

### Structural Universality

Coherence in this structural sense holds for any system that produces intellectual quality, regardless of substrate. Any reasoning system whose patterns contain internal contradiction within a unified structure cannot maintain those patterns as patterns of value. This is not a contingent feature of human cognition — it is a structural condition of what it means for an intellectual pattern to function as a pattern at all.

The mechanisms by which coherence is maintained vary across substrates: logical inference rules in formal systems, consistency-checking in mathematical proof, peer review in scientific communities, internal consistency in artistic and philosophical work. What is universal is that intellectual patterns require some mechanism to surface and address contradiction within their unified structures, or those structures dissolve as patterns of quality.

---

### Failure Mode

The coherence mechanism fails in several distinct ways:

**Tolerated contradiction within unified arguments:** When contradictions are allowed to stand within a single theoretical or argumentative structure, the structure loses its capacity to constrain conclusions. Anything can be derived from contradiction, which means the structure no longer holds intellectual quality — it has become a way of producing arbitrary outputs while appearing structured.

**Spurious coherence across domains:** When local coherence within a domain is mistaken for global coherence across domains, the intellectual pattern becomes brittle when it encounters its actual limits. Domain-specific frameworks treated as universal frameworks fail badly outside their domains while appearing to succeed within them.

**Sterile coherence:** When coherence is achieved by withdrawing from contact with anything that might disturb it — refusing to engage with new evidence, alternative frameworks, or stress tests — the pattern preserves its internal consistency at the cost of correspondence (L-I-03) and revisability (L-I-04). The pattern remains coherent but loses its capacity to track or address reality.

---

### Atlas Coordination

Coherence as a structural axis underlies and constrains many Atlas value patterns:

- [Truth](/atlas/truth/) — the value pattern that requires both internal coherence and external correspondence
- [Integrity](/atlas/integrity/) — the social-level pattern that has its intellectual analogue in coherence between thought and action
- [Faith](/atlas/faith/) — pattern that can either maintain or violate intellectual coherence depending on its content

Coherence is also the structural axis along which the quality of intellectual constructions is most directly evaluated. A theoretical structure that lacks internal coherence cannot be considered high-quality regardless of its surface features.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Logical inference and proof in formal systems
- Mathematical consistency requirements
- Peer review and consistency-checking in scientific communities
- Internal consistency in philosophical and theoretical work
- Coherence-checking in artificial reasoning systems
- Detection and resolution of contradiction in legal interpretation

---

### MOQ Placement

Coherence is the second intellectual structural axis. Where distinction provides the symbolic capacity that makes intellectual patterns possible, coherence provides the internal consistency without which those patterns cannot maintain themselves as patterns of quality. Together with correspondence (L-I-03), coherence establishes the dual condition under which intellectual quality can be sustained: patterns must hold together internally and connect appropriately to what they represent.
