## L-I-04: Revisability

**Statement:**
Intellectual quality requires that patterns retain the capacity to be revised when evidence, argument, or higher-quality alternatives warrant revision. Patterns that have lost this capacity persist as forms but cannot maintain their quality across changing conditions.

---

### Structural Function

Revisability is the condition under which intellectual patterns can maintain their quality over time. Conditions change, evidence accumulates, better patterns emerge, and intellectual patterns that cannot accommodate these developments lose contact with what makes them patterns of value. A pattern that was once high-quality but can no longer be updated becomes a frozen form — coherent perhaps, perhaps even still corresponding to a narrow slice of reality, but unable to track quality as it shifts.

The structural requirement is the *capacity* for revision, not the *active pursuit* of revision at all times. A pattern that is stable across decades because nothing has emerged to warrant its revision is not failing the revisability requirement — it retains the capacity to be revised when something does emerge. A pattern that resists revision in the face of strong warrant is the failure case.

In MOQ terms, revisability is what allows static intellectual patterns to remain static patterns of *quality* rather than merely static patterns. A pattern that has frozen — that can no longer respond to higher-quality alternatives — has stopped tracking quality and become merely a persistent form. The intellectual level operates by elevation toward higher quality; patterns that have lost the capacity to participate in that elevation have lost their place in the intellectual hierarchy.

This is not an invocation of Dynamic Quality as a mechanism. Revisability is a static structural property of intellectual patterns themselves — the capacity built into them, when they are well-formed, to be updated when warranted. What revises them is encounter with evidence, argument, and alternatives — all of which are themselves static patterns. The capacity for revision is what distinguishes living intellectual quality from frozen intellectual form.

---

### Structural Universality

Revisability in this structural sense holds for any system that produces intellectual quality, regardless of substrate. Any reasoning system whose patterns cannot be updated when warrant for revision arises will accumulate forms that no longer track quality. Over time, the system's patterns and reality progressively diverge, and the patterns lose their function as patterns of intellectual value.

The mechanisms vary across substrates and domains: paradigm shifts in scientific communities, doctrinal revision in mature traditions, theoretical refinement in mathematics, learning in artificial reasoning systems. What is universal is the structural requirement that intellectual patterns retain updatability — the form that updatability takes varies enormously, but the requirement does not.

---

### Failure Mode

The revisability mechanism fails in several distinct ways:

**Frozen patterns:** When patterns cannot be revised even in the face of strong warrant — through ideological commitment, institutional protection, or status investment — they persist as forms but lose their quality as patterns. Dogma is the most familiar form of this failure: patterns that have foreclosed their own capacity for revision and now resist all warrants for change.

**Excessive plasticity:** When patterns are revised at every encounter without stability, intellectual quality is lost in a different way. Constant revision produces patterns that cannot accumulate into understanding because they are never stable enough to function as patterns. Revisability is the capacity for warranted revision, not the obligation to revise constantly.

**Fashion-driven revision:** When patterns are revised according to social rather than intellectual warrants — what is currently popular, what is currently rewarded, what is currently fashionable — revision occurs but in the wrong direction. The patterns are responsive to social pressure rather than to evidence or argument, which is a violation of integrity (L-I-05) operating through revisability.

---

### Atlas Coordination

Revisability as a structural axis interacts with several Atlas value patterns:

- [Truth](/atlas/truth/) — value pattern that requires both stability and updatability
- [Faith](/atlas/faith/) — pattern that can operate within or against revisability depending on its content
- [Hope](/atlas/hope/) — orientation toward patterns that may emerge through revision
- [Pride](/atlas/pride/) — value pattern that can either support or block revisability depending on what it is invested in

Revisability is the structural axis along which the difference between living intellectual tradition and dead intellectual form is most directly measured. Traditions that retain the capacity for warranted revision remain alive as patterns of quality; those that have lost it persist as forms but no longer track quality.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Paradigm shifts in scientific knowledge (Kuhn)
- Doctrinal development in long-lived religious and philosophical traditions
- Theoretical refinement and replacement in mathematics
- Learning and updating in artificial reasoning systems
- Legal interpretation evolving through new cases and precedents
- Personal intellectual development through encountering new arguments and evidence

---

### MOQ Placement

Revisability is the fourth intellectual structural axis. It establishes the temporal condition under which intellectual patterns maintain their quality across changing conditions. Where coherence and correspondence describe the conditions under which patterns hold quality at a given time, revisability describes the condition under which they continue holding quality as conditions change.

The relationship to Dynamic Quality is structural rather than mechanistic: revisability is the capacity of static intellectual patterns to participate in elevation toward higher quality. Dynamic Quality is not the mechanism of revision; it is what reveals the warrant for revision when it arises. The capacity to respond to that warrant is the structural property revisability describes.
