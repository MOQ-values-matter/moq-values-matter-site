## Scope

These laws describe the **biological level** of static value patterns: the mechanisms through which living systems maintain organisation, respond to conditions, propagate their patterns, integrate into higher structures, and selectively eliminate what no longer serves viability.

The biological level is distinguished from the physical level by one defining feature: active resistance to entropy. Physical patterns persist passively within conservation constraints. Biological patterns persist only through continuous active maintenance — they are thermodynamically open systems that maintain local order by exporting disorder to their environment.

In MOQ terms, biological patterns have moral precedence over physical patterns: it is right for biological systems to consume and reorganise physical matter in service of life. The biological level is not reducible to the physical level — its patterns have their own logic, their own failure modes, and their own functional requirements.

## The Five Biological Laws

| ID | Law | Core function |
|---|---|---|
| [L-B-01](/fundamentals/laws/biological/law-b-01/) | Preserve Pattern | Active self-maintenance against physical entropy |
| [L-B-02](/fundamentals/laws/biological/law-b-02/) | Adapt | Adjustment to changing conditions — phenotypic plasticity and evolutionary selection |
| [L-B-03](/fundamentals/laws/biological/law-b-03/) | Reproduce | Transmission of heritable pattern across the mortality boundary |
| [L-B-04](/fundamentals/laws/biological/law-b-04/) | Cooperate | Integration into higher-order structures through mutual dependence and division of labour |
| [L-B-05](/fundamentals/laws/biological/law-b-05/) | Renew | Selective elimination of dysfunctional components as condition for continued viability |

---

## The Biological Laws as a System

The five laws are not independent — they describe a functionally integrated system:

- **L-B-01** establishes the baseline: a biological system must actively maintain itself or dissolve.
- **L-B-02** establishes that maintenance must be flexible: conditions change, and fixed patterns that cannot adjust lose viability.
- **L-B-03** establishes that the pattern must extend beyond individual mortality: biological continuity is a population-level property, not an individual one.
- **L-B-04** establishes that isolated biological units can achieve greater functional capacity through integration: cooperation enables what competition alone cannot.
- **L-B-05** establishes that all of the above requires selective elimination: without the removal of dysfunctional components, self-maintenance degrades, adaptation stalls, reproduction accumulates damage, and cooperation is undermined by non-contributing elements.

No biological law operates independently of the others. Failure in any one degrades the function of all.

---

## Why Expansion and Consumption Are Not Separate Laws

Biological behaviours such as growth, resource acquisition, territorial expansion, and competitive exclusion are functional expressions of L-B-01 through L-B-03 under conditions of resource availability — not independent laws.

Under different conditions — resource scarcity, environmental pressure, high predation — contraction, dormancy, mutualism, and withdrawal serve the same underlying biological laws more effectively. Expansion is a context-dependent strategy, not a universal biological value.

## Biological Laws and the MOQ Hierarchy

The biological level enables the social level by producing organisms capable of coordination, learning, and collective behaviour — but does not generate social patterns directly. Social patterns emerge when biological cooperation reaches sufficient scale and complexity that shared meaning, norm enforcement, and role differentiation become the primary mechanisms of group organisation.

| Level | Mode | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Passive persistence within conservation constraints | — |
| Biological | Active resistance to entropy through self-maintenance | Physical substrate |
| Social | Coordination through shared meaning and enforcement | Biological organisms capable of cooperation |
| Intellectual | Abstraction, coherence-testing, error correction | Social structures that protect intellectual activity |
