## L-B-01: Preserve Pattern

**Statement:**
A biological system must actively maintain its own organisation against the continuous pressure of physical entropy — sustaining internal conditions within functional limits through ongoing energy expenditure.

---

### Function

Biological self-maintenance is the defining characteristic that distinguishes living systems from physical ones. Where physical patterns persist passively within conservation constraints, biological patterns require active work to persist. A living system continuously expends energy to maintain temperature, chemical gradients, structural integrity, and functional organisation. When that active maintenance stops, physical entropy proceeds and the biological pattern dissolves.

This is not stasis. Biological self-maintenance is dynamic — the organism is constantly repairing, replacing, and regulating its components while preserving the overall pattern. The same cells are not present from one year to the next; the same organism is. The pattern persists through continuous renewal of its material substrate.

In MOQ terms, biological self-maintenance is the primary static biological value pattern: the most fundamental expression of what it means for life to exist as a distinct level of reality above the physical. It is the condition on which all other biological laws depend.

---

### Cross-Level Grounding

Biological self-maintenance depends entirely on the physical level — it operates by exploiting physical conservation, working against entropy by exporting disorder to the environment, and functioning within the constraints of physical lawlike regularity. No biological self-maintenance is possible without a stable physical substrate.

Biological self-maintenance does not generate social or intellectual patterns. It is the necessary but not sufficient condition for them. Social and intellectual patterns emerge from biological systems capable of self-maintenance, but they are not produced by self-maintenance alone.

---

### Failure Mode

Biological self-maintenance fails when the energy or material inputs required to sustain internal organisation are insufficient, when damage accumulates faster than repair mechanisms can address it, or when regulatory systems lose the capacity to maintain functional limits. At the individual level this produces illness, injury, and death. At the population level it produces local extinction. At the ecosystem level it produces collapse of dependent biological patterns.

The failure is not entropy itself — entropy is the physical baseline. The failure is the breakdown of the biological mechanisms that locally resist entropy.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Homeostasis — maintenance of temperature, pH, and osmotic balance within functional ranges
- Immune response — identification and elimination of threats to biological integrity
- Metabolic regulation — continuous energy management across all living systems
- Tissue repair — replacement of damaged structural components
- Cellular turnover — programmed replacement of cells throughout organism lifespan

---

### MOQ Placement

Preserve Pattern is the foundational biological law. It establishes that the biological level is defined by active resistance to physical dissolution — not as a philosophical claim but as a functional condition. All other biological laws — adaptation, reproduction, cooperation, renewal — operate within and depend upon the capacity for self-maintenance that this law describes.
