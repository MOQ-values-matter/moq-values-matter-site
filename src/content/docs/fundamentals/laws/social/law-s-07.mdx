## L-S-07: Shared Value Representation

**Statement:**
Any system of multiple coordinating agents whose stability depends on members responding coherently to non-routine situations requires a shared representation of the group's value configuration — a collectively held way of understanding identity, value, threat, and meaning — that members can refer to when established procedures do not apply.

---

### Structural Function

Shared value representation is the structural condition under which multi-agent coordination remains coherent under conditions that the operational mechanisms (hierarchy, commitment, reciprocity, protection, continuity, sanction) cannot directly address. Established procedures cover routine situations; they cannot cover every novel circumstance the group will face. Without a shared representation of what the group values, members confronting non-routine conditions interpret events differently, propose incompatible responses, and the coordination that operational mechanisms maintained dissolves under stress.

The shared value representation provides:

**Collective identity** — what makes "us" different from others, who counts as a member, what membership obligates
**Value orientation** — what the group treats as worth preserving, what it treats as threat, what it treats as opportunity
**Boundary interpretation** — how to recognise threats to the group, how to respond to outsiders, when to extend the boundary
**Meaning under stress** — how to interpret loss, sacrifice, betrayal, and disorder when they occur
**Justification** — why the group's hierarchy, sanctions, and obligations are what they are

The mechanism operates as the configuration of values the group holds collectively, transmitted and reinforced through the patterns members enact together — narratives, rituals, gossip, ceremonies, founding stories, ongoing interpretive practice. The representation is not located in any individual member; it exists as the collective pattern that members reference, sustain, and modify through participation.

A critical distinction: shared value representation is structurally distinct from shared belief. Members of a coherent group do not necessarily believe identical propositions about their group's representation; they share it as the framework within which they interpret events. The Atlas Faith entry handles the related but distinct phenomenon of belief; this law is structurally about shared value representation whether or not its content is held as factual.

The rain dance example illustrates this directly. A group dancing to invoke rain through gods they share belief in is operating at the social level. The dance works as social coordination regardless of meteorology. The shared representation — that the gods exist, that the dance honours them, that the group is the kind of group that performs this dance — produces collective identity, value orientation, and coordinated action under non-routine conditions (drought). The intellectual-level question of whether the gods exist is a separate question at a separate level. The social mechanism does not require accuracy to function; it requires sharedness.

In MOQ terms, shared value representation is what holds the social-level static patterns together as a coherent system. The other six social laws describe operational mechanisms; this law describes the value configuration that makes those mechanisms make sense to members and motivates their participation when individual cost-benefit calculation alone would not.

---

### Structural Universality

Shared value representation in this structural sense passes the universe test. Any multi-agent coordinating system facing non-routine conditions requires a shared configuration of values its members can reference, regardless of substrate or evolution path.

The mechanisms vary across systems and across the value patterns that instantiate the requirement:

- Origin narratives in human societies, both religious and secular
- Founding stories in institutions, organisations, and movements
- Identity-defining histories in ethnic, national, and ideological groups
- Operating principles and codified mission statements in formal organisations
- Symbolic systems and ritual practice in traditional societies
- Gossip and informal storytelling at small-group scale, where the same mechanism operates without formalisation
- Family lore transmitted across generations
- Office stories and organisational legends within working groups

What is universal is the requirement: coordinating systems that cannot maintain shared value representation cannot respond coherently to non-routine conditions, and cannot motivate the commitment and sacrifice that operational mechanisms alone cannot produce. The forms vary by scale and formalisation; the structural mechanism is consistent.

The persistence of any specific shared value representation depends on its capacity to provide meaningful interpretation of group experience. Representations that fail to interpret members' actual experience under stress lose their binding function, regardless of how authoritatively they are taught. This is the social-level analogue of the empirical correspondence test that operates at the intellectual level (L-I-03) — but it operates through felt resonance and lived experience rather than through propositional verification. A representation that does not feel true under stress dissolves; one that does feel true under stress can persist for centuries.

---

### Failure Mode

The shared value representation mechanism fails in several distinct directions:

**Meaning collapse:** When the shared representation loses its capacity to make sense of members' actual experience — through accumulated contradiction, loss of plausibility, or failure to address conditions members actually face — members lose the basis for coherent collective response to non-routine situations. The group fragments into sub-groups with different value configurations, or dissolves into individuals operating without shared meaning.

**Rigidity:** When the representation becomes so fixed that it cannot accommodate new conditions, it stops serving its structural function. Members confronting genuinely novel circumstances find that the representation has no resources for interpreting them, and either abandon the representation or impose it on situations it cannot handle. Both responses degrade coordination.

**Capture:** When the representation becomes the instrument of particular interests within the group rather than the shared resource of the group as a whole, it loses legitimacy. Members observe that the representation is being used to justify outcomes that serve some members at the cost of others, and its structural function as common reference point degrades.

**Substitution of belief for shared participation:** When the representation is operated as if it required propositional belief in its content rather than shared interpretive participation, members who cannot believe propositions are excluded from coordination. The representation becomes a test of conformity rather than a resource for collective interpretation, narrowing the group it can hold together.

---

### Atlas Coordination

The shared value representation axis is instantiated in human societies through multiple value patterns analysed in the Atlas:

- [Faith](/atlas/faith/) — belief-based commitment within shared value representation
- [Hope](/atlas/hope/) — orientation toward possibilities articulated in shared representation
- [Honor](/atlas/honor/) — reputation interpreted within shared value framework
- [Truth](/atlas/truth/) — intellectual-level pattern that can either reinforce or destabilise shared representation
- [Beauty](/atlas/beauty/) — aesthetic dimension through which shared representation often operates
- [Celebrity](/atlas/celebrity/) — modern attention pattern that has partly displaced traditional representational figures

A note on Myth as a value pattern: "myth" in the strong, formalised sense — large-scale foundational narrative with cosmological scope — is one specific instantiation of shared value representation, operating at high stakes and across long time horizons. It is a candidate Atlas value pattern in its own right, but it is one position along this axis rather than the entire axis. The same structural mechanism operates at much smaller scales (gossip, family lore, office stories) and through non-narrative forms (rituals, symbolic systems, shared aesthetic practice).

The relationship between shared value representation and the other social structural axes is foundational: it provides the value configuration within which hierarchy is recognised as legitimate, commitment is felt as belonging, reciprocity is understood as fair, protection is recognised as defending what matters, continuity is felt as preserving identity, and sanction is experienced as just rather than arbitrary. Without shared value representation, the other six structural axes operate as bare mechanisms; with it, they cohere into a meaningful pattern that members participate in rather than merely comply with.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Origin narratives in religious and secular traditions
- National and ethnic identity narratives
- Founding stories in institutions, organisations, and movements
- Symbolic systems including flags, rituals, sacred objects, and shared aesthetic traditions
- Operating representations in modern organisations (mission, vision, founder narratives)
- Gossip and informal storytelling as small-scale instantiations of the same structural mechanism
- Narrative cognition documented in cognitive science of religion and anthropology

---

### MOQ Placement

Shared value representation is the seventh and integrating social structural axis. Where the previous six laws describe operational mechanisms — authority, commitment, exchange, defence, transmission, enforcement — this law describes the value configuration within which those mechanisms cohere into a meaningful system. It is the social-level pattern most easily mistaken for an intellectual-level pattern, but the project's strict level discipline places it firmly at the social level: shared value representation operates through felt resonance and shared participation, not through propositional verification.

This placement is one of the project's most important boundary commitments. Treating shared value representation as intellectual rather than social produces systematic misanalysis of social pattern — confusing the question of whether a representation is structurally functional with the question of whether its content is empirically accurate. These are distinct questions operating at distinct levels.

The renaming of this law from "Myth" to "Shared Value Representation" reflects two architectural principles: laws name structural axes rather than specific value patterns located within them, and law names should use vocabulary appropriate to their level. Myth as a strong-form Atlas pattern remains available; the structural axis is broader than any single instantiation and deserves a name that does not imply truth-status concerns or limit the mechanism to its formalised-narrative form.
