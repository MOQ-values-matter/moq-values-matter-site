## L-S-02: Commitment

**Statement:**
Any system of multiple coordinating agents whose continuation depends on members preferring the group's interests over their own short-term advantage requires structural mechanisms that produce that preference reliably enough to keep defection rates below the threshold for coordination collapse.

---

### Structural Function

Commitment is the structural condition under which group coordination remains stable when individual short-term interest would otherwise drive defection. In any multi-agent system where coordination produces collective benefits, individual members can in principle defect — extracting the benefits while avoiding the costs. If defection rates exceed the threshold the system can absorb, coordination collapses. Commitment is the structural mechanism by which defection rates are kept below that threshold.

The mechanism operates through preference that is not contingent on moment-to-moment individual calculation. A member who recalculates their group-preference against immediate self-interest in every situation is not committed — they are an opportunistic cooperator whose participation depends on continuous favourable conditions. Commitment as a structural pattern produces preference that persists through periods of unfavourable individual conditions, accepting short-term costs because the larger pattern is valued above immediate calculation.

In MOQ terms, commitment is what allows social coordination to extend beyond the direct biological mechanisms — kin selection, immediate reciprocity — that produce cooperation at the biological level. Without commitment as a structural mechanism, social-level coordination collapses back to biological-level cooperation among genetically related or directly reciprocating individuals.

---

### Structural Universality

Commitment in this structural sense passes the universe test. Any multi-agent coordinating system whose continuation depends on members bearing short-term costs for collective benefit requires some mechanism that reliably produces this preference — regardless of substrate or evolution path.

The mechanisms vary across systems and across the value patterns that instantiate commitment:

- Kin selection produces commitment in genetically related groups (biological foundation, but extends into the social level when reinforced by cultural mechanisms)
- Reciprocal altruism produces commitment among individuals with sustained interaction
- Identity-based bonding produces commitment among individuals who categorise themselves as belonging to a shared category
- Ideologically grounded conviction produces commitment around shared symbolic systems
- Costly signalling produces commitment by making defection visible and reputationally damaging
- Sworn oath and formal vow produce commitment through ritualised public undertaking

What is universal is the structural requirement: coordinating systems that cannot keep defection rates below the collapse threshold do not persist as coordinating systems. Different mechanisms achieve this in different cases, and different value patterns express commitment in different forms.

---

### Failure Mode

The commitment mechanism fails in two directions:

**Insufficient commitment:** When mechanisms producing group-preference are too weak to keep defection rates below the collapse threshold, the group fragments into smaller units capable of coordination through direct biological mechanisms, or dissolves entirely. Members extract benefits while avoiding costs until cooperation is no longer rational for anyone.

**Excessive commitment:** When mechanisms become unconditional, members continue contributing to groups that no longer serve any structural purpose, or to leaders and institutions that exploit that commitment. The group persists past the point where it serves the structural requirements that commitment was supposed to enable. This produces dysfunction in the form of cult-like attachment, blind obedience, and the perpetuation of failing institutions through sunk-cost commitment.

Effective commitment operates between these failure modes — sufficient group-preference to keep defection rates below collapse threshold, conditional enough to withdraw when the group no longer serves its structural function.

---

### Atlas Coordination

The commitment axis is instantiated in human societies through multiple value patterns analysed in the Atlas:

- [Loyalty](/atlas/loyalty/) — the most familiar commitment pattern, operating through identification with a specific group, leader, or cause
- [Solidarity](/atlas/solidarity/) — commitment operating across larger group scales through shared category identification
- [Treason](/atlas/treason/) — the specific failure mode of commitment breakdown
- [Faith](/atlas/faith/) — commitment under conditions of incomplete information
- [Sacrifice](/atlas/sacrifice/) — the cost-bearing dimension of commitment made explicit
- [Duty](/atlas/duty/) — commitment to role-defined obligations within institutional structure

Different Atlas value patterns occupy different positions along the commitment axis. Loyalty is one specific instantiation; solidarity operates at different scale; faith operates under different epistemic conditions; sacrifice expresses the cost dimension. None of these patterns is the entire axis — each is a position within the dimensional space the law defines.

Hope and Faith support commitment under uncertainty; Pride can either reinforce or undermine it depending on whether the pride is in the group or in the individual.

---

### Observable Expressions

- Kin commitment across human and non-human social species
- Institutional commitment in long-term employment, military service, and professional engagements
- Identity-based commitment in ethnic, religious, and ideological groups
- Costly signalling through behaviours that would not be undertaken by defectors
- Group-defining initiation rituals that make commitment visible and difficult to fake
- Sworn oath traditions across cultures, from medieval feudal vows to contemporary citizenship and professional oaths

---

### MOQ Placement

Commitment is the second social structural axis. It establishes the group-preference condition without which social coordination cannot extend beyond direct biological mechanisms. Where hierarchy provides the asymmetric decision structure, commitment provides the preference structure that keeps members within the hierarchy when individual interest would otherwise drive them out.

The renaming of this law from "Loyalty" to "Commitment" reflects an architectural principle: laws name structural axes, while Atlas entries name specific value patterns located within the dimensional space the laws define. Loyalty is one of several value patterns that operate along this axis; the axis itself requires a name that does not collide with any single instantiation.
