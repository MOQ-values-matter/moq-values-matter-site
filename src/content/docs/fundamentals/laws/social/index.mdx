## Scope

These laws describe the **social level** of static value patterns: the structural conditions that any system of multi-agent coordination must satisfy to persist beyond what direct biological mechanisms can produce.

The social level is distinguished from the biological level by the type of cooperation it enables. Biological cooperation operates through direct mechanisms — genetic relatedness, immediate reciprocity, kin recognition. Social coordination extends beyond these direct mechanisms through structured authority, sustained commitment, predictable obligation patterns, organised defence, transmission across generations, enforcement of expectations, and shared interpretive framework.

In MOQ terms, the social level has moral precedence over the biological level: social patterns can rightly override biological impulse when group survival requires it. The social level is not reducible to the biological level — its patterns have their own logic, their own failure modes, and their own structural requirements.

→ For the MOQ level context, see [Social Level](/fundamentals/glossary/social-level/)

## What These Laws Describe

The social laws are **structural axes**, not descriptions of human social patterns. They describe conditions that any system of multi-agent coordination must satisfy to persist — regardless of whether the agents are humans, other social species, or hypothetical alien or artificial coordinating systems.

This distinction matters because the project uses the laws as a dimensional space within which value patterns are evaluated. The Atlas entries describe specific value patterns that arise in human societies; the laws describe the structural axes along which those patterns are scored. A value pattern can serve hierarchy at the cost of reciprocity, or strengthen commitment at the cost of shared value representation — and the resulting profile across the seven structural axes constitutes a measure of the pattern's overall quality at the social level.

Each law is presented through:

- **Statement** — the structural condition expressed universally
- **Structural Function** — what the axis does at the social level
- **Structural Universality** — why the requirement holds beyond the human case (the universe test)
- **Failure Mode** — how the structural mechanism breaks down
- **Atlas Coordination** — which Atlas value patterns instantiate or interact with this axis
- **Observable Expressions** — specific instances of the pattern in human and non-human systems
- **MOQ Placement** — the law's position within the social-level structure

## The Social Laws as a System

The seven laws describe a structurally integrated system of conditions for multi-agent coordination:

**Operational mechanisms (L-S-01 through L-S-06):** Hierarchy provides asymmetric decision authority; Commitment maintains group-preference over time; Reciprocity makes sustained exchange tractable; Protection secures member survival; Continuity transmits pattern across generations; Sanction enforces expectations against deviation. These six describe what any coordinating system must do.

**Integrating framework (L-S-07):** Shared Value Representation provides the value configuration within which the operational mechanisms cohere into a meaningful system rather than a set of independent constraints. Members participate in coordination because the operational mechanisms make sense within a shared understanding of identity, value, and meaning.

No social law operates independently of the others. A coordinating system that satisfies hierarchy but fails reciprocity becomes structurally exploitative; one that maintains sanction but fails shared value representation becomes coercive without legitimacy; one that achieves continuity but fails adaptation becomes brittle. Quality at the social level is measured by how well a value pattern serves multiple structural axes simultaneously without trading them off.

## Atlas Entries as Located Within Structural Space

The relationship between social laws and Atlas value patterns is dimensional. The laws define the structural space; the Atlas entries are positions within it. A value pattern's quality at the social level is its profile across the seven structural axes:

- A pattern that serves hierarchy, commitment, reciprocity, protection, continuity, sanction, and shared value representation simultaneously is a high-quality social pattern
- A pattern that serves some axes at the cost of others is a lower-quality pattern that produces specific structural distortions
- A pattern that serves no structural axis is not a social pattern at all but something else (perhaps a biological pattern, an intellectual pattern, or a pathology)

This is what the project's scoring methodology captures: a pattern's quality is not a single number but a structural profile. Coercion serves hierarchy and sanction at significant cost to reciprocity and loyalty; Authority serves hierarchy and sanction with much smaller cost. The structural profiles differ; the quality assessment differs accordingly.

## Why the Social Level Has Moral Precedence Over the Biological Level

None of these laws depend on abstract reasoning, ideology, or truth-seeking. They are **structural patterns of coordination** that operate prior to and independently of intellectual development. They predate philosophy, religion, democracy, and science as social phenomena because they are the structural conditions that any persistent social organisation must satisfy.

When the intellectual level emerges, it begins to question these patterns — reason against authority, justice against commitment, truth against tradition. These are legitimate intellectual challenges, but they rest upon and depend upon the social substrate they critique. A society that has degraded its hierarchical, committed, reciprocal, protective, continuous, sanction-enforced, value-representing foundations cannot sustain the intellectual life that critiques them.

This is why MOQ places intellectual above social only conditionally: the intellectual level has moral precedence over the social level only when the social level is functioning. A social system in collapse cannot be repaired primarily through intellectual means; the structural conditions must be addressed at the level where they actually operate.

## Hierarchy of Levels

| Level | Mode | Depends on |
|---|---|---|
| Physical | Passive persistence within conservation constraints | — |
| Biological | Active resistance to entropy through self-maintenance | Physical substrate |
| Social | Multi-agent coordination through structural axes | Biological organisms capable of cooperation |
| Intellectual | Abstraction, coherence-testing, error correction | Social structures that protect intellectual activity |
