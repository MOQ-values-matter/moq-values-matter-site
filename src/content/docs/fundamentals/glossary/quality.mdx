## Quality

**Quality** is the primary reality from which both subjects and objects arise — prior to any division between mind and matter, observer and observed.

Quality is not a property that objects possess, nor a judgment that subjects project. It is experienced directly, before it is named, categorized, or explained. The recognition of Quality precedes its description.

Quality is **not**:
- a subjective opinion or personal preference
- an objective property measurable by instruments
- a standard of excellence defined by experts or institutions
- reducible to either matter or mind

In conventional thinking, quality is typically treated as either subjective ("I like it") or objective ("it meets the specification"). Pirsig's insight is that both of these are secondary — derivative of a more fundamental reality that precedes the subject-object split entirely.

Within this project, Quality functions as the **ontological ground** — the reason value is real rather than constructed, and the reason the MOQ hierarchy is a moral hierarchy rather than a descriptive taxonomy. All four static levels are levels of Quality, ordered by their capacity to preserve and expand what is genuinely valuable.

Quality is not defined further here. Attempting to define it fully is, in Pirsig's terms, precisely what fails — because any complete definition would make Quality into an object, which it is not.
