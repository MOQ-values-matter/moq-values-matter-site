## Static Patterns

**Static patterns** are stabilized forms of value that emerge from Dynamic Quality and persist over time. They are what remains after novelty has been recognized, selected, and preserved — the accumulated record of what has worked across the four levels of reality.

Static patterns:
- preserve what has proven valuable
- provide continuity and predictability
- resist change in order to protect what has been earned
- make coordinated life, society, and thought possible

Static patterns exist at four distinct levels — physical, biological, social, and intellectual — each with its own logic of persistence and its own relationship to the levels above and below it.

Static patterns are **not**:
- mere habit or inertia
- rigid rules imposed from outside
- the opposite of quality
- inferior to Dynamic Quality

In conventional thinking, "static" often implies dead, outdated, or opposed to progress. In MOQ, static patterns are the *preservation* of quality — they are what quality looks like once it has stabilized. Without them, every generation starts from nothing. Without Dynamic Quality to challenge them, they become rigid and eventually harmful.

Within this project, Canon entries are the intellectual static patterns of the knowledge base itself — stabilized because they have earned stability, not because they are permanent. The four static levels are the framework within which all value pattern analysis is conducted.
