## 1. Neutral Definition

Honor is a social value pattern in which an individual or group maintains recognised standing within a shared evaluative hierarchy by adhering to expected standards of conduct.

Honor typically manifests as:

- Public reputation for role-consistency, courage, or reliability under pressure
- Demonstrated alignment between role and conduct within a recognised code
- Defence of standing under challenge
- Refusal to accept humiliation or status-degrading treatment
- Willingness to incur personal or collective cost to preserve standing

Honor is relational and recognition-capable. It presupposes a shared evaluative code within which conduct can be judged, even when recognition is not immediately public. Honor cannot operate in the absence of such a code; without it, no standing exists to preserve.

Honor differs from neighbouring patterns in specific structural ways:

- **Shame** operates as the negative pole of the same status-recognition mechanism (loss of standing through norm violation)
- **Guilt** operates internally through self-recognition of wrongdoing, independent of external standing
- **Integrity** operates through alignment between internal commitment and action, independent of recognition or status
- **Status** is hierarchical position without normative dimension; honor adds the conduct-conformity requirement
- **Dignity** is inherent worth independent of recognition or earned standing

Honor concerns earned standing within a recognised code. Integrity concerns alignment between commitment and action. A person may possess integrity without high honor (their commitments may not be visible or recognised), and may defend honor without internal consistency (status defence may proceed regardless of underlying commitment).

This entry describes honor's structural operation. No moral status is implied by its analysis. The verdict in Section 10 addresses when honor increases or degrades coherence; the analysis throughout treats honor as a pattern with describable effects, not as a virtue or a vice.

## 2. Multi-Level Evaluation (0-2 per level)

### Physical — 0

Honor has no functional role at the physical level. Physical patterns operate independently of recognition or evaluative codes.

### Biological — 1

Honor interacts with biological substrate without being grounded in it. Status arousal, reputation sensitivity, and threat responses to perceived insult are biological mechanisms that honor patterns recruit but do not constitute. Biological systems lacking the social-level capacity for shared evaluative codes (most non-primate species) do not exhibit honor pattern even when they exhibit dominance hierarchies and status defence.

The biological score of 1 reflects honor's recruitment of biological mechanisms (cooperative behaviour stabilisation, status-related arousal) without operating as a primary biological pattern.

### Social — 2

Honor's primary functional role. Honor strongly stabilises hierarchical predictability, role-consistency expectations, reputation-based trust, collective identity coherence, and norm compliance under reduced surveillance. The social level is where honor originates as a value pattern, where it does its core work, and where its failure modes most directly degrade coordination.

### Intellectual — 1

Honor has secondary intellectual function with significant conditionality. It can support commitment to principled codes that have intellectual content (professional ethics, codified standards, articulated traditions). It can support coherence between expressed commitment and action within those codes. It can support communication of standards across reasoning instances through reputation as testimony.

However, honor frequently operates against intellectual quality. It can suppress critique that threatens standing, prioritise reputation over correspondence to evidence, and resist warranted revision of patterns when revision would damage status. The intellectual contribution is real but conditional on the honor system's operating conditions.

The intellectual score of 1 reflects this conditional positive contribution, with the understanding that the contribution can become net negative under specific failure conditions described in subsequent sections.

## 3. Structural Axis Profile

Honor's structural contribution to each Law within the levels where it has functional role.

### Social Level — Where Honor Operates Primarily

**L-S-01 Hierarchy: 2** — Honor is a core stabilising mechanism for asymmetric coordination authority. Standing within hierarchy is what honor recognises, defends, and reinforces. Without honor's recognition function, hierarchical authority degrades to pure power and loses its legitimating basis.

**L-S-02 Commitment: 1** — Honor supports commitment through reputation effects (committed conduct earns honor; defection loses it). The contribution is secondary because commitment has its own structural drivers (kin selection, ideological conviction, sustained reciprocity) that operate independently of honor systems. Honor amplifies commitment where present but does not generate it.

**L-S-03 Reciprocity: 1** — Honor supports reciprocity through reputation-based enforcement (those who violate reciprocity lose honor). Like loyalty, reciprocity has its own structural drivers and does not depend on honor; honor amplifies and stabilises existing reciprocity patterns rather than generating them.

**L-S-04 Protection: 1** — Honor contributes to both external and internal protection. Externally, honor codes can support coordinated defence. Internally, honor systems can establish standards of treatment within the group that protect members from arbitrary harm. The contribution is secondary because protection has its own structural drivers (immediate threat response, kin selection, formal enforcement) that operate independently.

**L-S-05 Continuity: 2** — Honor is a core mechanism of social continuity. It transmits role expectations across generations, encodes accumulated standards in transmissible form, and provides motivational basis for new members to accept inherited patterns. Honor systems are among the most effective mechanisms for transmitting social pattern across generational replacement without requiring continuous formal instruction.

**L-S-06 Sanction: 2** — Honor is a core sanctioning mechanism, operating primarily through informal sanction. Loss of honor is a powerful informal sanction that requires no institutional infrastructure to operate. Gain of honor is the corresponding positive sanction. Honor systems often achieve compliance with norms more reliably than formal sanction systems because they operate continuously through reputation rather than discretely through enforcement events.

**L-S-07 Shared Value Representation: 2** — Honor is deeply integrated with shared value representation. The shared representation provides the value orientation that determines what counts as honourable conduct; honor provides the recognition mechanism that rewards alignment with that representation's standards. The two structural axes are mutually reinforcing — coherent shared value representation requires coherent honor codes, and coherent honor requires shared value-representational interpretation of what conduct deserves recognition.

### Biological Level — Where Honor Recruits Substrate

**L-B-01 Preserve Pattern: 0** — Honor does not directly contribute to self-maintenance.

**L-B-02 Adapt: 0** — Honor does not directly contribute to biological adaptation. Some honor systems specifically resist adaptation, treating change as threat to standing.

**L-B-03 Reproduce: 0** — Honor does not directly contribute to biological reproduction (though high-honor individuals may have reproductive advantages, this is a derivative effect of social standing rather than a direct biological function).

**L-B-04 Cooperate: 1** — Honor supports biological cooperation by extending the operational range of trust-based coordination. Cooperative arrangements that depend on long-term reliability are stabilised by reputation systems, of which honor is one. The contribution is secondary because cooperation has multiple structural drivers operating at the biological level itself.

**L-B-05 Renew: 0** — Honor does not directly contribute to biological renewal.

### Intellectual Level — Where Honor Has Conditional Effects

**L-I-01 Distinction: 0** — Honor does not directly support symbolic representation capacity.

**L-I-02 Coherence: 1** — Honor can support coherence between expressed commitment and action within established codes. The contribution is conditional: coherence is supported within the code, but honor can resist coherence revision when revision would damage standing.

**L-I-03 Correspondence: 0** — Honor does not directly support correspondence to reality. In failure modes, honor actively undermines correspondence by prioritising reputation over evidence.

**L-I-04 Revisability: 0** — Honor does not directly support revisability. In failure modes, honor systematically resists revision of patterns invested with status.

**L-I-05 Epistemic Responsiveness: 1** — Honor can support causal responsiveness to relevant epistemic conditions when the honor code itself includes commitment to such responsiveness (scientific honor codes, professional ethics requiring evidentiary standards). Conditional contribution; honor codes that prioritise commitment or hierarchy over epistemic responsiveness undermine intellectual quality at this axis.

**L-I-06 Communication: 1** — Honor supports communication by providing reputation-based authentication of testimony and transmitted patterns. Honourable transmitters are more readily believed. Conditional contribution; honour-based authentication can fail when honor and accuracy diverge.

**L-I-07 Synthesis: 0** — Honor does not directly support synthesis. In failure modes, honor can block synthesis by treating integration with patterns from outside the honor code as boundary violation.

**L-I-08 Orientation Toward Higher Quality: 0** — Honor does not directly support orientation toward higher quality. In failure modes, honor systems can crystallise around current patterns and resist the orientation that L-I-08 requires, treating provisional holding of patterns as standing-degrading.

## 4. Multi-Level Coherence Analysis

Honor originates at the social level and operates through cross-level interaction with biological substrate and intellectual application.

The pattern operates through this functional sequence: shared evaluative code → role conformity → recognition by others → standing within the code → motivational reinforcement of conformity → continued operation of the code. Each link in this sequence is necessary; failure at any link degrades the pattern's operation.

Where shame threatens identity through standing loss, honor protects and elevates identity through standing maintenance. Both are aspects of the same status-recognition mechanism, operating in opposite directions.

Cross-level interaction operates as follows. The social code structures conduct expectations. Conduct that conforms generates reputation. Reputation produces recognition. Recognition triggers biological responses (status-related arousal, stress reduction, cooperative inclination) that reinforce continued conformity. The biological response is recruited by the social pattern but does not generate it; the same biological mechanisms operate in non-honor-bearing dominance hierarchies without producing honor patterns.

Intellectual interaction is more conditional. Honor codes can encode intellectual commitments (commitment to truth, methodological discipline, evidentiary standards) but can also encode opposition to intellectual operations (prohibition of public disagreement, suppression of critique that threatens standing, refusal of warranted revision). The intellectual contribution depends entirely on what the honor code itself contains.

Coherence strengthens when the honor code's standards are clear, recognition is proportionate to actual conduct, and the code's standards align with the structural conditions at the levels honor operates within. Coherence degrades when reputation overrides evidence, retaliation norms produce escalating conflict, and standing becomes rigid and insulated from correction.

A pattern-specific dynamic worth noting: honor's relationship to challenge is structurally distinct from most other social patterns. Most social patterns operate through compliance and tolerate non-engagement. Honor specifically requires defence under challenge. An honor system that allows challenges to standing to go unanswered begins to dissolve as a pattern. This produces honor's characteristic escalation dynamic — challenges to standing trigger defensive response, defensive response can trigger counter-response, and the pattern can produce cycles that exceed the cost the original challenge would have produced if ignored.

This escalation dynamic is structural, not pathological. It is the price honor pays for the strong norm-compliance properties that make it valuable. Honor systems that successfully avoid escalation are typically those whose codes explicitly bound the response to challenge.

## 5. Agent vs Group Instantiation

Honor operates differently when carried by individual agents versus when carried by groups, and the difference is not merely scale but structural character.

### Agent-Carried Honor

Individual honor operates through role-consistency within the agent's recognised role in the hierarchy. The agent's honor is their personal reputation for conducting themselves according to the code applicable to their role. The mechanisms of operation are personal: individual conduct, personal reputation, personal challenge response, personal cost-bearing.

Agent honor's structural emphasis falls on Hierarchy (L-S-01) and Sanction (L-S-06). The agent's standing within the hierarchy is what honor protects; informal sanction operates through individual reputation effects. The escalation dynamic in agent honor is personal — the agent personally responds to personal challenge, with personal consequences.

### Group-Carried Honor

Group honor operates through collective identity and shared standing within larger evaluative frames. The group's honor is the group's reputation for conducting itself according to its constitutive code. The mechanisms shift from personal to representational: members act as bearers of group standing, individual conduct reflects on the group, individual cost is borne for collective standing.

Group honor's structural emphasis falls on Loyalty (L-S-02), Protection (L-S-04, especially the external dimension), and Myth (L-S-07). Group standing is what loyalty maintains; protection of the group's standing motivates external defence; mythic interpretation of the group's identity provides the framework within which standing is recognised and defended. The escalation dynamic in group honor is collective — challenges to group standing can trigger collective response that exceeds what any individual member would undertake alone.

### Why the Distinction Matters Structurally

The two instantiations have different failure modes. Agent honor can fail through individual rigidity, status-defence overreach, or refusal to admit personal error — failures localised to the agent. Group honor can fail through collective escalation, ingroup-outgroup conflict amplification, and substitution of group identity for individual judgment — failures that scale across the membership and can produce conflicts much larger than any individual member would have generated.

The two also have different relationships to the other social structural axes. Agent honor's connection to L-S-01 Hierarchy is direct (the agent's standing in the hierarchy). Group honor's connection to L-S-01 is indirect — it operates more through the group's standing relative to other groups, which is a different structural relationship.

A practical implication: when analysing how honor operates in a specific situation, the question of whether the operative pattern is agent-carried or group-carried is structurally significant. Agent honor in a context that calls for group-carried operation produces undersupply of collective response; group honor in a context that calls for agent-carried operation produces oversupply of collective response. Both are failures of bearer-context fit.

## 6. Relational Impact Map

How honor ripples value effects across the four field perspectives, with bearer dependence noted where it matters.

### Internal Field

The field of those whose value experience depends on the bearer — dependents, members, followers, those for whom the relationship to the bearer is constitutive rather than chosen.

Honor produces strong positive value effects in the internal field for both bearers. Agent honor stabilises the agent's relationships with dependents through predictability and demonstrated commitment. Group honor stabilises the membership's relationship to the group through coherent collective identity and shared standards. The internal field is where honor delivers its most reliable value — dependents benefit from honourable bearers because the bearer's standing produces predictable conduct toward them.

The strength is robust across bearers. Both agent-carried and group-carried honor score 2 in the internal field.

### Allied Field

The field of those with active committed engagement in the bearer's success — treaty partners, formal collaborators, parties who have invested in the relationship.

Honor produces positive value effects in the allied field, with significant bearer-dependence. Agent-carried honor scores 1 in the allied field — allies benefit from the agent's reliability but their own commitment to alliance is the primary driver of the relationship. Group-carried honor scores 2 in the allied field — when groups coordinate through honor codes, allied groups can rely on collective reputation in ways that strengthen alliance value beyond what individual reliability alone would produce.

The bearer differential here reflects something real: alliance between groups operates partially through reputation systems that group honor reinforces. Alliance with individuals operates more through personal trust, which honor supports but does not as directly amplify.

### Neutral Field

The field of those without active commitment to the bearer — observers, the broader social environment, parties forming expectations through observable conduct.

Honor produces moderate positive value effects in the neutral field for both bearers. Score 1. Neutral observers form favourable expectations of honourable bearers through reputation, which produces value effects (easier engagement when the relationship becomes active, lower default suspicion, willingness to extend credit of various kinds). The effect is real but diluted by neutrals' lack of direct stake.

The bearer-equivalence here reflects that observation operates similarly for individual and collective reputation. Observers form expectations from both, with similar weight.

### Hostile Field

The field of those whose interests run counter to the bearer.

Honor produces no net value effect in the hostile field for either bearer. Score 0. This is structurally interesting and worth attention.

Honor's effects in hostile fields are mixed and tend to cancel. On one hand, honourable bearers can be more predictable adversaries (their conduct is constrained by their code), which is a value-positive effect for hostile parties — they can plan against an opponent who will not deceive in ways the code prohibits. On the other hand, honor's defensive escalation dynamic increases conflict intensity when challenge occurs. Honourable opponents are harder to dissuade, less willing to accept lossful compromise, and more likely to escalate over standing-related issues.

These two effects roughly cancel for both bearers, producing the neutral score. The score reflects a structural ambivalence rather than a measured neutrality — honor's hostile-field effects are real but in opposite directions, and the net effect is approximately zero.

### Cross-Field Pattern

The field-bearer matrix as a whole shows honor as a pattern with strong positive effects in fields connected to the bearer (internal, allied) and weaker but still positive effects in observation fields (neutral), with structural ambivalence in hostile fields. This profile is characteristic of patterns that strengthen in-relationship coordination at the cost of cross-relationship friction — honor is a pattern that creates strong internal coherence, with hostile-field cost as the trade-off.

## 7. Persistence Logic

Honor persists across cultures and historical periods because it solves several recurring functional problems efficiently.

**Reduces enforcement cost.** Honor systems achieve compliance with norms through reputation effects rather than continuous formal sanction. This is more energy-efficient than formal enforcement and operates in contexts where formal enforcement is impossible (small-scale societies, distributed networks, contexts of weak institutional authority).

**Produces voluntary compliance.** Members who internalise honor codes comply with norms even when not observed, because the value of standing is internalised. This produces compliance that scales beyond surveillance capacity.

**Stabilises hierarchy.** Hierarchical positions that are honour-recognised are more stable than those held only through power, because honor produces voluntary acceptance by lower-ranked members. Honor is what distinguishes legitimate authority from raw dominance.

**Enables trust signalling at scale.** Reputation systems allow trust assessment between parties who have not directly interacted, which is essential for coordination at scales beyond direct personal relationship.

**Transmits standards across generations.** Honor codes encode accumulated standards in motivationally engaged form. New members are taught not just what the standards are but why standing depends on them, which produces stronger transmission than instruction alone.

The pattern weakens or disappears under several conditions. Recognition systems lose credibility when they become disconnected from actual conduct. Norm standards collapse when too many members violate them without consequence. Status becomes disconnected from conduct when honour can be inherited or purchased rather than earned. In each of these conditions, the reinforcement loop breaks and honor cannot self-sustain.

A further condition matters under modern circumstances: when alternative sources of value-recognition emerge (formal credentials, market valuation, attention metrics), they can compete with honor systems and erode their reach. This does not destroy honor but shifts the domains in which it operates.

Persistence reflects functional efficiency at solving coordination problems, not moral superiority. Honor systems exist where they work and dissolve where they do not.

## 8. Empirical Evidence

Honor pattern is documented across substantial empirical literature spanning multiple disciplines.

### Anthropology

Honor cultures have been studied extensively across Mediterranean, Middle Eastern, Latin American, Appalachian, and pastoral societies (Peristiany 1965, Pitt-Rivers 1966, Nisbett and Cohen 1996). The pattern shows recurrent features: emphasis on reputation, defence against insult, retaliation norms calibrated to perceived severity of standing damage, and inheritance of family or clan honor across generations.

Cross-cultural variation in honor codes shows that the specific standards vary while the structural mechanism remains consistent — what honor recognises differs, but the recognition function and its effects on conduct are recurrent.

### Sociology

Military honor codes have been documented as regulating conduct beyond formal enforcement capacity (Dixon 1994, Bourke 1999). Professional honor codes (medical, legal, scientific, engineering) operate through similar mechanisms — reputation effects produce compliance with professional standards that formal regulation alone could not achieve.

Studies of organisational reputation systems show measurable effects on individual conduct under conditions of observability and standing-investment.

### Psychology

Status-threat research demonstrates measurable physiological and behavioural responses to perceived insult, with stronger responses in subjects raised in honor-emphasising contexts (Cohen, Nisbett, Bowdle, and Schwarz 1996). Public recognition has been shown to increase compliance with group norms in experimental contexts.

Reputation effects on cooperation in repeated games are well-documented (Axelrod 1984, Nowak 2006), with reputation-based cooperation enabling coordination at scales exceeding what direct reciprocity alone produces.

### Behavioural Economics

Costly signalling theory (Zahavi 1975, Smith and Bird 2000) provides theoretical grounding for understanding honor as a credible signalling mechanism — willingness to incur cost in standing-defence increases credibility of standing claims.

Public goods experiments show that reputation effects substantially increase contribution rates in repeated interactions, with the effect increasing when reputation is observable to subsequent interaction partners.

### Structural Conclusion from Evidence

Across the empirical literature, several consistent findings emerge:

- Honor patterns produce measurable effects on conduct beyond what formal sanction alone produces
- The pattern operates through reputation mechanisms that scale beyond direct interaction
- Status threat triggers consistent physiological and behavioural responses across cultures, with cultural variation in intensity and specific expression
- Rigid honor systems are associated with elevated conflict intensity, particularly retaliation cycles
- Honor systems function effectively in contexts of weak formal enforcement but can produce dysfunction in contexts where formal enforcement is available

No claim is made that honor universally increases moral quality. The empirical claim is that honor is a real and recurrent pattern with describable effects, both functional and dysfunctional depending on operating conditions.

## 9. MOQ Coherence Verdict

Overall coherence profile: **Stabilising-with-Escalation-Risk**

Honor is a high-quality social pattern at its primary level under specific operating conditions, with significant degradation potential outside those conditions.

### When Honor Increases Coherence

Honor increases coherence when the following conditions hold simultaneously:

- The honor code's standards are clear, transmissible, and reasonably stable across the relevant population
- Recognition is proportionate to actual conduct rather than to inherited or purchased markers
- Codes are bounded — they specify what conduct deserves recognition without claiming jurisdiction over all conduct
- Retaliation norms are bounded — escalation under challenge is constrained to scales the system can absorb
- The code's standards align with the structural conditions at the levels where honor operates (compatibility with hierarchy, reciprocity, continuity, sanction, and myth at the social level)

Under these conditions, honor produces strong stabilisation of social coordination. The pattern delivers high value across internal and allied fields with sustainable cost in hostile fields.

### When Honor Degrades Coherence

Honor degrades coherence when:

- Reputation overrides correspondence to reality, producing intellectual integrity violations
- Retaliation escalation produces conflict cycles whose costs exceed the standing-defence value
- Standing becomes rigid and insulated from correction, blocking warranted revision (L-I-04 violation)
- The honor code prioritises status defence over coordination quality, producing pattern operation that damages the very coordination honor exists to support
- Group honor escalation produces inter-group conflict at scales exceeding what individual members would generate

### Relationship to Other Levels

Honor's intellectual-level effects are conditional and asymmetric. Where the honor code includes intellectual commitments (truth, methodological discipline, evidentiary integrity), honor supports intellectual quality. Where the honor code does not include such commitments, honor can systematically work against intellectual quality by penalising warranted revision and rewarding reputation-protection over correspondence.

Honor's biological-level interaction is recruitment of substrate without origination. Honor patterns activate biological mechanisms (status arousal, threat response) but do not generate them. Biological systems lacking the social-level capacity for shared evaluative codes do not exhibit honor pattern even when they exhibit dominance hierarchies.

Honor's primary placement at the social level is structurally stable. The pattern originates here, operates here, and has its core function here.

### Bearer-Specific Verdicts

Agent-carried honor produces strong stabilising effects with localised failure modes (individual rigidity, personal escalation). Quality is high under good operating conditions, degrading principally through individual-scale dysfunction.

Group-carried honor produces stronger collective stabilisation but with collective failure modes that scale across membership. The escalation potential is higher; the cost of failure is higher; the value when functioning well is also higher. Group honor's quality is more bimodal than agent honor — it tends to work very well or fail quite badly, with less middle ground.

## 11. Pattern-Specific Boundary Rejections

This entry explicitly rejects framings that have appeared in conventional, popular, or academic treatments of honor.

### Honor is not virtue

Honor is a structural pattern of recognised standing within evaluative codes. It is not a moral quality or virtue in the sense of Aristotelian ethics or contemporary virtue theory. Honourable conduct may align with virtuous conduct in many cases, but the alignment is contingent on the honor code itself, not structural to honor as a pattern. Honor codes have historically demanded conduct that contemporary ethical reasoning would reject (duelling, vendetta, certain forms of class deference). The pattern operates whether or not its content meets independent ethical standards.

### Honor is not equivalent to integrity

These patterns are structurally distinct, as established in Section 1. Integrity operates through alignment between internal commitment and external action regardless of recognition. Honor operates through recognition of conduct within an evaluative code. A person of integrity may have low honor (their commitments may not align with the code or may not be visible). A person of high honor may have low integrity (status-defence may proceed regardless of internal coherence). Treating these as the same pattern produces analytical confusion about what each does.

### Honor is not status

Status is hierarchical position. Honor adds the conduct-conformity requirement to status — it is status that is conditional on demonstrated alignment with the code's standards. Inherited status without conduct-requirement is not honor; purchased status without conduct-requirement is not honor; status held purely through dominance without normative dimension is not honor. The pattern requires the evaluative code to operate.

### Honor is not inherently masculine, Western, or culturally specific

Honor patterns are documented across the full range of human cultures with variation in specific content. Some treatments associate honor with specific cultural contexts (Mediterranean honor cultures, European warrior codes, Japanese bushidō) and treat these as the entire pattern. The structural pattern is universal; the specific codes vary. Treating honor as culturally specific obscures the structural mechanism and produces analytical confusion about why similar patterns appear across unrelated cultures.

### Honor's persistence is not evidence of moral truth

Honor patterns persist across cultures and historical periods. This persistence reflects structural efficiency at solving coordination problems, not moral superiority. The patterns that persist include both honourable patterns by contemporary ethical standards (codes of professional integrity, codes of mutual respect) and patterns most contemporary ethical reasoning rejects (codes of vendetta, codes of caste deference). Persistence as evidence of moral truth is a category error; persistence is evidence of functional efficiency in operating conditions.

### Honor is not reducible to costly signalling alone

Costly signalling theory provides genuine insight into one mechanism by which honor operates — willingness to bear cost increases credibility of standing claims. This is valid analysis but not exhaustive. Honor also operates through mechanisms costly signalling alone does not capture: collective identity formation, generational transmission of standards, shared interpretive framework integration. Reducing honor to signalling alone produces an impoverished analysis that misses structural functions the pattern actually performs.

### Honor is not a pattern that can be neutrally adopted or abandoned at will

Honor codes exist as social patterns that members are positioned within rather than choosing freely. An individual cannot simply decide to operate outside their society's honor system without bearing the standing costs of that decision. Treating honor as a freely chosen orientation that individuals can take up or set aside misunderstands the pattern's structural operation. The pattern can be reformed, transformed, or eventually replaced, but these are social-level processes rather than individual choices.
