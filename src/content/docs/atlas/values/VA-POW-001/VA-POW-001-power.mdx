## Power — Narrative Articulation

### Character of the Pattern

Power is the social pattern of accumulated effective capacity to shape what happens within a value field. It is the capacity to control what is at stake, who can reach what, and what follows from action. An agent or group with power can shape reality beyond its immediate reach; an agent or group without power cannot protect what it values or sustain the patterns it depends on.

Power is *effective capacity*. It is not a belief, not a right, not an intention, and not a means. It is the actual ability to affect outcomes, built up over time and durable once accumulated. This is what distinguishes power from the patterns it is constantly confused with. Authority is the recognised right to command, which may exist without the effective capacity to enforce it. Legitimacy is the belief that power is rightly held, which may accompany power or be absent from it. Violence is one physical means power may draw on, not power itself. Influence is one softer mechanism among power's several. Each of these is a distinct pattern standing in a relation to power, captured in the relation layer.

Power is recognisable before it is justified. A party subject to power recognises it in the experience of having its stakes, access, and consequences shaped by another's capacity, prior to any theory of whether that capacity is rightful. This pre-justificatory recognisability marks power as a genuine social-stratum pattern.

### Why Social is Primary, with Substantial Cross-Stratum Engagement

Power's primary stratum is social: its mode of realisation is multi-agent coordination, and it is constituted in the relations among agents and groups within a value field. But power engages every other stratum substantially, which is why its secondary strata are all three.

Physically, power depends on substrate — territory, infrastructure, tools, logistics, and the physical means of enforcement. These enable and constrain power without constituting it; they are substrate relations, captured separately.

Biologically, power shapes outcomes — stress, fear, security, survival pressure, resilience. The presence or absence of power affects biological flourishing across the bearers in its field. This is modulation of biological substrate, and its character is conditional: power bound to protective patterns reduces biological stress; power bound to predatory patterns increases it.

Intellectually, power enables intellectual patterns to propagate, institutionalise, and scale — but does not generate them, and may suppress them when misaligned. This is power's modulation of and by intellectual patterns, again conditional on binding.

### The Conditionality That Defines Power

The most important structural feature of power, and the one that most distinguishes it from coercion, is that its character relative to its field is *conditional on what it is bound to*. Coercion is internally consonant but externally dissonant by its nature — it degrades the field it operates within regardless of intent. Power is not like this. Power bound to reciprocity, trust, and legitimacy is consonant with its field: it protects, stabilises, coordinates, and defends the patterns the field is composed of. Power bound to predation, self-justification, or coercion is dissonant with its field: it substitutes for reciprocity, amplifies lower-quality patterns, and degrades the conditions for voluntary coordination.

This is why power's compositional character is recorded as `conditional` rather than as a fixed signature. Power amplifies the values to which it is bound; it does not correct them and does not originate them. It is a multiplier on value-flow whose sign is set by its binding. Power is neither moral nor immoral, it is decisive. Its quality in any instance is the quality of what it is bound to and what it is used to feed or starve — not a property of power itself.

This holds the structural-neutrality discipline the framework requires. The folk vocabulary around power is dense with moral loading — tyranny, oppression, empowerment, authority — but the structure underneath all of these is the same conditional capacity. The valence belongs to the binding, not to the pattern.

### Empirical Recurrence

Power recurs with great consistency. Political systems concentrate it to coordinate defence, law, and resource allocation. Organisations distribute it through hierarchy to align action at scale. Groups lacking it cannot protect their members or sustain their institutions. Power structures shape incentives more reliably than stated ideals do.

The failure patterns are equally consistent and are, importantly, failures of *binding* rather than failures of power as such: power detached from value alignment trends toward predation and internal decay; systems relying primarily on coercion experience rising resistance; suppression of intellectual dissent through power reduces adaptive capacity. Long-term stability appears to correlate with alignment between power and higher-quality value hierarchies rather than with coercive capacity alone — recorded as interpretive hypothesis, not established finding.

### Dynamics of Power

Power's operation has three primary dynamics-side characteristics: accumulation, binding-mediated amplification, and cross-stratum modulation.

**Accumulation.** Power accumulates as distinct stocks — coordinative capacity, reserves of compliance, resources, reputation, and institutional structure. Each stock builds through operation when conditions hold (successful coordination accumulates compliance; demonstrated effectiveness accumulates reputation; sustained operation accumulates institutional structure) and depletes when reserves are drawn faster than replenished. Accumulation operates across multiple timescales simultaneously: immediate exercise, episodic campaigns, sustained operation, generational institutional persistence. The reinforcing structure (success → accumulated capacity → further success) is power's primary persistence mechanism. The opposing dynamic — accumulated counter-power, resentment, eroded legitimacy — operates with characteristic delays and produces bounded-persistence dynamics over longer timescales.

**Binding-mediated amplification.** Power amplifies the patterns it is bound to. The amplification is the enabling of field-recruitment beyond what the bound pattern could coordinate alone, not the production of flow from nothing. Power bound to reciprocity enables reciprocity to recruit coordinated flow at scales unaided reciprocity cannot manage. Power bound to predation enables predation to recruit destructive flow at scales unaided predation could not coordinate. The same operation produces different field-effects depending on the bound pattern's character and the binding's quality.

The control of stakes, access, and consequences operates through the binding portfolio. The control of stakes is determined by which bindings make what contingent on what. The control of access is determined by which bindings gate what. The control of consequences is determined by which bindings propagate what downstream. Power's binding portfolio in any specific instance — the aggregate of all its simultaneous bindings — determines its operational character.

**Cross-stratum modulation.** Power modulates biology directly (reducing or increasing stress, security, vitality across the field's bearers depending on binding quality), modulates intellectual patterns directly (enabling propagation through institutional support, suppressing through denial of substrate), and modulates physical reality through cascade (mediated through biological and institutional agency, not direct origination). The cross-stratum operation is mediated, transformative, and typically delayed; higher strata are not reduced to social configurations, and lower strata are not originated by social ones.

### Relations Recorded Separately

Power's structural relations to other patterns are captured as relation records, not restated here. They include: substrate dependence on physical patterns (territory, infrastructure, tools) and on biological and social substrates; modulation of biological substrate (conditional — supportive or distorting by binding); modulation of intellectual patterns (conditional); distinction-relations to authority, legitimacy, violence, and influence (each a separate pattern power is commonly confused with); binding-relations to reciprocity, legitimacy, authority, violence, influence, coercion, and self-perpetuation; and field-relative effects across allied, neutral, hostile, and internal positions. Each is its own record in the relation layer. See VA-POW-001-relations.md.
