## 1. Neutral Definition

Solidarity is a social value pattern in which individuals align themselves with a defined collective identity, accepting shared risk and coordinated action in defense or advancement of that identity.

It typically manifests as:

- Public identification with a group
- Willingness to incur personal cost for collective goals
- Coordinated resistance or defense
- Boundary reinforcement under pressure
- Collective mobilization during conflict or stress

Solidarity differs from:

- **Loyalty** (sustained relational commitment across time)
- **Care** (maintenance-oriented support)
- **Empathy** (affective resonance)
- **Reciprocity** (exchange expectation)
- **Justice** (impartial rule enforcement)

Solidarity does not require:

- Personal familiarity between members
- Long-term relational persistence
- Emotional warmth
- Intellectual coherence

It is an identity-bound alignment pattern that increases collective action capacity.

No moral status is implied.

## 2. Multi-Level Evaluation (0–2 per level)

### Physical — "0"

Solidarity has no direct stabilizing function at the physical level.

---

### Biological — "1"

Solidarity indirectly enhances survival through coalition formation and coordinated defense.

Coalitional psychology research (Tooby & Cosmides) indicates evolved mechanisms for alliance tracking and free-rider detection. Parochial altruism studies (Choi & Bowles) show that in-group cooperation combined with out-group resistance can provide evolutionary advantages under intergroup competition.

However, solidarity depends primarily on symbolic identity structures rather than biological bonding alone.

Contribution is indirect and mediated through social structures.

---

### Social — "2"

Solidarity strongly stabilizes:

- Group cohesion
- Collective mobilization
- Boundary defense
- Norm enforcement
- Institutional formation

It enables coordinated sacrifice and sustained collective action under stress.

---

### Intellectual — "0"

Solidarity does not preserve abstraction, inquiry, or coherence as such.

It may use ideology, but does not prioritize truth over group identity. It can suppress dissent when cohesion is prioritized over critique.

## 3. Multi-Level Coherence Analysis

Primary service level: **Social**

Cross-level resonance:

Shared identity → Coordinated action → Collective success → Identity reinforcement.

Solidarity binds individuals into a higher-order social unit.

Where loyalty stabilizes bonds across time (centripetal), solidarity synchronizes individuals across a boundary (centrifugal).

Tension zones:

- Solidarity vs impartial justice
- Solidarity vs individual autonomy
- Solidarity vs intellectual dissent
- Solidarity vs cross-group cooperation

Solidarity can stabilize institutions when aligned with governing structures, or destabilize them when mobilized as counter-solidarity against perceived injustice or exclusion. Its structural power is direction-neutral.

Coherence strengthens when:

- Group boundaries are clear
- Norm enforcement is consistent
- Shared identity is stable

Coherence degrades when:

- Internal dissent is suppressed
- Boundary rigidity intensifies
- Polarization escalates into inter-group conflict

## 4. Relational Impact Map

### Allies (Ingroup Members)

- Experience protection
- Share risk burden
- Increase coordinated bargaining power

### Neutrals

- May face pressure to align
- May experience exclusion if non-alignment is stigmatized

### Adversaries (Outgroup Members)

- Encounter coordinated opposition
- May face intensified boundary enforcement
- Risk dehumanization under conflict escalation

### Internal POV Group

Within solidarity structures:

- Conformity pressure increases
- Sacrifice is valorized
- Deviance is costly
- Enforcement strengthens identity cohesion

## 5. Persistence Logic

Solidarity persists due to:

### Evolutionary Drivers

- Coalition advantage under intergroup competition
- Parochial altruism patterns
- Free-rider detection mechanisms

### Social Drivers

- Identity formation
- Shared narratives
- Boundary maintenance
- Collective bargaining mechanisms

### Institutional Drivers

- Labor unions
- National states
- Religious movements
- Political mobilization structures

Reinforcing loop:

Shared identity → Coordinated action → Collective outcome → Identity reinforcement.

Persistence weakens when:

- Identity fragmentation increases
- Resource distribution undermines cohesion
- External threats dissipate
- Internal legitimacy declines

Persistence reflects functional advantage, not moral legitimacy.

## 6. Empirical Evidence

Solidarity is observable across biological, sociological, and political domains.

### Evolutionary and Behavioral Research

- Coalitional psychology (Tooby & Cosmides) identifies evolved mechanisms for alliance formation.
- Parochial altruism research (Choi & Bowles) demonstrates cooperative in-group alignment under competitive pressure.
- Group competition models suggest coalition cohesion increases survival probability under threat.

---

### Political Science

- Rally-around-the-flag effects during wartime.
- Labor strike mobilization.
- Revolutionary and resistance movements.

---

### Sociology

- Social Identity Theory (Tajfel).
- Collective action theory (Olson).
- Group-based conformity under threat.

---

### Structural Conclusion from Evidence

Across domains:

- Solidarity increases collective coordination.
- Solidarity amplifies enforcement capacity.
- Solidarity strengthens boundary rigidity.
- Solidarity escalates conflict when boundaries harden.

No claim is made that solidarity universally increases moral quality.

## 7. MoQ Coherence Verdict

Overall coherence profile: **Socially potent and boundary-sensitive**

Strong alignment:

- Group cohesion
- Collective defense
- Institutional mobilization

Primary risk zones:

- Polarization
- Escalatory conflict
- Suppression of dissent
- Inter-group hostility

Relationship to Dynamic Quality:

Solidarity does not define or operationalize Dynamic Quality.

It may enable collective transformation, or suppress internal correction when cohesion overrides critique.

High-value when:

- Coordinating defense or reform
- Supporting stable collective projects
- Bounded by justice and accountability

Low-value when:

- Driving exclusion
- Suppressing dissent
- Escalating conflict

Degenerative when:

- Boundary rigidity produces systemic fragmentation or violence.
