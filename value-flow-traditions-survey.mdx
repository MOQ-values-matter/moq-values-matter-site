## Purpose

This document surveys existing intellectual traditions that bear on **value-flow** — the territory the dynamics half of the project's framework must cover. The statics half describes patterns as they stand: their composition, substrate, modulation, persistence, profile-coherence. It cannot describe how value moves through the connections between patterns, how that movement shifts over time, how patterns connect, disconnect, divert, amplify, and starve one another, or on what timescales effects propagate. The dynamics half is the framework's largest outstanding construction, and the migration work across the coercion–guilt–shame–power cluster has surfaced enough dynamics-territory from inside the statics that the construction must now begin.

This survey is Task 1 of that construction, parallel to the state-operator-reference-survey that opened the statics half. It does what the statics survey did: examines existing traditions, evaluates each against the project's commitments, and produces a recommendation about what to adopt, adapt, or build from scratch. It is not the dynamics framework itself — that is Task 2.

## What This Survey Looks For

Each tradition is evaluated against five criteria:

1. **Does it offer a clear account of value-flow** — not just any flow (energy, information, money), but flow *of value*, in a sense consistent with value as the primary organising principle of reality?

2. **Does it preserve MOQ stratum discipline** — does it respect the four-stratum hierarchy, the prohibition on origination across strata, the prohibition on reduction across strata, while permitting the holistic cross-stratum interaction the framework requires?

3. **Does it hold the Dynamic Quality boundary** — that is, does it avoid the metaphysical claim that some static account has *captured* Dynamic Quality itself? This is the criterion most easily misread. The boundary does NOT prohibit theorising about change, novelty, emergence, or becoming — all of these are legitimate objects of static-pattern analysis, and the intellectual traditions surveyed below are themselves examples of static-pattern work on dynamic processes. What the boundary forbids is the inflated metaphysical claim that an account of change has captured becoming-itself. As soon as something is captured in a static pattern, that pattern is by definition not Dynamic Quality — that is what makes the discipline non-trivial. The criterion is a constraint on inflation, not on inquiry into change. The dynamics half will be most vulnerable to drift here, but the drift to guard against is the metaphysical over-claim, not the analytical project.

4. **Does it offer primitives the project could adopt or adapt** — concepts, distinctions, or operations that could become part of the dynamics half's vocabulary without requiring the full ontological commitments of the tradition that produced them.

5. **What would adoption cost** — what commitments would the project take on if it adopted the tradition's framework, and are those commitments compatible with the project's grounding?

The survey treats Circle/Ripple Theory first, separately from the others, because it is the project's own existing prior work on this territory and stands in a different relation to the construction than the external traditions.

A framing note before the surveys begin. All the surveyed traditions, including Circle/Ripple Theory itself, are intellectual-level static patterns — concepts and frameworks built to capture and analyse dynamic processes. Building such patterns is what intellectual work does, and recognising the traditions as static-pattern work is honouring them properly rather than dismissing them. The evaluations below are not attacks on the traditions for "trying to understand change"; they are evaluations of how those static-pattern accounts can be borrowed, where they overreach into metaphysical inflation, and how their analytical content can be separated from their inflated claims where they make them. Most traditions offer something usable; some are more vulnerable to inflation than others; none should be dismissed as illegitimate inquiry. The discipline is discrimination, not refusal.

## 1. Circle/Ripple Theory — The Project's Existing Prior Work

Circle/Ripple Theory, authored by Kim Roar Spørkel within the project, is the existing body of work on value-flow that the dynamics half must integrate rather than evaluate against external standards. It is treated first and at greater length because it is not one tradition among many — it is the project's living prior work on exactly the territory the dynamics half must formalise.

### What it offers

Circle/Ripple Theory provides a substantial vocabulary that already covers much of the dynamics territory:

- **Value Node** — an individual understood as a living point where value is received, integrated, and emitted. The conception is active and processual: the node *does* something with value, it is not merely a container.
- **Becoming Value Node** — the same node understood explicitly as a process rather than a fixed entity, continuously shaped by past ripples and present context.
- **Ripple** — the propagation of value effects caused by action, inaction, or presence. Ripples are explicitly cross-stratum and cross-temporal; they travel beyond intent, beyond the immediate context, and beyond the agent.
- **Value Field** — the environment in which ripples travel, stabilise, accumulate, or decay. The field is the medium of propagation.
- **Priming** — the accumulated influence of past ripples that shapes a node's perception, interpretation, and response. Priming is the temporal residue of prior value-flow in the bearer.
- **Reserves** — stored value buffers that absorb stress and delay collapse. Reserves capture the capacity of a node or field to absorb dynamics-perturbations without losing structural integrity.
- **Circle** — a socially bound, multi-level anchored value field. Circles are the structured environments in which ripples propagate among nodes; they are made real by people, resources, shared meaning, reciprocity, and enforcement.
- **Zone of Value Resonance** — a region within a circle where certain ripples are amplified, normalised, dampened, or suppressed. This concept already names what the dynamics half needs to formalise as modulation of flow.
- **Keychain** — the higher-order binding of circles into a larger social structure that carries accumulated value across multiple circles. This is the supra-circle binding structure.
- **Collapse → Repair → Re-entry** — the cyclical temporal architecture by which value fields fail, are re-patterned, and admit renewed participation. This is the project's existing account of failure-and-recovery dynamics.
- **Field-Relative Valuation** — the principle that the same act produces different real value effects in different fields. Crucially, this is not relativism: the effects are objective within their fields, arising from real physical, biological, and social consequences.
- **Non-Relative Value Hierarchy** — local success does not imply high Quality; some values are objectively higher quality because they preserve more strata and reduce destructive ripple propagation.

### Where it does well

Circle/Ripple Theory is already MOQ-grounded, having been built as an extension of Pirsig's framework rather than imported from another tradition. It preserves the stratum discipline explicitly — the canonical rule "Stories, narratives, myths, and symbolic meaning are always social, never intellectual" appears in its glossary as a hard constraint. It holds the Dynamic Quality boundary explicitly through the diagram-language rule "Dynamic Quality must not be diagrammed as a mechanism." The vocabulary is concrete enough to apply to specific situations (priming, reserves, collapse) and abstract enough to generalise (ripples, fields, circles). The author has been refining it over roughly two decades of applied use across personal, professional, and organisational contexts, so the concepts have been tested against reality, not merely articulated.

### What it does not yet fully formalise

The theory offers vocabulary but has not yet been formalised at the same level the statics half achieved in framework v2.1. Several gaps are visible from inside the migrations:

The **coupling mechanism** between nodes and patterns is named ("ripples travel," "circles bind") but not specified at the level of primitives. What does it mean structurally for one pattern to feed another's value-flow? This is the binding-relation gap the power migration surfaced as the strongest evidence the dynamics half was needed.

The **conservation question** is open. Do ripples conserve value across propagation, or do they amplify, dissipate, or transform it? Circle/Ripple Theory uses "amplification" and "decay" but does not state whether value-flow is conserved-by-default, dissipated-by-default, amplified-by-default, or context-dependent.

The **cascade dynamics across strata** are named (ripples travel "across levels") but not specified. What determines whether a ripple stays at one stratum versus cascades to others? Framework v2.1 added the direct-vs-cascaded modulation distinction, which begins to address this on the statics side; the dynamics treatment is still open.

The **temporal scales** are recognised but not differentiated. Some ripples are nearly instantaneous (the slap that injures); some build over years (priming that shapes a generation); some are sustained-covert (betrayal hidden through long deception). The same metaphor ("ripples") does not capture these scales equally well, and the dynamics half will need to distinguish them.

The **primitives versus configurations distinction** is implicit but not made explicit. Some Circle/Ripple concepts seem to be fundamental operations (propagation, accumulation, amplification, damping); others are configurations or compositions (the collapse-repair-re-entry cycle, the keychain). The dynamics half should articulate which are the building blocks and which are the patterns those blocks compose.

The **relationship between flow and the patterns that channel or direct it** is named in places ("power is a social-level pattern: the organised capacity to enforce boundaries") but the formal relation between a pattern's structural function and the value-flow it operates on or with is unspecified.

### Verdict

Circle/Ripple Theory is the foundation the dynamics half will build on. It is not an external tradition to be evaluated and selectively adopted; it is the project's living account of the territory the dynamics half must formalise. The dynamics half's task is therefore to **formalise and extend** Circle/Ripple Theory, in the way the statics half formalised and extended Pirsig's MOQ — not departing from the foundation but giving it the architectural rigour needed to support systematic analysis.

The traditions surveyed below should be evaluated for what they can contribute to that formalisation, not as alternatives to it.

## 2. Systems Dynamics (Forrester, Senge, Meadows)

Systems dynamics, originating with Jay Forrester at MIT in the 1950s and developed extensively through Donella Meadows' work and Peter Senge's organisational applications, offers a rigorous account of how stocks and flows change over time through feedback loops.

### What it offers

The core primitives are **stocks** (accumulated quantities) and **flows** (rates of change of those quantities), connected by **feedback loops** (reinforcing or balancing) and modulated by **delays** (time lags between cause and effect). System archetypes — recurring dynamics patterns like "drift to low performance," "tragedy of the commons," "escalation," "shifting the burden" — name common configurations the primitives produce when arranged in characteristic ways.

### What it does well

The stock/flow distinction is one of the most analytically powerful conceptual moves available for thinking about dynamics. It separates the quantity-at-a-moment from the rate-of-change, and makes feedback dynamics tractable. The system archetypes capture recurring configurations across many domains. The tradition is field-tested across decades of application in industry, ecology, public policy, and organisational learning.

### What it does not offer

Systems dynamics is **value-neutral by default**. Stocks and flows are quantities, not values. Money flows, water flows, populations flow — but the framework does not distinguish high-quality flow from low-quality flow except by reference to externally-supplied criteria. A reinforcing loop that produces collapse is structurally the same as a reinforcing loop that produces flourishing; the framework names the dynamics but not the value.

Systems dynamics is **stratum-blind**. Biological, social, and intellectual dynamics are all modelled with the same stocks-and-flows-and-loops vocabulary, with no equivalent of the MOQ stratum discipline. This collapses distinctions the project requires.

Systems dynamics tends toward **quantitative modelling**. Forrester's original applications used differential equations and computer simulations. The tradition's most rigorous expressions assume that stocks and flows can be measured numerically, which does not transfer to most value phenomena cleanly.

### DQ boundary check

Systems dynamics is **vulnerable** to DQ operationalisation in two characteristic ways. First, "emergence" in systems dynamics tends to be treated as the predictable-in-principle outcome of feedback dynamics — which would deny that novelty is pre-conceptual and not operationalisable. Second, system archetypes can be wielded as if they explained creative breakthroughs ("this organisation transformed through a reinforcing feedback loop of innovation"), which crosses the DQ boundary.

### What is adaptable

The stock/flow distinction maps directly onto concepts Circle/Ripple Theory already operates with informally. **Priming** is a stock (accumulated past influence); **ripples** are flows (rate of value propagation); **reserves** are stocks (buffered capacity); the rate at which reserves are depleted or replenished is a flow. The dynamics half can adopt the stock/flow distinction as a formal primitive without taking on the value-neutral or stratum-blind commitments of the parent tradition.

**Feedback loops** are also adaptable. Reinforcing loops correspond to phenomena like accumulation (power's compounding), escalation (coercion's counter-pattern formation that destabilises it), and amplification (shame's signaling spreading through observation infrastructure). Balancing loops correspond to phenomena like reintegration (shame's reintegration cycle), reciprocity-restoration (guilt-and-repair), and homeostasis at the field level.

**Delays** are adaptable and probably important. Several dynamics-territory phenomena identified in the migrations involve delays: betrayal's effects propagating long after the act; coercion's counter-pattern formation accumulating until it exceeds enforcement capacity; priming shaping perception of events long after the priming-events themselves.

### Adoption cost

The cost is the discipline of *not* adopting the value-neutral and stratum-blind assumptions that come with the tradition's standard formulations. Adopting stock/flow without adopting "value is whatever stock we measure" requires constant vigilance.

### Verdict

**Adaptable selectively.** The stock/flow distinction, feedback loops, and delays are useful primitives to formalise what Circle/Ripple Theory already operates with informally. Reject the value-neutral and stratum-blind aspects. Reject the operationalisation of emergence. Recommend specifically adopting:
- Stock/flow as a formal distinction within the dynamics half
- Feedback loops (reinforcing and balancing) as a vocabulary for cycle-dynamics
- Delays as an explicit temporal primitive

These adoptions should be made with explicit disclaimers about the parent tradition's value-neutrality, and should not import system archetypes wholesale.

## 3. Network Flow Theory

Network flow theory, a branch of graph theory and operations research, formalises the movement of quantities through a graph with capacity-constrained edges and conservation at nodes.

### What it offers

The core primitives are **directed graphs** of nodes and edges, **capacities** on edges (maximum flow that can pass), **sources** and **sinks** (where flow originates and terminates), **conservation** at intermediate nodes (flow in equals flow out), and theorems like **max-flow/min-cut** that identify critical bottlenecks.

### What it does well

The graph-theoretic structure is rigorous and cleanly captures the topology of coupling between elements. Capacity constraints introduce limitation in a structurally honest way. The max-flow/min-cut theorem identifies critical bottlenecks formally — which patterns or bindings, if removed, would most degrade overall flow.

### What it does not offer

Like systems dynamics, network flow theory is **value-neutral and stratum-flat**. It treats flow as an abstract quantity that satisfies conservation; it has no concept of value or stratum.

**Conservation is a hard assumption** that does not transfer cleanly to value. Value can be created (a high-quality binding amplifies what flows through it) and destroyed (a low-quality binding dissipates value); flow networks assume the total quantity is preserved across the graph. The mismatch is structural, not cosmetic.

Network flow theory **treats nodes as passive routing points**. Circle/Ripple Theory's value nodes actively receive, integrate, and emit value — they are not mere conduits. The active processing of value at nodes is not represented in standard flow networks.

### DQ boundary check

Network flow theory **holds the DQ boundary** because it does not claim to model emergence or novelty. It models the routing of quantities through a fixed graph.

### What is adaptable

The **graph-theoretic representation** of the binding-graph between patterns is adaptable. Showing patterns as nodes and bindings as edges allows for visualisation and formal analysis of cluster structure. The migration work has been moving toward this implicitly through the relation records; making it graph-theoretic explicitly may clarify several questions.

The **capacity** concept may be useful for formalising reserves: a node's capacity to absorb stress (its reserves) maps onto a graph-theoretic capacity. The **bottleneck/cut** concept may be useful for identifying which bindings are critical to a field's stability.

### Adoption cost

The cost is again discipline about what *not* to adopt. Conservation must not be imported as an assumption about value-flow. Value is not conserved across high-quality versus low-quality bindings; the framework's whole point about quality differences depends on this.

### Verdict

**Adaptable structurally but not as substance.** Graph-theoretic representation may be useful for the binding-graph visualisation and analysis. Capacity and cut concepts may inform reserves and critical-binding analysis. But the substantive flow theory (max-flow algorithms, conservation theorems) does not transfer to value-flow.

## 4. Process Philosophy (Whitehead, Bergson)

Process philosophy, principally Alfred North Whitehead's *Process and Reality* and Henri Bergson's work on duration and the élan vital, treats reality as fundamentally constituted by processes and events rather than by substances.

### What it offers

The core orientation is that **becoming is more fundamental than being** — that reality is fundamentally a process of events concrescing into actual occasions (Whitehead) or of duration as continuous qualitative becoming (Bergson). Specific concepts include **prehension** (how present events relate to past events), **concrescence** (the constitution of an actual occasion through prehensions), **duration** (Bergsonian lived time, distinct from clock time), and **the fallacy of misplaced concreteness** (treating abstractions as if they were the concrete reality).

### What it does well

Process philosophy is the **closest philosophical tradition to MOQ** in its rejection of substance metaphysics. The orientation toward process over thing is already aligned with the project's commitments. The fallacy of misplaced concreteness is a useful disciplinary reminder for the dynamics work: dynamics primitives must be tools for describing flow, not substantively-existing things in their own right.

**Duration** as distinct from clock time may be valuable for the dynamics half. Some value-flow phenomena have a temporal character that clock time does not capture — the way guilt's pressure "holds live" in an agent over time, the way priming accumulates non-linearly, the way betrayal's effects propagate on a timescale set by the relationship rather than by external clocks.

### What it does not offer

Process philosophy is **highly abstract** and difficult to operationalise. Whitehead's system is notoriously difficult to apply; Bergson's is more accessible but also more impressionistic.

Bergson's **élan vital** — a vital force underlying biological becoming — risks being read as an operationalised version of something like Dynamic Quality. The dynamics half must not import élan vital or anything structurally similar.

Whitehead's **concrescence** can be read as a *mechanism* by which actual occasions come into being — which would cross the DQ boundary, since the dynamics half must not specify the mechanism by which novelty enters the world.

Process philosophy has **no empirical grounding** comparable to MOQ's quality-recognition. The traditions live in conceptual analysis and metaphysical argument; they have not been tested against the world in the way Pirsig's quality has.

### DQ boundary check

**At risk of metaphysical inflation, with traditions varying in vulnerability**. Whitehead and Bergson both build static-pattern accounts of becoming, which is legitimate inquiry. The DQ-boundary concern arises specifically when Whitehead's concrescence is read not as a description of how certain occasion-structures appear in analysis but as a capture of *what becoming itself is*; similarly when Bergson's élan vital is read not as a useful concept about vital becoming but as the captured source of vital becoming itself. Used as static concepts about how change appears in experience and analysis, both can contribute. The metaphysical claims that overreach into capturing-becoming-itself are what the boundary disciplines against — not the analytical content beneath those claims.

### What is adaptable

The **general orientation** toward process over substance is already MOQ-aligned. The **fallacy of misplaced concreteness** is a useful disciplinary reminder for dynamics construction. The **duration concept** as distinct from clock time may inform the dynamics half's account of temporal scales. The discipline of not treating processes as having substance-like persistence may inform how the dynamics half describes value-flow patterns.

### Adoption cost

The cost is selectivity. Adopting Whitehead or Bergson wholesale would import metaphysical commitments incompatible with the project's MOQ grounding. Selective adoption requires careful work.

### Verdict

**Philosophically nourishing, but adopt with extreme care.** The general orientation aligns. Specific concepts can be borrowed only after evaluation against the DQ boundary. Recommend selective adoption of:
- Duration as a temporal-scale concept distinct from clock time
- The fallacy of misplaced concreteness as a disciplinary check

Recommend explicit rejection of:
- Élan vital and any structurally similar concept
- Concrescence as a mechanism for the production of novelty

## 5. Buddhist Tradition

The Buddhist intellectual tradition, spanning roughly 2500 years across multiple cultures and schools (Theravāda, Mahāyāna, Vajrayāna, Zen, and others), has developed sophisticated analytical frameworks for how phenomena arise, persist, transform, and dissolve through conditioning relationships rather than through inherent essences.

### What it offers

The most relevant analytical concepts are **paticcasamuppāda** (dependent origination — that all phenomena arise in dependence on multiple causes and conditions, none existing independently), the **Twelve Nidānas** (a specific model of dependent arising: ignorance → formations → consciousness → name-and-form → six sense bases → contact → feeling → craving → grasping → becoming → birth → ageing-and-death), **anattā** (non-self — the absence of fixed inherent essence in arising phenomena), **anicca** (impermanence — all conditioned things change), **karma** (the conditioning influence of past action on present and future, separable from mystical-retribution readings), and **śūnyatā** (emptiness — the absence of inherent existence in any phenomenon).

### What it does well

Buddhist analysis offers **process-oriented metaphysics without substance commitments** — close in spirit to MOQ's rejection of subject-object metaphysics. The dependent-origination framework systematically rejects single-cause thinking; phenomena arise from constellations of conditioning factors, which parallels the project's treatment of patterns arising from substrate, modulation, and binding rather than from isolated causes.

The tradition is **cross-stratum in its thinking**. Mental, physical, social, and intellectual phenomena are treated within one analytical framework rather than partitioned into separate disciplines. This is not stratum-collapse — Buddhism preserves distinctions among phenomena — but rather integrated thinking that resembles the project's commitment to the four strata as a unified hierarchy.

Buddhism has a **long empirical tradition** through contemplative observation, with refined methodology for the phenomenological analysis of how patterns arise and persist. The empirical content is not laboratory-experimental but is also not merely conceptual; it is grounded in sustained observation of mental and behavioural patterns under controlled conditions.

Most importantly, the tradition has a **structural alignment with the DQ-boundary discipline**. The teaching of emptiness, properly understood, is not the claim that phenomena are unreal but the claim that no phenomenon can be captured by any concept as if the concept were the phenomenon's essence. This is precisely the discipline the framework requires for Dynamic Quality: do not mistake the static patterns of understanding for what they are about. Buddhist epistemology has spent two and a half millennia thinking carefully about this exact problem.

### What it does not offer / problems

Buddhism is **deeply embedded in a soteriological framework** — a path to liberation from suffering. The analytical content cannot always be cleanly separated from the soteriological framing in the source texts. Adopting analytical concepts requires careful extraction.

The tradition has **specific religious and cultural commitments** that vary across schools and that the project cannot adopt wholesale. Reincarnation as a cosmological claim, specific accounts of nirvāṇa, the institutional structures of the saṅgha, and ritual practices are not analytical content the framework needs.

**Translation and terminological issues** are significant. The same Pāli or Sanskrit terms are translated variably across English Buddhist literatures, and the analytical content shifts subtly with each translation. Selecting terminology requires care.

**Anattā can be misread** as denying the reality of patterns themselves rather than denying their inherent independent existence. This misreading would be hostile to the project's value-pattern realism. The doctrine, correctly understood, says patterns arise dependent on conditions — which is consistent with the project. The doctrine misread says patterns don't exist — which is not.

### DQ boundary check

**Holds, with significant structural alignment**. The Buddhist emptiness teaching is in some sense the project's closest philosophical ally on the specific point of the DQ-boundary discipline: both insist that conceptual capture is not the underlying reality. Buddhism's account of dependent origination does not claim to have captured *what becoming is*; it describes how phenomena that arise can be analysed in terms of conditioning relationships. The analytical work is itself a static pattern, not a capture of Dynamic Quality.

### What is adaptable

**Dependent origination as a vocabulary for pattern arising**. The framework already has substrate-dependence, modulation, and (provisionally) binding-relations. Dependent origination is a complementary framework for thinking about how a pattern arises from a constellation of conditioning factors — which is what each pattern's relations file already encodes, but the Buddhist tradition provides analytical depth on the conditioning relations themselves.

**The Twelve Nidānas as a model of cascading causal influence**. The specific structure of the Twelve Nidānas may be too specific to adopt directly, but the analytical move of identifying a sequence of conditioning factors that feed into one another is exactly what the dynamics half will need for cascade dynamics.

**Anicca as a stance toward pattern persistence**. The project already treats persistence as conditional rather than absolute; the Buddhist articulation of impermanence as a general property of all conditioned things provides philosophical depth for this stance.

**The emptiness teaching as DQ-discipline**. The structural alignment between Buddhist emptiness and the framework's DQ-boundary discipline can be made explicit. The dynamics half can borrow the emptiness teaching's clarity about why conceptual capture is never identical to what is captured.

### Adoption cost

Moderate. The soteriological framework and religious commitments must be left behind; the analytical content extracted carefully. Terminology requires careful choices to avoid misreading.

### Verdict

**Philosophically valuable with significant structural alignment**. Adopt as inspiration for dependent-origination thinking about pattern arising, for the Twelve Nidānas as a model of conditioning cascade, and for the emptiness teaching's structural support of the DQ-discipline. The dynamics half can draw on this tradition's careful analytical work without taking on its soteriological frame.

## 6. Thermodynamics and Energy Flow

The physics of thermodynamics, particularly far-from-equilibrium thermodynamics in the lineage of Ilya Prigogine, offers a rigorous account of energy flow, entropy increase, gradients, and dissipative structures.

### What it offers

The core primitives are **energy** (a conserved quantity at the physical stratum), **entropy** (a measure of disorder that increases in closed systems), **gradients** (differences in concentration or potential that drive flow), **dissipation** (entropy production through irreversible processes), and **dissipative structures** (organised patterns that maintain themselves by importing low-entropy material and exporting high-entropy material).

### What it does well

Thermodynamics is the most rigorous existing account of flow at the physical stratum. The entropy concept captures the asymmetry of time (the second law) in a way few other frameworks do. Dissipative structures provide a model for how patterns can persist far from equilibrium by maintaining flow through themselves.

### What it does not offer

Thermodynamics is **physical-stratum only**. The concepts do not translate cleanly to higher strata. "Social entropy" and "intellectual entropy" are at best loose analogies; treating them as direct extensions of the physical concept commits exactly the stratum-collapse the framework prohibits.

**Conservation of energy** does not apply to value-flow at higher strata. Value can be created (high-quality bindings) and destroyed (low-quality bindings); energy cannot. The mismatch is foundational.

### DQ boundary check

**At risk of metaphysical inflation**. Thermodynamic analogies are analytically legitimate as static-pattern descriptions of how flow-maintained structures persist. Prigogine's dissipative structures, taken analytically, describe a real pattern type — structures sustained by flow through them. The DQ-boundary risk arises when this analytical content is inflated metaphysically — when "order out of chaos" is read not as a description of how certain pattern-types operate but as a capture of how all order ultimately arises. The analytical content is borrowable; the inflated metaphysical claim is what the framework must reject. The distinction matters because thermodynamics has genuine analytical content the dynamics half can use; treating the whole tradition as DQ-violating would miss what's actually borrowable from it.

### What is adaptable

The **gradient concept** — that flow tends to occur from high potential to low potential — may inform how value-flow couples between patterns. A pattern with high accumulated value-capacity may flow into a pattern with lower capacity, the way water flows down a gradient.

The **dissipation concept** — that flow can be lost to entropy production — may inform how value is lost in low-quality bindings. Coercion's degradation of the field could be characterised as value-flow that produces high-entropy outcomes (counter-pattern formation, breakdown) rather than maintaining or amplifying high-quality flow.

The **far-from-equilibrium dynamics** concept may inform persistence character. High-quality patterns may be far-from-equilibrium dissipative structures that maintain themselves by flowing value through them.

### Adoption cost

The cost is the constant risk of stratum collapse. Thermodynamic concepts must be used as **inspiration** for higher-stratum dynamics primitives, never as direct analogies. The temptation to say "social systems obey thermodynamic laws" is exactly the reductionism the framework prohibits.

### Verdict

**Useful as conceptual inspiration but must not be applied as direct analogy.** Recommend inspiration-level adoption of:
- Gradient as a metaphor for value-flow direction
- Dissipation as a metaphor for value-loss in low-quality bindings
- Dissipative structures as a metaphor for high-quality patterns sustained by flow

Recommend explicit rejection of:
- Energy conservation as an assumption about value-flow
- Direct application of thermodynamic laws to higher strata
- Operationalisation of emergence via dissipative structures

## 7. Complex Adaptive Systems / Complexity Theory

Complexity theory, associated with the Santa Fe Institute and a broad interdisciplinary movement since the 1980s, treats systems of many interacting components as producing **emergent** behaviour not predictable from the components alone.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **emergence** (collective behaviour arising from local interaction rules), **self-organisation** (the appearance of order without central direction), **attractors** (states or trajectories the system tends toward), **phase transitions** (qualitative shifts in system behaviour), and **adaptive landscapes** (fitness surfaces over which systems evolve).

### What it does well

Complexity theory recognises that systems can produce behaviour not predictable from components, which is a useful corrective to reductive analysis. The attractor concept gives vocabulary for stable patterns the system "settles into." Phase transition concepts capture qualitative shifts.

### What it does not offer

Complexity theory **systematically operationalises emergence**. The whole point of the tradition is to give an account of how complex behaviour arises from interaction rules — which is precisely the operationalisation of novelty the framework forbids. The "edge of chaos" concept, the "rules of complexity," the "laws of self-organisation" are all attempts to specify the mechanism by which the new comes into the world.

Much complexity science is **reductive in a more sophisticated way than classical reductionism**. Everything reduces to interaction rules among components, even when the components are themselves complex. The framework prohibits this kind of reduction across strata.

Complexity theory is **largely value-neutral**. The "fitness" in adaptive landscapes is whatever the modeller specifies; the tradition itself does not have a built-in concept of value or quality.

### DQ boundary check

**Vulnerable to metaphysical inflation rather than fundamentally at odds**. Complexity theory's project of giving conceptual accounts of emergence is legitimate static-pattern inquiry into change processes; building theories of how complex behaviour arises is exactly the kind of intellectual work the project also engages in. The vulnerability is at the metaphysical level: complexity theory in some hands inflates into the claim that emergence-as-described captures the structure of becoming itself, or that "edge of chaos" identifies where novelty truly comes from. These overreaches the framework must reject. But used analytically — as a vocabulary for describing observed dynamics of pattern stability, threshold transitions, and multi-scale organisation — complexity theory has substantial content. Used metaphysically as a capture of novelty's source, it crosses the boundary. The discrimination is between the analytical content (borrowable) and the metaphysical inflation (rejected).

### What is adaptable

The **attractor concept** may apply to stable value patterns: patterns that are stable in the sense that field-dynamics tend to return to them after perturbation. The **phase transition concept** may apply to collapse-and-repair dynamics: qualitative shifts in field state when a threshold is crossed.

But these adoptions must be made with extreme care to avoid importing the operationalisation of emergence that comes with them.

### Adoption cost

The cost is high. Complexity theory's foundational commitments are at odds with the project's commitments. Adopting concepts from complexity theory without adopting its operationalisation of emergence requires constant discipline.

### Verdict

**Adopt selectively, with discrimination between analytical content and metaphysical inflation.** The vocabulary is suggestive and the analytical content is real; the underlying ontology of strong emergence-as-mechanism is what must be guarded against. Recommend cautious adoption of:
- Attractor as a vocabulary for stable value patterns
- Phase transition as a vocabulary for qualitative shifts in field state
- Adaptive landscape as a metaphor for pattern selection (with explicit non-utility-reduction)

Recommend explicit rejection of:
- Strong-emergence claims framed as captures of how novelty comes into being
- "Laws of complexity" or "rules of self-organisation" as universal mechanisms
- The reductive ontology in which higher-stratum phenomena are explained as outputs of lower-stratum interaction rules

This is the tradition where metaphysical inflation is most common in the literature. Anything borrowed must come with explicit guardrails against importing the inflation along with the analytical content.

## 8. Economic Flow and Capital Theory

Economic theory, particularly post-classical and Marxist political economy, provides detailed accounts of how value (in the form of capital, money, or commodities) circulates, accumulates, and is transformed through social processes.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **circulation** (flow of value through exchanges), **accumulation** (the durable buildup of value over time), **surplus value** (Marxist: the value produced by labour beyond what is paid to labour), **multiplier effects** (how spending propagates through an economy), and **crisis dynamics** (how circulation breakdown produces collapse).

### What it does well

Economic theory has the most developed account of accumulation dynamics among the surveyed traditions. The multiplier effect captures amplification in a structurally explicit way. Crisis dynamics, particularly in heterodox economics, capture collapse-and-recovery cycles. Marxist analysis, despite its ideological loading, has produced detailed examination of how value-flow can be diverted, captured, and accumulated asymmetrically.

### What it does not offer

Economic theory **reduces value to commodity or money**. The "value" in economic theory is exchange-value or use-value in commodity terms; it is not value in the MOQ sense. The reduction is foundational to the tradition's analytical apparatus.

Mainstream economics is often **ideologically committed** to particular value frameworks (utility maximisation, market efficiency) that are not consistent with the project's MOQ grounding. Marxist economics has its own ideological commitments (historical materialism, class struggle as universal) that similarly do not transfer.

Economic theory is **single-stratum** (social and intellectual, intermixed). It does not have an account of biological-stratum or physical-stratum value-flow that integrates with its social-stratum analysis.

### DQ boundary check

**Holds**, in the sense that economics does not claim to operationalise novelty itself. It treats novelty as exogenous (innovations come from outside the model) and analyses how circulation and accumulation respond to it.

### What is adaptable

The **accumulation dynamic** in capital theory parallels the accumulation Circle/Ripple Theory and the power entry have identified. Capital accumulates by being invested in productive capacity that generates more capital; power accumulates by being deployed in coordinative capacity that generates more power. The structural parallel is real.

**Multiplier effects** parallel binding amplification. When investment in one sector produces secondary spending in others, the structural dynamic resembles how power feeds the flow of bound patterns and amplifies them.

**Crisis dynamics** parallel collapse-and-repair cycles. Marxist crisis theory in particular has detailed accounts of how circulation breakdowns produce systemic restructuring, which parallels Circle/Ripple Theory's collapse-and-repair cycle.

**Value-flow configurations** — diversion, capture, extraction — have detailed treatment in critical political economy. The framework's value-flow configurations (diversion, capture, parasitism, betrayal) can borrow analytical depth from this tradition without importing its ideological commitments.

### Adoption cost

The cost is rejection of the value-as-money reduction. Adopting economic-theory concepts requires explicit reframing: capital accumulation as one *instance* of value-flow accumulation, not as the model for value-flow accumulation.

### Verdict

**Adaptable selectively, with explicit reframing.** Concepts useful for adoption:
- Accumulation dynamics (already in the power and Circle/Ripple framework)
- Multiplier effects as inspiration for binding amplification
- Crisis dynamics as a developed treatment of collapse cycles
- Value-flow configurations: diversion, capture, extraction (from critical political economy)

Recommend explicit reframing:
- Value-flow is broader than economic value; the framework's concept of value is MOQ-grounded, not money-grounded
- The configurations are analytical patterns; the ideological commitments of any specific tradition (Marxist, neoclassical, Austrian) are not adopted

## 9. Game Theory and Evolutionary Dynamics

Game theory, originating with von Neumann and Morgenstern and extended through evolutionary game theory (Maynard Smith, Axelrod), formalises strategic interaction among agents with preferences.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **agents with preferences**, **strategies** they can adopt, **payoffs** that result from strategy combinations, **equilibria** (stable strategy configurations), **repeated games** (where interactions continue over time, enabling reputation and conditional cooperation), and **evolutionary stable strategies** (strategies that resist invasion by alternatives once adopted by enough of a population).

### What it does well

Game theory captures multi-agent value-flow at the strategic level. Reciprocity dynamics are particularly well-modelled: tit-for-tat in iterated Prisoner's Dilemma, evolutionary stability of cooperation under repeated interaction, the conditions under which cooperation can be sustained versus when defection takes over. Signaling theory captures how observable signals propagate through populations.

### What it does not offer

Game theory is **utility-reductive** at its foundations. Agents have preference orderings; outcomes have utility values; choices maximise expected utility. The framework collapses value into a single comparable scalar, which is exactly what MOQ refuses.

Standard game theory is **static-equilibrium-oriented**. The "dynamics" in game theory is often pursued just to find equilibrium states; the trajectory between states is treated as theoretical machinery. Evolutionary game theory partially corrects this but still tends toward equilibrium analysis.

Game theory is **stratum-blind**. It treats biological, social, and intellectual interactions with the same game-theoretic vocabulary, with no equivalent of the MOQ stratum discipline.

### DQ boundary check

**Mostly holds**, in the sense that game theory does not claim to operationalise novelty itself. Strategies are taken as given; the framework analyses interactions among them.

### What is adaptable

**Iterated/repeated game dynamics** are valuable for understanding reciprocity stabilisation. The conditions under which cooperation is stable in repeated interaction, the role of reputation, the discount rate on future interactions — these are well-developed and applicable to the dynamics of reciprocity, trust, and betrayal.

**Evolutionary dynamics** — particularly replicator dynamics — may inform pattern selection and persistence. Patterns that produce stable outcomes in field interactions tend to persist; patterns that produce unstable outcomes tend to be displaced.

**Signaling models** are relevant to reputation/standing dynamics. How signals propagate through populations, how their fidelity degrades, how strategic agents can manipulate them — all bear on phenomena like shame's distributed signaling.

### Adoption cost

The cost is rejection of utility-reduction. Game theory's elegance depends on collapsing value into a scalar; the framework cannot do this.

### Verdict

**Some specific concepts adaptable, with explicit reframing.** Recommend selective adoption of:
- Iterated game dynamics as a vocabulary for reciprocity stabilisation
- Evolutionary stability concepts as a vocabulary for pattern persistence
- Signaling models as a vocabulary for reputation and standing dynamics

Recommend explicit rejection of:
- Utility-reduction as a foundation
- The assumption of well-defined preferences over outcomes
- Equilibrium-orientation as the analytical goal

## 10. Information Theory

Shannon's information theory, developed in the 1940s, provides a rigorous account of how information is transmitted through channels with bandwidth and noise.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **information content** (measured in bits, related to surprise), **channel capacity** (maximum information transmissible), **noise** (corruption of signal during transmission), **bandwidth** (rate of information flow), and **mutual information** (shared information between sources).

### What it does well

Information theory is the most rigorous existing account of how signals propagate through media with finite capacity. The signal-to-noise distinction is structurally clean.

### What it does not offer

**Information is not value**. Shannon was explicit that his theory deals with the form of communication, not its meaning. Two messages with equal information content can have completely different value; two messages with equal value can have completely different information content.

Information theory is **stratum-flat** by design. The same mathematical framework applies whether the information is biological signaling, social communication, or intellectual transmission.

### DQ boundary check

**Holds**. Information theory does not claim to operationalise meaning, value, or novelty.

### What is adaptable

The **signal-to-noise distinction** may apply to reputation propagation: clear signals about a target's deficiency propagate differently from noisy ones. **Bandwidth limitations** may apply to coordination capacity: a field can only process so many simultaneous regulatory signals.

These adoptions are narrow but useful for specific phenomena.

### Adoption cost

Low, because the adoptions are narrow and the parent tradition does not have ideological commitments incompatible with the project.

### Verdict

**Limited but specific utility.** Adopt narrowly for specific phenomena (reputation propagation, signaling bandwidth). Do not treat information theory as a foundational framework for value-flow; it is a useful tool for one aspect of one kind of flow.

## 11. Ecological Flow Theory

Ecology has developed detailed accounts of energy and matter flow through ecosystems, particularly through trophic levels (primary producers → primary consumers → secondary consumers → decomposers) and biogeochemical cycles.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **trophic flows** (energy passing from one trophic level to the next, with characteristic losses at each step), **nutrient cycles** (matter circulating through living and non-living components), **ecosystem services** (the value an ecosystem provides), **resilience** (capacity to absorb disturbance without collapse), and **succession** (long-term changes in ecosystem composition).

### What it does well

Ecological flow theory captures **cross-stratum cascade** explicitly — energy flowing from the physical stratum (sunlight) through biological levels with characteristic losses at each step. The cascade discipline is built into ecological thinking in a way few other traditions match.

**Resilience** as a concept is well-developed: the ability of an ecosystem to absorb disturbance, the existence of multiple stable states, the role of redundancy in maintaining function under stress.

### What it does not offer

Ecological flow theory is **biological-stratum-focused**. Its concepts do not transfer cleanly to social or intellectual strata. Trophic cascades have biological substrate that does not exist at higher strata in the same form.

It is **value-neutral by default**. Ecosystem services have value to humans, but the ecological flow itself is treated as just flow.

### DQ boundary check

**Holds**. Ecological flow theory does not claim to operationalise novelty.

### What is adaptable

**Cascade dynamics** across strata — already incorporated into framework v2.1 as the direct-vs-cascaded modulation distinction. The dynamics half can build on this by treating the cascade across strata as a fundamental dynamics pattern.

**Resilience** as a concept may apply to field-level dynamics: the capacity of a field to absorb perturbation without collapse, the role of redundancy in patterns that share regulatory function.

**Trophic concepts** may inform pattern hierarchies: how some patterns are foundational (analogous to primary producers) and others depend on them (analogous to higher trophic levels).

### Adoption cost

Modest. The biological-stratum framing is the main thing to leave behind.

### Verdict

**Modestly useful.** Adopt:
- Cascade dynamics across strata (already in framework v2.1; reinforce)
- Resilience as a field-level concept
- Trophic structure as an analogy for pattern dependence hierarchies

## 12. Neuroscience and Predictive Processing

Neuroscience, particularly the predictive processing / active inference framework developed by Karl Friston, Andy Clark, and others, offers detailed mechanistic accounts of how brains form and update patterns of expectation through prediction-error minimisation.

### What it offers

The most relevant analytical concepts include **predictive processing** (brains as systems that continuously predict incoming signals and update internal models based on prediction errors), **active inference** (Friston's extension treating action as also driven by prediction-error minimisation), **reward prediction error** (a specific computational model of value-related learning at the dopaminergic level), **Hebbian learning** ("cells that fire together wire together" as a principle of pattern formation), **neural plasticity** (the brain's capacity to change with use), **distributed representation** (knowledge as patterns of activation across neural populations rather than discrete symbols), and **large-scale network organisation** (default mode network, task-positive network, salience network, and their interactions).

### What it does well

Neuroscience offers **detailed mechanistic accounts of pattern formation and change at the biological stratum**. Predictive processing in particular offers a unified framework for cognition that has gathered substantial empirical support across perception, motor control, learning, and clinical disorders. The tradition is **empirically grounded** in measurable phenomena (neural recordings, neuroimaging, behavioural experiments) in ways that few other traditions surveyed here are.

Predictive processing has a structurally interesting feature relevant to the project: it treats the brain not as a passive receiver but as an active prediction-generator continuously testing predictions against incoming signals. This is **process-oriented** in a way that aligns with Circle/Ripple Theory's treatment of nodes as active rather than passive.

The framework offers a specific mechanistic account of how **priming** might work at the biological stratum: cumulative prior experience shapes the prediction models that determine how present signals are interpreted. This parallels Circle/Ripple Theory's "priming" concept without operationalising it across strata.

### What it does not offer / problems

Neuroscience has **strong reductive tendencies**. The default move in much of the literature is to treat higher-stratum phenomena (consciousness, meaning, value, social patterns) as ultimately reducible to neural patterns — what is sometimes called "neurocentrism." The "neural correlates of consciousness" research project is a particularly clear case of stratum-collapse: it treats consciousness as the kind of thing that has neural correlates rather than as a higher-stratum phenomenon arising from biological substrate.

Neurophysiologisation parallels psychologisation as a misframing of value patterns. The user has been explicit that psychologisation is a dead end for value description; the same discipline applies to neurophysiologisation. Treating shame, guilt, power, or coercion as "neural phenomena" or as fundamentally located in brain states would mislocate the patterns just as treating them as fundamentally psychological does. Patterns at the social and intellectual strata depend on biological substrate (including neural substrate) but are not constituted by it.

The framework is often **value-neutral**. Predictive processing describes how prediction-error-minimisation operates without specifying what value the predicted states have. Reward prediction error specifies value at a narrow computational level that does not transfer cleanly to value in the MOQ sense.

The tradition's empirical grounding, while a strength, is also a constraint: phenomena that resist laboratory operationalisation tend to be marginalised or reduced to operationalisable proxies, which biases the field's conceptual development.

### DQ boundary check

**Mixed, with the dominant risk being stratum-collapse rather than direct DQ-inflation**. Neuroscience as analytical description of biological-stratum patterns holds the boundary cleanly. The boundary issues arise when biological-stratum descriptions are inflated into accounts of all cognition, meaning, or value — which is how the tradition is often deployed. This is stratum-collapse rather than direct DQ-operationalisation, but it is a closely related discipline failure. Predictive processing offered as the mechanism of mind generally crosses the boundary; offered as the mechanism by which the biological stratum's prediction patterns operate, it does not.

### What is adaptable

**Predictive processing as inspiration for how priming works at the biological stratum**. Circle/Ripple Theory's priming concept can borrow from predictive processing's account of how prior experience shapes present interpretation — at the biological stratum specifically, without inflating into higher-stratum claims.

**Reward prediction error as a specific model of value-related learning at the biological stratum**. Within the biological stratum's treatment of how organisms learn from outcomes, the reward prediction error model is genuinely useful. Cross-stratum application is what must be avoided.

**Hebbian-style co-activation as inspiration for how cumulative ripples form priming**. The Hebbian principle ("cells that fire together wire together") generalises into a principle that "patterns that co-occur tend to bind." At the biological stratum specifically, this is a useful mechanistic account.

**Distributed representation as inspiration for how patterns can be present without being localised**. The idea that knowledge is patterns of activation across populations rather than discrete symbols may inform how the framework treats patterns as distributed across the nodes of a field.

### Adoption cost

High discipline required. The reductive tendencies of the tradition must be actively guarded against. Neuroscience can contribute to biological-stratum-specific accounts; it must not be allowed to claim the whole. The discipline against psychologisation extends naturally to neurophysiologisation: do not import biological-stratum descriptions to higher-stratum patterns as their constituting reality.

### Verdict

**Useful for biological-stratum specifics only, with stratum-discipline guardrails throughout**. Adopt narrowly for biological-stratum priming dynamics and biological-stratum value-related learning. Recommend explicit adoption of:
- Predictive processing as inspiration for biological-stratum priming mechanisms
- Reward prediction error for biological-stratum value-learning specifically
- Distributed representation as inspiration for non-localised pattern presence

Recommend explicit rejection of:
- Neurophysiologisation of higher-stratum patterns (parallel to the project's discipline against psychologisation)
- "Neural correlates of consciousness/value/meaning" framings as captures of those higher-stratum phenomena
- Reduction of higher-stratum patterns to neural patterns of any sophistication

## 13. Field Theory (Physics)

Physics field theory — from classical electromagnetism through quantum field theory — provides a rigorous account of fields as continuous structures that mediate interactions between particles or other localised entities.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **fields** (continuous spatial structures with values at every point), **potentials** (scalar or vector fields that determine the behaviour of test particles), **gradients** (directional rates of change in field values), **field interactions** (how multiple fields couple to each other), and **particles as field excitations** (in quantum field theory).

### What it does well

Field theory is the most developed account of action-at-a-distance via continuous medium. The field concept captures the medium of propagation rigorously. Gradients capture directional flow.

### What it does not offer

Physics field theory is **highly mathematical**, with formalisms (Lagrangians, Hamiltonians, tensor fields) that do not transfer to higher strata.

**Conservation assumptions** are foundational and again do not transfer cleanly to value-flow.

### DQ boundary check

**Holds**. Physics field theory does not operationalise novelty; it describes the structure of fields and their interactions.

### What is adaptable

The **field concept** is already largely incorporated through Circle/Ripple Theory's "value field." Physics field theory may provide inspiration for formalising the field concept further: a value field has values at every point in some sense, with gradients between points and interactions among multiple fields where they overlap.

**Gradient concepts** may apply to value-flow direction: a high-quality node embedded in a low-quality field has a gradient that drives flow.

**Field interactions** — how fields couple to each other — may inform the dynamics of multiple overlapping value fields and the cross-field structural costs the matrix snapshot does not capture.

### Adoption cost

Low, because the adoption is largely conceptual (the field concept is already in the project). The mathematical formalism is not what the project needs.

### Verdict

**Already largely incorporated; reinforce.** Continue the field concept from Circle/Ripple Theory, with field-theory-inspired refinements:
- Gradient as a vocabulary for value-flow direction
- Field interactions as a vocabulary for overlapping value fields

## 14. Phenomenology of Time (Husserl, Heidegger)

The phenomenological tradition, particularly Husserl's analysis of internal time-consciousness and Heidegger's existential analytic of temporality, gives an account of time as constituted in experience rather than as a neutral container in which events occur.

### What it offers

The core primitives include **retention** (how the past is held in present consciousness), **protention** (how the future is anticipated in present consciousness), **the temporal flow of experience** as distinct from objective clock time, and Heidegger's **temporality** as the basic structure of being-in-the-world (past as having-been, present as making-present, future as coming-toward).

### What it does well

Phenomenology of time gives a deep account of temporal structure as it actually shows up in experience, distinct from the abstract time of physics. This is important because some value-flow phenomena have temporal characters that clock time does not capture — the way priming accumulates non-linearly, the way certain ripples remain "live" in agents over years.

### What it does not offer

Phenomenology of time is **focused on individual consciousness**. The user has been clear throughout the project that psychologisation is a "dead end" for value description, and phenomenology of time is paradigmatically psychological in its method.

Heideggerian ontology has its own substantial **metaphysical commitments** that are not the project's.

### DQ boundary check

**Mixed**. The traditions do not directly operationalise novelty, but Heidegger's accounts of authenticity and the ecstatic temporality of Dasein can be read in ways that come close.

### What is adaptable

The **distinction between lived time and clock time** is potentially valuable, though it must be reframed away from individual consciousness toward the temporal character of value-flow patterns themselves (which can also be argued to have temporal structures not reducible to clock time).

**Retention/protention** may inform priming/expectation, again with reframing away from consciousness toward the structural carrying-forward of past flow into present pattern operation.

### Adoption cost

High, given the user's stated preference for non-psychological framings. Adoption would require significant reframing.

### Verdict

**Limited direct utility given the project's discipline against psychologisation.** Recommend not adopting at the foundational level. May serve as a check-in resource when temporal-scale questions arise, but the foundational vocabulary should come from elsewhere.

## Synthesis

Across the fourteen traditions, several patterns emerge:

**No external tradition offers what the project needs as a whole.** Each tradition offers some concepts that are adaptable, but each also carries commitments incompatible with the project's MOQ grounding. This parallels the finding of the statics survey: no existing framework fits.

**Circle/Ripple Theory is the foundation.** It is the only existing body of work that combines MOQ grounding, stratum discipline, DQ-boundary preservation, and substantive content about value-flow. The dynamics half should formalise and extend Circle/Ripple Theory, not depart from it.

**The Buddhist tradition is the closest philosophical ally on the DQ-boundary discipline specifically.** Its emptiness teaching aligns structurally with the project's discipline against operationalising Dynamic Quality; conceptual capture is never identical to what is captured. This alignment makes the Buddhist analytical content particularly relevant.

**Several traditions offer specific adaptable primitives.** The most useful adoptions:

**Several concepts must be explicitly rejected or guarded against.** The most important rejections, reframed in light of the corrected DQ-boundary understanding (rejection of metaphysical inflation, not of inquiry into change):

- **Metaphysical inflation of change-accounts into captures of becoming-itself.** Having theories of emergence, novelty, change, or becoming is legitimate static-pattern inquiry — what the DQ boundary forbids is the metaphysical claim that any such account has captured Dynamic Quality itself. Complexity theory, thermodynamics, and process philosophy each contain analytical content that can be borrowed alongside metaphysical inflation that must be rejected. The discrimination is between the two layers, not blanket rejection of either tradition.
- **Energy / information / utility conservation** as assumptions about value-flow. Value can be created and destroyed; flow is not conserved.
- **Stratum collapse** through cross-stratum direct application of any tradition. Particularly tempting from thermodynamics, complexity theory, and neuroscience. The corresponding discipline at each stratum: physical concepts do not transfer directly to higher strata; biological-stratum mechanisms do not constitute higher-stratum patterns; specific inflated claims of cross-stratum reduction (neural correlates of consciousness/value/meaning, social systems "obeying thermodynamic laws", "edge of chaos" as the source of all genuine novelty) are rejected.
- **Value reduction to a single scalar** (utility, money, energy, information). The framework's whole point depends on rejecting this.
- **Psychologisation** at the foundation, per the user's stated discipline.
- **Neurophysiologisation** as the parallel discipline at the biological stratum: higher-stratum patterns are not constituted by their neural substrate, even though they depend on it.
- **Soteriological framings** from Buddhist tradition (path to liberation, specific cosmological claims) — the analytical content is borrowable; the religious-soteriological framework is not.

## Recommendation

The dynamics half should be constructed as a **formalisation and extension of Circle/Ripple Theory**, drawing selectively on the adaptable primitives identified above.

Specifically, Task 2 (the dynamics framework construction) should:

1. **Begin from Circle/Ripple Theory** — the existing vocabulary (ripple, field, priming, reserves, circle, keychain, collapse-repair-re-entry) is the starting point, not a reference among others. The construction's job is to formalise these concepts at the architectural level the statics half achieved.

2. **Adopt the stock/flow distinction explicitly** as a formal primitive. Priming, reserves, and accumulated capacity are stocks; ripples and binding-flows are flows. This is the cleanest single adoption from systems dynamics.

3. **Adopt feedback loops as a formal vocabulary** for reinforcing and balancing cycles, applied to phenomena already in the project (reciprocity stabilisation, repair cycles, escalation dynamics).

4. **Adopt delays as an explicit temporal primitive** for time-lagged effects.

5. **Adopt cascade as a cross-stratum dynamics primitive** (already in framework v2.1; reinforce with ecological-flow-inspired discipline).

6. **Adopt gradient as a metaphor for value-flow direction**, drawing inspiration from thermodynamics and field theory without importing their conservation assumptions.

7. **Adopt the binding-relation as the primary new structural primitive**, building on the gap identified in the power migration. This is the dynamics half's central addition. The binding-relation is the value-flow coupling by which one pattern feeds another's flow.

8. **Adopt accumulation and amplification as derived primitives** dependent on binding, building on Circle/Ripple Theory's existing treatment.

9. **Adopt the dissipation concept** as a vocabulary for value-loss in low-quality bindings.

10. **Distinguish value-flow configurations from value-flow primitives.** Diversion, capture, extraction, parasitism, betrayal are configurations — characteristic ways primitives combine — not primitives themselves.

11. **Hold the DQ boundary explicitly throughout.** No primitive describes where value comes from; all primitives describe how value moves among existing patterns. The framework v2.1 signpost language is the discipline: "value-flow describes how value moves among existing patterns; it does not describe where new value comes from."

12. **Test the resulting framework against the migrated patterns** (power, coercion, guilt, shame) before promoting any element to Canon. This was the discipline applied to the statics half and should be applied here.

The framework's authorship attribution should be the same as the statics half: Pirsig provides the MOQ foundation, Spørkel authors the project's framework including its dynamics half, and the survey above documents what is borrowed (with explicit attribution) from external traditions.

## Closing Note

The dynamics half is the framework's largest outstanding construction. This survey has demonstrated that the work cannot be outsourced to any existing tradition — none of the twelve surveyed offers what the project needs as a whole. But the work is not from scratch either: Circle/Ripple Theory provides substantial existing vocabulary, and several external traditions offer specific adaptable primitives that can be integrated with explicit discipline about what is and is not being adopted.

The construction proper, Task 2, can now begin from a clear understanding of what is being built on, what is being borrowed, and what is being explicitly rejected. The DQ boundary remains the most easily violated commitment; the construction must guard it actively at every step — understanding the boundary as a constraint on metaphysical inflation rather than as a constraint on inquiry into change itself.
