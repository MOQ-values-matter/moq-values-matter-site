## What This Framework Does

The statics framework (v2.1) describes value patterns as they stand: their composition, substrate, modulation, persistence, profile-coherence. It answers the question *what is a pattern and how is it structured*. It cannot answer *how value moves among patterns, what couples patterns to each other, how flow builds, propagates, dissipates, and shifts over time*. That second set of questions is what this framework — the dynamics half — exists to address.

This framework is built on two foundations. The first is **Circle/Ripple Theory**, the project's prior work on value-flow authored by Kim Roar Spørkel and developed over roughly two decades of applied use. The survey (Task 1) established that Circle/Ripple Theory is not one tradition among many but the project's existing analytical work on exactly this territory, and the dynamics half's job is to **formalise and extend** it — not to depart from it, not to replace it. The vocabulary of ripples, fields, priming, reserves, circles, keychains, and the collapse-repair-re-entry cycle is carried forward and given the architectural rigour the statics framework achieved for the four strata.

The second foundation is the set of **adaptable primitives** identified in the survey: the stock/flow distinction and feedback loops from systems dynamics, graph-theoretic representation and capacity-cut analysis from network flow theory, dependent origination from the Buddhist tradition, predictive processing for biological-stratum priming, cascade dynamics from ecological flow, gradient and dissipation concepts from thermodynamics and field theory, accumulation and crisis dynamics from critical political economy, iterated game dynamics from game theory. Each is borrowed with explicit attribution and disciplined against the inflated metaphysical claims that sometimes accompany them in the source traditions.

A reading guide. Section 1 establishes the framework's foundational commitments — the six non-negotiable claims that ground the dynamics half. Section 2 defines the six primitives of value-flow and how they relate to each other. Section 3 treats the field as the medium of propagation, formalising Circle/Ripple Theory's field concept. Section 4 treats configurations — the characteristic ways primitives combine, including feedback loops, value-flow configurations like diversion and capture, and the collapse-repair-re-entry cycle. Section 5 addresses temporal dimensions of value-flow. Section 6 treats cross-stratum dynamics. Section 7 specifies the relationship between dynamics and statics, including how pattern entries integrate dynamics observations. Section 8 maps traceability — what is borrowed from where. Section 9 covers implications, limits, and the disciplines that must be held going forward.

## 1. Foundational Commitments

The dynamics framework rests on six commitments, each of which constrains the construction and prevents specific drift errors.

### 1.1 Value-flow is real, not metaphorical

When the framework speaks of value flowing between patterns, this is not a poetic gloss on conceptual relations. Value-flow is a real phenomenon: patterns affect each other through actual movement of value across the field, and this movement is what makes the dynamics observable in the first place. Without real value-flow, the question of how patterns interact has no traction — they would be merely conceptually related, not dynamically coupled.

This commitment matters because it distinguishes the dynamics framework from purely descriptive accounts of pattern relations. The statics framework describes patterns and their relations as they stand; the dynamics framework describes the value-flow that constitutes those relations in operation. The relation between two patterns is real because value moves between them; the framework's task is to articulate how.

### 1.2 Value-flow has real and tractable quality differences

Value-flow is a real phenomenon, and patterns differ substantially in the value-flow they enable. Some patterns recruit, coordinate, and amplify flow more effectively than others; some bindings carry flow with high fidelity to their structural purpose, others with low fidelity; some fields organise their members' contributions into coordinated coherent flow, others fragment them; some configurations sustain the conditions of their own continued operation, others deplete the reserves their operation depends on. The framework's analytical commitment is to make these differences traceable and articulable.

The framework does not commit to a metaphysical claim about whether value is conserved across all redistributions. The traditions surveyed (thermodynamics, information theory, network flow) assume conservation in their own analytical domains; the framework borrows their analytical machinery (gradient, dissipation, capacity, max-flow) without adopting their conservation assumptions, but it also does not assert the opposite — that value is metaphysically created or destroyed. Whether the redirection of an agent's loyalty-contribution from one field to another counts as "loss" for the field receiving less and "creation" for the field receiving more, or simply as redirection of what the agent contributes, is a metaphysical question the framework leaves open. What the framework claims operationally is that the differences between fields, bindings, and patterns are real, traceable, and concern enabling capacity and direction.

This commitment matters for how the framework treats persistent patterns whose intellectual-stratum analysis is less than glowing. Loyalty, hierarchy, in-group preference, ritual compliance, and other patterns that persist robustly across cultures and historical periods are sometimes treated dismissively by intellectual-stratum critique as "primitive" or "regressive." The framework rejects this stance. Patterns persist because they enable value at their stratum, often substantially. Loyalty as a value pattern persists because fields composed of loyal agents have coordinative advantages over fields without it — the 1000 loyal coordinated agents in a coherent circle outperform 50 better-equipped but uncoordinated agents because the social-stratum value-flow loyalty enables exceeds what its absence permits. Whether loyalty is high-quality from an intellectual-stratum perspective is a different question from whether it enables value at the social stratum, and the framework's discipline is to keep these questions analytically distinct.

The framework's commitment to quality differences therefore rests on:
- differences in **enabling capacity** (some patterns enable more value-flow than others),
- differences in **direction** (some patterns direct flow toward stratum-preserving uses, others toward stratum-destructive uses),
- differences in **fidelity** (some bindings carry flow faithfully to its structural purpose, others distort or redirect it),
- differences in **durability** (some patterns sustain the conditions of their own continued operation, others deplete those conditions).

These differences are real and traceable. They do not require a metaphysical claim about whether value-flow is conserved across all transformations.

### 1.3 Value-flow respects but does not reduce to substrate

Flow at one stratum has substrate dependence on the strata below it — social value-flow depends on biological substrate, biological value-flow depends on physical substrate. But flow at each stratum has its own character that does not reduce to flow at the substrate stratum. Social value-flow is not "merely biological flow at the social scale"; intellectual value-flow is not "merely social flow with symbolic content." The stratum discipline from the statics framework carries into dynamics: substrate enables, modulation operates across strata, but reduction across strata is prohibited.

Cascade across strata (treated in Section 6) is the legitimate cross-stratum dynamics phenomenon. Reduction is its illegitimate counterpart and is explicitly rejected.

### 1.4 Value-flow is field-relative and objective; agents and fields are co-constitutive

The Non-Relative Value Hierarchy principle from Circle/Ripple Theory carries directly into the dynamics framework. The same flow can produce different real effects in different fields — what amplifies in one field may dissipate in another, what binds well in one configuration may extract in another. These differences are objective within their fields, arising from real consequences at the relevant strata. Field-relative valuation is not relativism: the effects are real, just field-specific.

This field-relative analytical move depends on a more foundational claim about agents and fields: they are not separable analytical levels. Agents exist as participants in fields; fields exist as structured regions through the patterns enacted by participating agents. Asking whether a social-stratum value pattern is "really about" the agent or "really about" the field is analogous to asking whether the biological stratum is "really about" cells or organs — the question presupposes a separation that does not exist. The framework therefore does not treat the agent-as-isolate or the field-as-isolate as primary analytical units; analysis operates on agent-field interaction, which is what constitutes both.

This commitment guards against a specific drift the framework must avoid: the Western intellectual tendency to focus on "the agent" — through psychology, medicine, biological intervention, "the self" — while neglecting the field-level patterns through which the agent is constituted. The framework's value patterns are not properties of agents nor properties of fields but properties of the agent-field structure that constitutes both. Treating the symptoms of pattern-formation in agents as if they were causes that could be controlled by intervening on the agent alone is exactly the misframing this commitment rejects. When an agent's loyalty-contribution moves from one field to another, this is a structural redirection involving the agent's embeddedness in both fields, not "the agent's choice" abstracted from the fields involved. When a field's coordinative capacity is described (the 1000 marching on 50), this is the field's enabling-of-coordinated-agent-flow, not "the field's property" abstracted from the agents composing it.

The bearer_mode distinction in pattern entries (agent_only, multi_bearer_minimum_two, group_only) captures the structural fact that some patterns operate intra-agent (guilt), some operate inter-agent (shame, coercion), and some operate at field-coordinative scale (loyalty, reciprocity) — but in all cases the analysis is of agent-field interaction at the relevant scale, not of agent-as-isolate or field-as-isolate. This commitment grounds the framework's treatment of value-flow as having different characters in different fields without thereby collapsing into "value-flow is merely what observers say it is" relativism.

### 1.5 Dynamic Quality is not capturable

This is the framework's discipline rather than a substantive claim about how value-flow works. All of the framework's primitives, configurations, and analyses are static-pattern accounts of dynamic processes. The framework does not claim to have captured Dynamic Quality itself, nor to have specified the mechanism by which novelty enters the world. What the framework specifies is how value moves among existing patterns and how patterns change through value-flow; what it does not specify is where the patterns themselves ultimately come from.

The discipline is properly understood as a constraint on metaphysical inflation rather than as a constraint on inquiry. Theories of how change works are legitimate static-pattern inquiry; what the boundary forbids is the inflated claim that any such theory has captured what becoming itself is. The dynamics framework engages substantively with change, propagation, accumulation, and transformation — it just does not claim to have captured Dynamic Quality in its primitives.

### 1.6 Dynamics couples; statics constitutes

The relationship between the dynamics half and the statics half is precise. Statics describes what patterns are — their constituents, their composition, their bearer mode, their characteristic operation. Dynamics describes how patterns interact through value-flow — what couples them, how flow moves between them, how their value-flow profiles shift over time. Dynamics does not constitute patterns; constitution is a statics matter. Dynamics couples patterns; coupling is a dynamics matter.

This division of labour prevents two specific errors: statics-side, the error of trying to treat coupling as constitution (which is what the pre-correction coercion entry did when it treated power asymmetry as a constituent rather than as a cost-source coupling); dynamics-side, the error of trying to treat constitution as coupling (which would dissolve patterns into pure flow with no stable structure). Patterns are constituted statically and coupled dynamically. The two halves operate on the same territory but answer different questions about it.

## 2. The Primitives of Value-Flow

The framework recognises six primitives of value-flow. Each is a fundamental operation that can compose into configurations, but is not itself reducible to other primitives. The primitives are: **propagation**, **binding**, **accumulation**, **dissipation**, **modulation-of-flow**, and **cascade**.

The primitives are derived from three sources in roughly equal measure. Circle/Ripple Theory provides ripple-as-propagation, accumulation-as-priming, and field-modulation as the conceptual ancestors of three primitives. Systems dynamics provides the stock/flow distinction that formalises accumulation. The migration work surfaced binding as the central new primitive and cascade as a cross-stratum dynamics phenomenon (the latter already partially named in framework v2.1 as a modulation modifier).

### 2.1 Propagation

Propagation is the basic phenomenon of value-flow: value at one location or pattern produces effects at other locations or patterns through some coupling or medium. Propagation is what makes value-flow a flow rather than a static distribution. Without propagation, value would be located but not moving.

Circle/Ripple Theory's "ripple" is the conceptual ancestor of propagation: the propagation of value effects caused by action, inaction, or presence, traveling across levels and time, often beyond intent. The dynamics framework adopts ripple as a specific kind of propagation — a propagation that arises from a node's activity and spreads outward through the field — while recognising that propagation itself is more general. Not all propagation is ripple-shaped; some is directed (along bindings), some is diffuse (through fields), some is cascaded (across strata).

Propagation has several characteristics that the framework names:
- **Direction** — propagation can be directional (from source to receiver, along a binding) or diffuse (radiating outward from a source into a field without specific receivers).
- **Magnitude** — the quantity of value that propagates. Not always meaningfully quantifiable, but always comparatively meaningful (more or less).
- **Speed** — propagation can be near-instantaneous (the slap that injures, the signal that propagates electronically) or slow (priming that builds over years, betrayal effects that propagate over decades).
- **Persistence** — propagation can be transient (a brief effect that dissipates quickly) or durable (a propagation whose effects persist long after the originating event).
- **Fidelity** — propagation can be high-fidelity (the value at the source is closely transmitted to the receiver) or low-fidelity (the value is degraded, distorted, or attenuated in transit).

These characteristics are not separate primitives; they are aspects of any specific propagation that the framework can describe.

### 2.2 Binding

Binding is the structural coupling between patterns by which value-flow into one pattern feeds another pattern's flow. Binding is the central new primitive of the dynamics framework, identified through the migrations as the gap the statics framework could not fill — particularly the power migration, where power's defining operation (amplifying what it is bound to) could not be cleanly typed using the statics-side relation vocabulary.

A binding is a specific kind of coupling between two (or more) patterns. It has the following structural characteristics:

- **Directionality** — bindings can be directional (one pattern feeds another's flow but not vice versa), bidirectional (each pattern feeds the other's flow), or coordinated-direction (multiple patterns feed one, or one feeds multiple).
- **Quality** — bindings can be high-quality (amplifying value through the coupling, supporting both patterns' coherent operation) or low-quality (dissipating value through the coupling, distorting one or both patterns). **The structural-character discipline (§9.3, v1.3) applies particularly here:** binding quality describes the structural character of the coupling — whether the binding amplifies both patterns' coherent operation, or distorts one or both. It does not describe field-level outcomes (whether the binding's broader effects on the field are constructive or destructive), sustainability (whether the binding can persist indefinitely), or value-evaluations (whether the pattern's effects are approved). A binding can be structurally high-quality (effective amplification, no distortion of either pattern) and still participate in value-flow configurations that produce massive field-degradation. The Mongol pattern (power bound to violence) is a paradigmatic case: extreme amplification of violence's reach, no structural distortion of either pattern, but operating in extraction-with-substrate-depletion configurations that produced catastrophic field-effects. The binding's quality and the configuration's field-effects are different dimensions. Conflating them imports value-judgment into structural assessment.
- **Strength** — bindings can be tight (significant flow coupling) or loose (minimal flow coupling).
- **Durability** — bindings can be standing (persistently operative) or contingent (operative only under specific conditions).
- **Conditions** — under what circumstances the binding operates.
- **binding_kind** (v1.2) — the structural sub-type of the binding. See discussion below.
- **companion_relation** (v1.2) — cross-reference to the companion binding-record in the other pattern's folder, where one exists. See discussion below.

#### binding_kind (v1.2)

The stress-tests of power and coercion surfaced that bindings, while sharing the binding primitive, operate in two structurally distinct ways. The `binding_kind` field articulates the distinction:

- **amplification-recruitment** — bindings where the source pattern's coordinative structure enables the target pattern to recruit additional flow from the field beyond what the target could coordinate alone. Power's bindings to reciprocity, legitimacy, authority, and other patterns are characteristically of this kind: power binds to amplify what it is bound to. The source pattern provides structure; the target pattern does the work; the field supplies the flow being recruited.

- **cost-source** — bindings where the target pattern's flow is leveraged as substrate by the source pattern. Coercion's bindings to power, guilt, shame, and obligation are characteristically of this kind: coercion binds to be fed by what it is bound to. The target pattern's flow is recruited into the source pattern's mechanism rather than being amplified by the source's coordinative structure.

These two kinds are structurally opposed in how they use binding even though both share the binding primitive. Power binds to amplify-what-it-binds-to; coercion binds to be-fed-by-what-it-binds-to. Same primitive, opposite structural use. The `binding_kind` field articulates the distinction without requiring separate relation types.

The vocabulary is open: more kinds may surface as further patterns are tested. Identified to date:
- `amplification-recruitment` (power's case)
- `cost-source` (coercion's case)
- `substrate-recruitment` (possibly distinct from the above two; may also be subsumed under one of them as analyses surface)

#### companion_relation (v1.2)

When a binding exists between two patterns and each pattern has a relation record from its side, the records describe the same coupling viewed from different sides. They are not redundant; they articulate the coupling's character from each pattern's analytical position. The `companion_relation` field cross-references the companion record in the other pattern's folder, making the two-sided nature explicit while preserving the folder convention (each relation lives in its source pattern's folder).

The first case where this was used substantively is the power-coercion coupling: REL-BND-POW-COE-001 (power's folder, source = power, binding_kind = amplification-recruitment from power's perspective) and REL-BND-COE-PWR-001 (coercion's folder, source = coercion, binding_kind = cost-source from coercion's perspective). Both records describe the same flow direction (power's capacity → coercion's operation), seen from each pattern's side.

Not every binding requires a companion record. Asymmetric companions are permitted: a pattern may have a relation to another without the other pattern having a corresponding record from its side. This is structurally appropriate when one side of the coupling has the relation as a significant analytical feature and the other side does not. Coercion's bindings to guilt, shame, and obligation are currently asymmetric in this sense.

#### Framework-pattern distinction discipline (v1.2)

A discipline that constrains how the binding primitive is used and applied. The pattern-specific analytical content of any particular binding — what makes the bound pattern what it is, what its characteristic dynamics are, what happens when the binding fails — belongs in the bound pattern's entry, not in the binding's structural fields.

This discipline matters specifically for cases involving mimicry, deception, bluff, and other patterns whose operation involves the appearance of being something they are not. The framework's correct analytical move is to recognise the mimicking pattern (bluff, deception, manufactured legitimacy, etc.) as its own value pattern with its own entry, characteristic operation, and characteristic dynamics — including its mimicry relation to the pattern it mimics. The binding from a pattern that uses a mimicking pattern as its target is a binding to the mimicking pattern itself, with the mimicking pattern's relation to the mimicked pattern captured separately.

For example, coercion's "bluff case" — where coercion's operation appears to leverage power but the underlying power is absent or smaller than presented — is not a binding to power with a different substrate-type. It is a binding to bluff (a distinct value pattern that mimics power), with bluff's relation to power captured as the bluff pattern's own mimicry-relation. The framework's binding primitive stays minimal; the analytical content of mimicry, bluff, deception, discovery-events, and consequence-scaling lives in the pattern entries for those patterns and in their characteristic dynamics. The framework's existing temporal vocabulary — sustained-covert flow (§5.4), delays (§5.2) — provides what is needed at framework level to support these pattern analyses without requiring framework-level substrate-type or perception-status fields.

The discipline generalises: when a stress-test surfaces what appears to be a framework-level refinement, check whether the analytical content is actually pattern-specific. If it is, the right move is to identify the patterns that should be in the atlas (when they have their own entries) and use the framework's existing primitives to analyse them, not to extend framework-level vocabulary to encode pattern-specific content.

Bindings are the connective tissue of the dynamics framework. Power binds to reciprocity, legitimacy, violence, or other patterns; the quality of power's operation in any specific case is set by what it is bound to. Coercion uses bindings to cost-sources (power, guilt, shame, obligation) to supply the contingent cost it leverages. Shame binds to the value patterns being enforced; the quality of shame's operation is set by what those bound patterns are. Across the migrated cluster, binding is the relation that does the most analytical work, and the statics framework could only flag it provisionally.

The framework also distinguishes binding from substrate-dependence (a statics relation). Substrate-dependence says a pattern depends on lower-stratum substrate for its realisation; binding says a pattern depends on another pattern's flow for its operation. The two are different. A social pattern depends on biological substrate (statics, substrate-dependence) and may depend on the flow of another social pattern (dynamics, binding). Both relations can hold simultaneously and are not reducible to each other.

### 2.3 Accumulation

Accumulation is flow compounding into persistent stock-formation. When flow into a pattern persists as durable capacity over time rather than being immediately consumed or dissipated, the pattern accumulates. Accumulation is the conceptual ancestor of Circle/Ripple Theory's **priming** (accumulated past influence that shapes present perception) and **reserves** (stored value buffers that absorb stress and delay collapse), and is formally captured by the stock/flow distinction adopted from systems dynamics.

Accumulation has both substrate-stratum and operation-stratum aspects. At the biological stratum, accumulation might be neural plasticity (Hebbian-style co-activation patterns becoming durable), endocrine baseline shifts, or behavioural habituation. At the social stratum, accumulation might be reputation (cumulative reactions to a node's past actions becoming a standing pattern), trust reserves (built through repeated reciprocal exchanges), or institutional standing (cumulative position in a relational structure). At the intellectual stratum, accumulation might be skill, understanding, or theoretical refinement (cumulative analytical work becoming durable capacity).

Accumulation is what makes some patterns durable across time despite the continuous flow of value through them. A node with deep reserves can absorb stress that would collapse a node without them. A node with deep priming experiences present situations through the lens of accumulated past flow rather than as if encountering them fresh. The dynamics framework's treatment of persistence (already in the statics framework as persistence_character) becomes more articulable: persistence is partly about a pattern's structural composition (statics) and partly about its accumulated stocks of value-flow over time (dynamics).

Accumulation is not always positive. **Negative accumulation** — the durable buildup of damage, trauma, debt, or compromised standing — is structurally the same primitive operating with opposite valence. A pattern can accumulate debt as durably as it accumulates capacity; both are stock-formations resulting from flow over time.

### 2.4 Dissipation

Dissipation is the configuration in which flow through a binding or field produces less downstream value-flow than the binding's input would have enabled under high-quality conditions. The framework borrows the concept from thermodynamics as an analytical term for reduced downstream enabling-capacity, without taking on the conservation assumption that frames it in physics.

In a binding with high dissipation, flow proceeds but the downstream pattern receives less effective contribution than the binding's input made available. The difference shows up as redirection toward less productive ends — flow absorbed by substrate damage (a degraded binding may strain the bound patterns), by counter-pattern formation (sustained dissipative binding generates resistance), by friction (coordination overhead consumes contribution), or by diversion (flow is directed away from the binding's structural purpose). The framework does not commit to a conservation principle that would require dissipated flow to be tracked to a specific destination, and it also does not commit to the opposite claim that dissipated flow is metaphysically destroyed. What the framework articulates analytically is the difference in downstream enabling capacity between high-quality and low-quality bindings.

Dissipation tracks binding quality. High-quality bindings have low dissipation — flow through them is carried with minimal loss to the binding's purpose, and the bound pattern often draws additional flow from the field beyond what the binding itself supplied (this is what makes amplification possible, see §4.1). Low-quality bindings have high dissipation — flow through them produces less downstream enabling than the binding's input made available, sometimes substantially less. This is part of what makes binding quality the central feature it is: quality differences are observable through dissipation patterns.

Dissipation also occurs in fields rather than only in bindings. A field with poor coherence dissipates flow carried within it; a field with strong coherence enables flow carried within it with low dissipation. This is part of what makes field-quality matter: the same propagation through different fields enables different real effects because the fields dissipate flow differently.

### 2.5 Modulation-of-flow

Modulation-of-flow is the operation by which field structures shape value-flow as it propagates. Circle/Ripple Theory's **zone of value resonance** is the conceptual ancestor: a region within a circle where certain ripples are amplified, normalised, dampened, or suppressed. The dynamics framework adopts modulation-of-flow as a primitive describing how the field itself participates in shaping flow, distinct from binding (which is between patterns) and propagation (which is the basic phenomenon).

Modulation-of-flow has four characteristic operations the framework names:
- **Amplification** — the field intensifies flow as it propagates through a zone.
- **Damping** — the field attenuates flow as it propagates through a zone.
- **Normalisation** — the field shapes flow to conform to a characteristic pattern of the field.
- **Suppression** — the field prevents flow from propagating.

These are not separate primitives; they are characteristic modes of the modulation-of-flow primitive operating in different zones.

Modulation-of-flow is distinct from the **modulation** primitive in the statics framework (where modulation describes how one pattern shapes another's realisation). The statics primitive concerns pattern-to-pattern relations; the dynamics primitive concerns field-to-flow relations. The two are related — both are about how operation is shaped — but they operate on different things. A binding has both a statics aspect (how it modulates the bound pattern's realisation) and a dynamics aspect (how it modulates the flow into and out of the bound pattern). Modulation-of-flow as a dynamics primitive specifically concerns the field's contribution, which the statics framework cannot articulate because it has no field primitive.

### 2.6 Cascade

Cascade is value-flow propagating across strata. The framework v2.1 introduced the direct-vs-cascaded distinction for modulation: a higher pattern can modulate the immediately-lower stratum directly, and can reach further strata only through cascade — by modulating an intermediate stratum whose altered realisation then modulates the next stratum below. The dynamics framework adopts cascade as a primitive applying not only to modulation but to value-flow generally.

Cascade has structural features the framework can specify:
- **Always mediated** — cascade does not skip strata. A social pattern's effect on physical reality runs through biological cascade; the social effect modifies biological substrate, which modifies physical conditions, which produces the physical effect. There is no direct social-to-physical flow.
- **Stratum-character-changing** — what cascades across strata is not the same in character as what propagated at the source stratum. A social cost-imposition cascading to biology becomes biological stress, not social cost in biological form. The cascade transforms the flow as it crosses strata, because each stratum has its own character of value-flow.
- **Often delayed** — cascade typically operates with delay, because each strata-crossing involves the realisation of the next stratum's response to the previous stratum's altered condition. Shame's biological effects through social cost-imposition appear over hours to days; betrayal's social effects through accumulated priming may appear over months to years.

Cascade is the framework's positive account of what is sometimes called "downward causation." It says: yes, higher-stratum patterns produce real effects on lower strata, but only through cascade, and the cascade is mediated, transformative, and typically delayed. The strict discipline against direct higher-to-lower origination (statics framework §2.4 and §4.5) is preserved; the productive cross-stratum interaction is captured by cascade.

### 2.7 The relationship among primitives

The six primitives are not independent. They compose into characteristic configurations and constrain each other in specific ways:

- Propagation is the basic phenomenon; the other five primitives describe specific aspects of how it occurs.
- Binding directs propagation (between patterns) and is where accumulation, dissipation, and amplification often occur most visibly.
- Accumulation and dissipation are partial opposites — accumulation builds stock from flow; dissipation loses value during flow. Both can occur simultaneously in different aspects of the same propagation (a pattern can accumulate priming while dissipating immediate flow).
- Modulation-of-flow is a field-side operation; binding is a pattern-side operation. The two together describe how flow is shaped in propagation: by the field through which it travels and by the bindings through which it is directed.
- Cascade is propagation across strata; it inherits binding, accumulation, dissipation, and modulation-of-flow as they operate at each crossed stratum.

The primitives are six because each is needed; none can be eliminated by reduction to others. They are not exhaustive of every phenomenon the framework can describe — many configurations require multiple primitives operating together — but they are the basic operations from which configurations compose.

## 3. The Field as Medium

Circle/Ripple Theory's **value field** concept becomes a foundational element of the dynamics framework. The field is the medium through which propagation occurs and the structure within which modulation-of-flow operates. Without a field, propagation would have no medium; without medium, the question of how propagation works could not be answered.

### 3.1 What the field is

A value field is a structured region of value-bearing relations within which value-flow occurs. It has the following characteristics:

- **Coherence** — the degree to which the field's value patterns hold together and reinforce each other. High-coherence fields transmit value with low dissipation; low-coherence fields dissipate value as it propagates.
- **Composition** — what value patterns operate in the field. A field is partly defined by the active value patterns within it.
- **Boundaries** — fields have boundaries, though these are typically permeable. Patterns within a field operate differently than patterns outside; flow within a field has different character than flow across field boundaries.
- **Multi-stratum anchoring** — fields are anchored across strata. Circle/Ripple Theory's circle concept names this: a circle is a "socially bound, multi-level anchored value field" — bound at the social stratum (through shared meaning, reciprocity, enforcement) but anchored across all strata (people as biological bearers, resources as physical substrate, ideas as intellectual content).

Fields are not patterns in the statics sense, though they contain patterns. A field is the relational structure within which patterns operate and through which value-flow propagates. Patterns are constituted; fields are structured. The distinction is similar to the distinction in physics between particles (localised structures) and fields (extended structures within which particles operate), though the analogy must not be pressed too hard.

### 3.2 Zones of value resonance

Within a field, certain regions modulate flow distinctively. Circle/Ripple Theory's **zone of value resonance** names this: a region within a circle where certain ripples are amplified, normalised, dampened, or suppressed. The dynamics framework adopts the zone concept as the specific site where modulation-of-flow occurs.

Zones have characteristic modulation modes (per Section 2.5): amplification, damping, normalisation, suppression. Different zones in the same field modulate different flows differently. A workplace field might amplify status-signaling flows, normalise reciprocity flows, dampen emotional flows, and suppress dissent flows — all within the same field, in different zones. The field's overall character is the composite of its zones' modulations.

Zones are not patterns either. They are regions of the field with characteristic flow-modulation behaviour. They emerge from the field's composition and structure, not from any single pattern.

### 3.3 Circles and keychains

Circle/Ripple Theory provides two specific kinds of value fields with structural importance the dynamics framework adopts:

- **Circle** — a socially bound, multi-level anchored value field. Made real by people (as biological bearers), resources (physical substrate), shared meaning (social), reciprocity (relational), and enforcement (regulatory). A circle is the basic unit of organised value-flow at the social stratum and above.

- **Keychain** — the higher-order binding of circles into larger social structures that carry accumulated value across multiple circles. A nation is a keychain of community circles; a profession is a keychain of practice circles; a tradition is a keychain of generation-by-generation circles. Keychains carry accumulated human value across longer time-scales than any single circle, and the binding between circles in a keychain is a specific kind of inter-field structure.

Circles and keychains are field-side concepts. They organise where value-flow occurs and how it propagates between organised units. The dynamics framework uses circles and keychains as the field-level structures within which pattern-level value-flow analysis operates.

### 3.4 Field-relative valuation

The Non-Relative Value Hierarchy principle constrains how value-flow is analysed across fields. The same flow can produce different real effects in different fields, but those effects are objective within each field, arising from real consequences at the relevant strata. The dynamics framework adopts field-relative valuation explicitly:

- Value-flow is analysed from within fields, not from a view-from-nowhere outside all fields.
- Different fields can experience the same flow differently; these differences are real, not artefacts of perspective.
- The hierarchy of value quality (per Pirsig and Circle/Ripple Theory) applies across fields: some bindings, modulations, and accumulation patterns are objectively higher quality because they preserve more strata and produce less dissipation, regardless of which field one analyses from.

This commitment is foundational to all of the framework's analysis. A pattern's value-flow profile must be analysable from multiple field positions to be properly characterised; analysing only from one field position misses the full structure.

## 4. Configurations

The six primitives compose into characteristic configurations — recurring ways primitives combine to produce specific dynamics patterns. Configurations are not new primitives; they are named compositions that occur often enough to warrant analytical recognition. The dynamics framework recognises several families of configurations: **amplification and damping**, **feedback loops**, **cycles**, and **value-flow configurations**.

### 4.1 Amplification and damping

**Amplification** is the configuration in which a high-quality binding enables the bound pattern to recruit more flow than it could recruit unbound. The binding provides coordinative structure that draws additional flow from the field — flow that the bound pattern alone could not effectively recruit, but that the field already contains and the binding makes accessible. Power's relation to its bound patterns is the clearest case: power amplifies what it is bound to, with the bound pattern drawing on resources, contributions, and coordinative capacity across the field that the bound pattern alone could not organise. The bound pattern is doing the work; power's binding provides the structural capacity through which the bound pattern can recruit the field's contribution into coordinated flow. The 1000 loyal coordinated agents are not producing value from nothing; their loyalty enables their field to recruit and coordinate their individual contributions into coherent collective flow that an uncoordinated field cannot match.

Structurally, amplification combines binding (the coupling) with accumulation (the bound pattern's flow builds beyond what the binding itself supplied) under conditions where the binding is high-quality and the field has flow available for recruitment. Amplification is therefore not a metaphysical generation of flow but a structural enabling of recruitment.

**Damping** is the inverse configuration: a binding through which the bound pattern recruits less flow than it would have recruited unbound, or through which flow is reshaped in ways that reduce its downstream effectiveness. Damping combines binding with dissipation; the binding exists, but flow through it is degraded in transit or recruited less effectively than would otherwise be possible. Some bindings are intentionally damping (institutional structures that attenuate enthusiastic but inexperienced contributions to maintain coordinative coherence); others are damping through poor quality (low-trust relations that suppress contribution through suspicion).

Both amplification and damping are configurations, not primitives. They name composite operations that occur often enough to merit their own analytical vocabulary, but they reduce to combinations of binding, accumulation, dissipation, and modulation-of-flow operating together.

### 4.2 Feedback loops

A **feedback loop** is a configuration in which value-flow returns to its source, either reinforcing or balancing the original flow. The systems dynamics tradition provides the analytical vocabulary; the dynamics framework adopts it with explicit attribution.

**Reinforcing feedback** is the configuration where flow returns to its source in a way that amplifies the source. The reciprocity-and-trust cycle is reinforcing: trust enables exchange, exchange produces shared value, shared value strengthens trust, strengthened trust enables more exchange. The cycle compounds positively (in high-quality cases) or negatively (in low-quality cases — the spiral of suspicion that destroys trust). Power's accumulation through its bindings is reinforcing: the more power has bound and amplified, the more it accumulates, the more it can bind and amplify.

**Balancing feedback** is the configuration where flow returns to its source in a way that attenuates the source. Reciprocity's restoration of equity is balancing: when one party gives more than they receive, the imbalance creates pressure (felt or structural) that produces return-flow to balance. Shame's reintegration is balancing: when shame has been imposed and the deficiency has been addressed, the field's continued cost-imposition is attenuated, allowing the bearer's standing to be restored.

Real value-flow systems typically contain multiple feedback loops operating simultaneously — reinforcing and balancing loops interacting, sometimes in tension, sometimes coordinated. The framework's analysis of any pattern's dynamics requires identifying the feedback loops that operate in its field and how they interact.

### 4.3 Cycles: collapse, repair, re-entry

Circle/Ripple Theory provides a characteristic cycle that the dynamics framework adopts: **collapse → repair → re-entry**. This is the temporal configuration by which value fields fail, are restored, and admit renewed participation.

**Collapse** is the configuration where reserves are depleted faster than they are replenished, eventually exceeding the field's capacity to absorb stress, at which point the field's coherence fails. Circle/Ripple Theory specifies collapse as the failure of a value field due to reserve depletion and broken reciprocity. The dynamics framework formalises this: collapse occurs when dissipation exceeds replenishment in critical bindings, when accumulated debt exceeds accumulated capacity, when modulation-of-flow no longer normalises flows into the field's characteristic patterns.

**Repair** is the configuration where, after collapse, the field's coherence is re-patterned. Repair is costly — it requires significant flow input from outside the collapsed region — and it cannot reverse history. Repair re-patterns under new constraints, building new bindings, restoring reserves, re-establishing modulation. Repair is not return-to-prior-state but rather creation-of-new-functional-state.

**Re-entry** is the configuration where bearers who lost participation in the field through collapse (or were excluded during collapse) are admitted to renewed participation. Re-entry is structurally a forgiveness-and-restoration sequence: the field accepts the bearer's renewed participation despite justified expectation of harm, often conditional on demonstrated capacity for the field's characteristic flow-patterns. Re-entry is never automatic and never cost-free; both the field and the re-entering bearer take on flow-commitments to make re-entry stable.

The collapse-repair-re-entry cycle is not single-instance but recurring. Fields go through cycles of stability, accumulating stress, collapse, repair, re-entry, and renewed stability. The cycle's timescales vary enormously — interpersonal relationships cycle over months to years; institutional fields cycle over years to decades; cultural fields cycle over generations.

### 4.4 Value-flow configurations

Several named configurations describe characteristic patterns of value-flow that recur across domains. The framework adopts these from Circle/Ripple Theory and critical political economy, with explicit attribution:

- **Diversion** — flow that should reach one pattern is redirected to another. Diversion is a configuration of binding (the redirecting coupling) combined with modulation-of-flow (the field structure that enables the redirection). The diverted flow reaches a destination other than its proper structural recipient.

- **Capture** — flow that should propagate onward is retained at a binding rather than passing through. Capture combines binding (the trapping coupling) with accumulation (the captured flow builds in the capturer rather than flowing onward). Institutional capture is the paradigmatic case: an institution that should serve a broader function instead retains the value-flow within itself.

- **Extraction** — flow is removed from a source system for delivery elsewhere, typically with significant dissipation in transit and minimal flow returned to the source. Extraction combines binding (the extracting coupling), propagation (movement away from the source), and significant dissipation. Resource extraction from communities and labour extraction from workers are paradigmatic cases.

- **Parasitism** — a binding that takes flow from a source without supporting the source's continued operation. Parasitic bindings are unidirectional in the destructive sense: flow goes from source to parasite without return-flow that sustains the source. The source is depleted; the parasite benefits.

- **Betrayal** — a sudden reversal of a binding's quality, typically from high-quality to low-quality. Betrayal is structurally a reversal-event in the binding's character: a binding that had been amplifying becomes diverting, capturing, extracting, or parasitic. The betrayed party experiences a sudden loss of expected flow, often with severe substrate consequences (loss of biological support, social standing, intellectual frameworks).

These configurations are analytically recognisable across domains. They are not primitives — each reduces to combinations of binding, accumulation, dissipation, propagation, and modulation-of-flow — but they recur often enough that having named configurations supports analysis.

#### Maintained-premise as a temporal-character (v1.2)

Several of the value-flow configurations exhibit a recurrent temporal-character: the configuration's operation depends on the source pattern's continued participation in some recognisable form. The configuration sustains itself as long as the source maintains its participation; it fails or collapses when the participation is withdrawn or its premise is revealed.

This temporal-character — **maintained-premise** — appears across multiple configurations:

- **Diversion-on-maintained-premise** (coercion's case): the subjected party continues participating in the field while value is extracted from its participation through cost-avoidance substitution. The premise being maintained is the subjected party's continued engagement under the contingent cost.
- **Hidden-reversal-on-maintained-premise** (betrayal's case): the betrayed party continues participating in the relationship under what it takes to be the original binding character. The premise being maintained is the appearance of the original binding character; the actual binding has reversed but this is hidden from the betrayed party.
- **Extraction-on-maintained-premise** (parasitism's case): the host continues providing substrate while the parasite extracts flow without supporting the host's continued operation. The premise being maintained is the host's function as substrate.

Maintained-premise is not itself a configuration; it is a temporal-character that certain configurations can have. The character is structurally important because it determines a configuration's durability profile: configurations with maintained-premise are sustainable as long as the premise holds, and typically have characteristic discovery-events or threshold-crossings at which the premise fails. The framework's sustained-covert flow temporal vocabulary (§5.4) supports the analysis of maintained-premise configurations whose premise involves hidden information.

The maintained-premise character connects to the framework-pattern distinction discipline (§2.2): the specific dynamics of maintaining premise, of discovery, of consequence-on-discovery, often involve patterns like deception, bluff, manipulation, or manufactured-appearance that are themselves value patterns with their own entries. The framework provides the temporal-character vocabulary; the patterns provide the specific analytical content for any particular case.

### 4.5 Binding-portfolios (v1.2)

A binding-portfolio is the set of bindings a pattern has at a given time. Where the binding primitive (§2.2) handles individual bindings, the binding-portfolio configuration articulates how multiple simultaneous bindings interact to determine a pattern's aggregate operational character. A pattern's character at any moment is determined not by any single binding but by the aggregate of all its bindings — qualities, strengths, durabilities, and the interactions among them.

The need for this configuration surfaced from the power stress-test (power's simultaneous bindings to reciprocity, legitimacy, authority, possibly violence, possibly coercion, possibly self-perpetuation) and was confirmed by the coercion stress-test (coercion's cost-source-portfolio of bindings to power, guilt, shame, obligation).

#### Specific kinds of binding-portfolios

Just as binding has structurally distinct kinds (binding_kind: amplification-recruitment, cost-source), binding-portfolios can be characterised by the dominant binding_kind within them:

- **Amplification-portfolio** — a binding-portfolio dominated by amplification-recruitment bindings, where the pattern's operational character is determined by what range of patterns it is amplifying. Power's portfolio is characteristically of this kind: power's character is set by the patterns it is bound to amplify (reciprocity-supportive, predation-supportive, self-perpetuating, etc.).
- **Cost-source-portfolio** — a binding-portfolio dominated by cost-source bindings, where the pattern's operational character is determined by what range of cost-sources it can leverage. Coercion's portfolio is characteristically of this kind: coercion's character is set by the cost-sources available (power-leveraging, guilt-leveraging, shame-leveraging, obligation-leveraging).
- **Mixed portfolios** — patterns whose portfolio includes both amplification-recruitment and cost-source bindings. These are likely more complex to analyse but probably common; many patterns will both amplify what they bind to and leverage what they bind to in different relations.

#### Aggregate character

A portfolio's aggregate character is not the sum of its individual bindings but the result of their interaction. Several characteristic interaction modes:

- **Constructive aggregation** — bindings reinforce each other, each strengthening the others. Power's portfolio of reciprocity, legitimacy, and authority is characteristically constructive: each binding strengthens the others (reciprocity supports legitimacy; legitimacy supports authority; authority supports reciprocity).
- **Destructive aggregation** — bindings undermine each other or compete for the pattern's substrate. Power's portfolio with tight binding to both reciprocity and coercion is characteristically destructive: the bindings pull in opposed directions, depleting the pattern's reserves.
- **Substitutive aggregation** — bindings can substitute for each other; loss of one is compensated by drawing more on another. Coercion's cost-source-portfolio is often substitutive: when power is unavailable as cost-source, coercion can substitute guilt, shame, or obligation. This gives the portfolio resilience (multiple cost-sources mean coercion can continue when any single one is lost) but also breadth-of-impact (each substitution distorts another pattern).
- **Independent aggregation** — bindings operate without significant interaction. Less common in practice; most simultaneous bindings have at least some interaction.

#### Portfolio dynamics

Portfolios change over time. New bindings form, existing bindings degrade, the quality or strength of bindings shifts. The portfolio's dynamics — how it builds up over time, how it can collapse, how it transitions between configurations — are themselves analytical content. Specific patterns will exhibit characteristic portfolio dynamics: power's portfolio typically builds slowly through reciprocity-binding and legitimacy-binding, can shift toward coercion-binding under stress, and (if other bindings collapse) can degenerate to self-perpetuation binding alone.

The portfolio configuration is a framework v1.2 addition. Specific analyses use it; the framework provides the configuration vocabulary.

### 4.6 The relationship between configurations and primitives

Configurations are static patterns about dynamic processes. They are intellectual-level patterns that describe how primitives combine in characteristic ways. The framework's discipline applies: configurations are not the dynamics they describe but accounts of dynamics. Naming a configuration (e.g. "this is a parasitic binding") is analytical work, not capture of the dynamics itself.

This is the same discipline applied throughout: the framework's intellectual content is static patterns about flow, not flow itself. The discipline against metaphysical inflation (per §1.5) applies to configurations as it applies to primitives: configurations are useful analytical concepts, not captures of Dynamic Quality or even of value-flow itself.

## 5. Temporal Dimensions

Value-flow occurs in time. The framework recognises several temporal dimensions of value-flow that bear on its operation, and adopts the systems dynamics treatment of delays as an explicit temporal primitive.

### 5.1 Timescales of value-flow

Value-flow operates on multiple timescales simultaneously, and the same pattern can involve flows on very different timescales:

- **Immediate flow** — propagation that occurs within seconds to hours. Most direct interactions involve immediate flow.
- **Episodic flow** — propagation that occurs over days to months. Many social patterns involve episodic flow (relationship cycles, institutional operations).
- **Sustained flow** — propagation that occurs over years to decades. Priming accumulates over sustained flow; long-term relationships build through it; institutional patterns are sustained at this timescale.
- **Generational flow** — propagation across human generations. Cultural patterns operate at generational flow; some keychains carry value across multiple generations.

The framework's analysis of any pattern's dynamics must attend to which timescales matter for that pattern. Power often involves multiple simultaneous timescales — immediate flow in specific exercises, episodic flow in policy cycles, sustained flow in accumulation, generational flow in institutional persistence. Shame typically involves episodic flow (the shaming sequence and its reintegration) but can accumulate to sustained or generational flow when reintegration is permanently blocked (identity-marking castes, intergenerational stigma).

### 5.2 Delays

The framework adopts **delay** from systems dynamics as an explicit temporal primitive: a time lag between cause and effect in value-flow. Delays are structurally important because they break the assumption of immediate causal feedback — patterns can produce effects long after they operated, and effects can be produced by causes that are no longer present.

Several characteristic delays operate in the migrated cluster:
- **Cost-credibility delay** — coercion's contingent costs may not be experienced immediately even when imposed; the bearer's recognition of cost-credibility may lag the actual imposition.
- **Counter-pattern formation delay** — coercion's structural cost in producing counter-pattern formation operates with delay; the counter-pattern accumulates over time and reaches operational scale only after sustained coercive operation.
- **Reintegration delay** — shame's reintegration is not immediate even when reintegration pathways exist; the field's restoration of standing operates over the timescale of the field's evaluation cycles.
- **Cascade delay** — cross-stratum cascade typically operates with delay because each strata-crossing involves realisation of the next stratum's response.

The framework's analysis must attend to delays because timing is structural. A coercion-bound field that has not yet generated counter-pattern formation looks stable at one timescale and unstable at another; the difference is the delay.

### 5.3 Standing versus eruptive flow

Some value-flow is standing — continuously operative as long as the pattern is active. Reciprocity flows continuously between parties in an ongoing relationship; power's accumulation builds continuously as the pattern operates; priming accumulates continuously through sustained activity.

Other value-flow is eruptive — operating in episodic bursts rather than continuously. Betrayal is eruptive in its primary event (though its consequences cascade and propagate continuously afterwards); a specific exercise of power is eruptive even when the standing pattern of power is continuous; an individual shaming sequence is eruptive even when the general operation of shaming in a field is continuous.

A pattern can have both standing and eruptive flow operating simultaneously. Power has standing flow (accumulation, ongoing modulation of bound patterns) and eruptive flow (specific exercises of power that draw on accumulated reserves). The framework's analysis attends to both.

### 5.4 Sustained-covert flow

A specific temporal configuration deserves naming because the migration work has surfaced it repeatedly: **sustained-covert flow** is value-flow that operates continuously but is hidden from the affected parties. Sustained-covert betrayal is the paradigmatic case: a binding that has reversed quality but whose reversal is hidden from the betrayed party, allowing the destructive flow to operate continuously without triggering the field's defensive responses.

Sustained-covert flow is structurally distinct from standing flow (which is operative and visible) and from eruptive flow (which is brief but visible). It is operative and hidden, which produces characteristic consequences: the affected party cannot mobilise defensive responses while the flow operates; consequences accumulate that would not have accumulated under standing-and-visible flow; the eventual revelation of the sustained-covert operation typically produces compound effects beyond what the flow itself would have produced if visible from the start.

The framework names sustained-covert flow because it is structurally important for analysis of betrayal, deception, and certain forms of institutional capture, but does not give it a separate primitive — it is a temporal configuration of binding with hidden quality reversal.

## 6. Cross-Stratum Dynamics

Value-flow at one stratum has substrate dependence on lower strata, can modulate lower-stratum patterns, and produces cascaded effects across strata. The dynamics framework's cross-stratum treatment builds on the statics framework's modulation discipline and adds the dynamics-specific analytical content.

### 6.1 The strata as flow contexts

Each stratum has its own characteristic value-flow, distinct from but coupled to the strata below:

- **Physical stratum flow** — value-flow at the physical stratum has the character of physical persistence and physical-resource circulation. Energy flows, matter flows, resource extraction and replenishment cycles. The physical stratum's flows are most amenable to thermodynamic-style analysis (per the survey, with conservation discipline).

- **Biological stratum flow** — value-flow at the biological stratum has the character of metabolic and informational coupling. Predictive-processing updates, reward-prediction-error learning, immune signalling, endocrine response. The biological stratum's flows are most amenable to neuroscience-derived analysis at the bearer level and ecological-flow-derived analysis at the field level (per the survey, with stratum discipline).

- **Social stratum flow** — value-flow at the social stratum has the character of coordination, signalling, reciprocity, regulation, recognition. Distributed evaluation, reputation propagation, role-bearing dynamics. The social stratum's flows are most amenable to game-theoretic, network-flow, and circle/ripple analyses.

- **Intellectual stratum flow** — value-flow at the intellectual stratum has the character of correction, testing, inference, transmission. Hypothesis-testing, error-correction, conceptual development. The intellectual stratum's flows are most amenable to Buddhist-style dependent-origination analysis and to the framework's own analytical work, which is itself intellectual-stratum value-flow about the other strata.

The framework treats each stratum's flow as having its own character. Cascade across strata transforms flow from one character to another, never carrying flow unchanged across the boundary.

### 6.2 Cross-stratum bindings

Bindings can be intra-stratum (between patterns at the same stratum) or cross-stratum (between patterns at different strata). Cross-stratum bindings are structurally constrained:

- A higher-stratum pattern can have substrate-dependence on a lower-stratum pattern; this is a statics relation but it produces dynamics: the lower-stratum pattern's flow enables the higher-stratum pattern's operation.
- A higher-stratum pattern can modulate a lower-stratum pattern through binding; this is the dynamics-side of the statics-modulation relation discussed in framework v2.1. The binding's quality (supportive, distorting, conditional, corrective per the framework v2.1 modulation kinds) shapes what kind of value-flow the binding carries.
- A lower-stratum pattern cannot originate a higher-stratum pattern; this prohibition from statics carries directly into dynamics. Cross-stratum binding does not create patterns at the bound-to stratum; it modulates them.

The framework's cross-stratum bindings preserve all the strata discipline of the statics framework while adding the dynamics-side analytical content (how flow propagates through the binding, with what character, on what timescale, with what dissipation).

### 6.3 Cascade discipline

Cascade across strata is real, mediated, transformative, and typically delayed (per §2.6). The framework's cascade discipline specifies what is permitted and what is prohibited:

**Permitted under cascade:**
- Higher-stratum patterns produce real effects on lower strata through mediated cascade.
- The character of the effect transforms as it crosses strata (a social cost-imposition becomes biological stress, not "social cost in biological form").
- Cascade can produce substantial effects at distant strata through accumulated propagation.

**Prohibited under cascade:**
- Direct higher-to-lower origination (a higher-stratum pattern producing a lower-stratum pattern from nothing).
- Stratum-skipping cascade (a social effect on physical reality that bypasses biological cascade).
- Reduction (explaining a higher-stratum pattern as merely the accumulated effects of lower-stratum cascade).

The discipline is the same as in the statics framework, applied to flow: cross-stratum interaction is real and substantial, but it is interaction, not reduction or origination.

### 6.4 The Buddhist contribution: dependent origination across strata

The Buddhist tradition's dependent origination (per the survey, §5) provides analytical depth for cross-stratum dynamics. The Twelve Nidānas describe a cascading conditioning sequence: ignorance conditions formations, formations condition consciousness, consciousness conditions name-and-form, and so on. Each link is not a direct cause of the next but a conditioning factor that, together with other factors, produces the next.

The framework adopts this analytical structure (not the specific Twelve Nidānas content, which carries cultural and soteriological specificity) for cross-stratum cascade. A cross-stratum cascade is not a direct causal sequence but a conditioning sequence: a higher-stratum pattern conditions the operation of the next-lower stratum, which conditions the operation of the next-lower stratum, each link operating through the substrate that lower stratum provides. This framing prevents the misreading of cascade as direct causation across strata.

## 7. Relationship to the Statics Framework

The dynamics framework and the statics framework operate on the same patterns from different analytical angles. The statics framework describes what patterns are; the dynamics framework describes how they interact through value-flow. The two are complementary, not competing, and the framework's discipline is to maintain the division of labour cleanly.

### 7.1 What dynamics adds

The dynamics framework adds the following analytical capacities that the statics framework could not provide:

- **Binding as a relation type** — captures the value-flow coupling between patterns. The migration work surfaced binding as the most consequential gap in the statics relation vocabulary, particularly in the power migration where power's defining operation could not be cleanly typed. The dynamics framework provides binding as the foundational new relation type.

- **Flow character of substrate-dependence and modulation** — the statics framework names substrate-dependence and modulation as relation types but does not articulate the value-flow that those relations carry. The dynamics framework adds: substrate-dependence has flow-character (the lower stratum's flow enables the higher stratum's operation), and modulation has flow-character (the modulating pattern shapes flow into and out of the modulated pattern).

- **Field as analytical context** — the statics framework operates at the level of patterns and their relations but does not have a field-level analytical primitive. The dynamics framework adds field as the analytical context for pattern interaction, with zones of resonance as specific sites where field-level modulation-of-flow occurs.

- **Temporal analysis** — the statics framework's persistence_character begins temporal analysis but does not articulate the multiple timescales on which patterns operate, the role of delays, or the distinction between standing and eruptive flow. The dynamics framework adds the temporal dimensions in Section 5.

- **Configurations** — the statics framework can describe patterns but not the characteristic ways they combine in operation. The dynamics framework adds amplification, damping, feedback loops, cycles, and value-flow configurations (diversion, capture, extraction, parasitism, betrayal).

### 7.2 What statics retains

The dynamics framework does not displace or replace any of the statics framework's analytical work. The following remain statics-side:

- **Pattern composition** — constituents, internal compositional character, the statics-side of composition relations.
- **Bearer mode** — agent_only, group_only, multi_bearer_minimum_two, etc.
- **Profile-coherence** — substrate fit, modulation responsibility, internal compositional coherence, field compatibility, bearer adequacy, dispositional consistency.
- **Persistence character** — at the statics-side, the type-character of how a pattern persists (conditional, bounded_by_counter_pattern_formation, etc.).
- **Constituents and substrate** — what a pattern is made of and what it depends on at lower strata.
- **Distinction-relations** — provisional opposition/incompatibility relations between dissociable patterns (these are about constitutive distinctions, not flow).

The clean separation: statics describes constitution; dynamics describes interaction-through-flow. Statics is about what patterns are; dynamics is about how they move value among each other.

### 7.3 Pattern entries and the dynamics layer

Pattern entries (in the four-artifact schema) acquire dynamics-side content with the dynamics framework. The structured frontmatter expands to include dynamics-specific fields, and the narrative articulation acquires dynamics-specific sections. The specific changes are recommendations for the schema update (Task 3, after this framework's stress-testing) but the analytical shape is clear:

- Pattern entries should record their characteristic binding-relations (what patterns they bind to, of what quality, with what dynamics).
- Pattern entries should record their accumulation character (what they accumulate, how durably, with what stock-formation pattern).
- Pattern entries should record their dissipation character (how value is lost in their bindings, under what conditions).
- Pattern entries should record their temporal profile (immediate, episodic, sustained, generational flow; characteristic delays).
- Pattern entries should record their cycle participation (what cycles they participate in, with what role).

The dynamics layer of pattern entries integrates with the existing statics-side content rather than replacing it. The same pattern entry has both faces: what the pattern is (statics) and how it moves value (dynamics).

### 7.4 The binding-relation type

The dynamics framework formally commits the **binding** relation type that was provisional in the schema v0.4. Binding is now a committed relation type with specific structural features:

- **source**: the pattern that the flow originates from (or in bidirectional bindings, one of the patterns)
- **target**: the pattern that the flow feeds (or in bidirectional bindings, the other pattern)
- **directionality**: directional, bidirectional, or coordinated
- **quality**: high (amplifying), low (dissipating), or conditional (set by what is bound)
- **strength**: tight, moderate, or loose
- **durability**: standing or contingent
- **conditions**: under what circumstances the binding operates

The binding relation type does not displace substrate-dependence or modulation; it complements them. Many patterns will have both substrate-dependence relations (statics) and binding relations (dynamics) to overlapping sets of other patterns, because substrate-dependence describes the structural enablement of operation while binding describes the value-flow coupling that operates through that enablement.

### 7.5 The cost-source relation as binding

The provisional cost-source relation type surfaced in the coercion migration (REL-SUB-COE-PWR/GUI/SHA/OBL-001) now finds its proper formal home as a specific kind of binding: a **cost-source binding**. Coercion's bindings to power, guilt, shame, and obligation are bindings in which the bound pattern supplies the contingent cost coercion leverages. This is a structurally specific binding type:

- The bound pattern (power, guilt, shame, obligation) is the cost-source.
- The binding directs flow from the cost-source pattern to coercion's operation.
- The binding is conditional — coercion can operate with any of several cost-sources, not all of them required simultaneously.

With binding committed as a relation type, the cost-source relations can be reclassified from "substrate_dependence with character: conditional" to "binding with binding_kind: cost-source." This is a schema-level update that depends on the framework's adoption.

## 9. Implications, Limits, and What Comes Next

### 9.1 What the framework enables

The dynamics framework enables several analytical capacities the statics framework could not provide:

- **Articulate the binding-relation gap.** Power's amplification of bound patterns, coercion's cost-source relations, shame's binding to the value patterns it enforces — all are now formally analysable with the binding relation type. The provisional flags throughout the migrated cluster can be resolved.

- **Specify the temporal profile of patterns.** Patterns can now be analysed for the timescales they operate on, the delays they involve, whether they have standing or eruptive flow, and whether they involve sustained-covert flow. Power's multi-timescale operation, coercion's counter-pattern delay, shame's reintegration cycles, and betrayal's sustained-covert character all become analytically tractable.

- **Articulate flow configurations.** Diversion, capture, extraction, parasitism, and betrayal can be analysed structurally rather than only descriptively. Their structural similarities and differences become visible, supporting comparison across domains.

- **Analyse fields, not only patterns.** Circles, keychains, zones of resonance, and field coherence become analytical primitives. Pattern analysis can be supplemented by field analysis, which addresses phenomena (humiliation culture, institutional capture, cultural transmission) that pattern-level analysis alone cannot fully articulate.

- **Specify cascade analytically.** Cross-stratum effects become analysable with discipline. Shame's cascade from social cost-imposition to biological damage, coercion's biological effects through fear, power's intellectual effects through accumulation — all can be analysed as cascade with the discipline of mediated, transformative, delayed character.

- **Resolve provisional relation flags.** The schema's flagged provisional relation types (`binding`, `cost_source`) find their formal home. The schema can be updated to commit these types with the framework's analytical backing.

### 9.2 What the framework does not enable

The framework has explicit limits:

- **It does not capture Dynamic Quality.** The framework is a static-pattern account of dynamic processes; it makes no claim to have captured Dynamic Quality itself. The DQ-boundary discipline holds at the level of meta-claim.

- **It does not predict specific outcomes.** The framework is analytical, not predictive. It provides vocabulary and structure for analysing value-flow but does not specify what value-flow will occur in any specific situation.

- **It does not provide quantitative measurement.** Value-flow has direction, magnitude, and character, but the framework does not commit to quantitative measurement. Many phenomena are comparatively quantifiable (more or less flow, stronger or weaker bindings) but not absolutely quantifiable.

- **It does not specify all dynamics.** Many specific phenomena will require their own analyses building on the framework's primitives. The framework provides the vocabulary; it does not provide pre-built analyses of every possible value-flow situation.

### 9.3 The disciplines that must be held

Three disciplines must be held throughout the framework's application:

**DQ-boundary discipline (refined).** The framework's primitives and configurations are static-pattern accounts of dynamics, not captures of Dynamic Quality. The boundary forbids metaphysical inflation of any framework component into a capture of becoming-itself. The boundary does not forbid analysis of change, novelty, or emergence as such — these are legitimate objects of static-pattern inquiry. The discipline is discrimination between analytical content (legitimate) and metaphysical inflation (rejected), applied throughout.

**Stratum discipline.** Cross-stratum dynamics is real and substantial, but it is interaction, not reduction or origination. Cascade is mediated, transformative, and delayed; direct higher-to-lower origination is prohibited; reduction is prohibited. The framework's cross-stratum analyses must continuously hold this discipline.

**Field-relative valuation without relativism.** Value-flow has different effects in different fields; these differences are objective within fields, arising from real consequences at the relevant strata. The framework adopts field-relative valuation as foundational while explicitly rejecting relativism. The Non-Relative Value Hierarchy applies: some bindings, modulations, and accumulation patterns are objectively higher quality because they preserve more strata and produce less dissipation.

**Framework-pattern distinction discipline (v1.2).** Pattern-specific analytical content lives in pattern entries; framework primitives stay minimal. When a stress-test surfaces what appears to be a framework-level refinement, the discipline is to check whether the analytical content is actually pattern-specific. Cases involving mimicry, deception, bluff, manufactured-appearance, and similar phenomena are characteristically pattern-level: the mimicking pattern is its own value pattern (with its own entry, characteristic dynamics, and mimicry-relation to the mimicked pattern), and the framework's existing primitives are sufficient to analyse it. The framework does not need substrate-type fields, perception-status flags, or other framework-level vocabulary to encode what is properly located in pattern entries. This discipline keeps the framework's primitives minimal and lets the atlas grow with the analytical depth those pattern-specific dynamics require. The discipline was articulated in v1.2 after the coercion stress-test surfaced a proposed field-perceptual substrate-type that was correctly identified as pattern-level analytical content belonging to bluff and deception patterns (not yet entered) rather than framework-level vocabulary.

**Structural-character discipline (v1.3).** Framework structural fields describe structural reality at their level of analysis. They do not describe field-level outcomes, sustainability, or value-evaluations of the patterns being analysed. The most consequential application is in binding quality (§2.2): quality describes the structural character of the binding — whether the coupling amplifies both patterns' coherent operation, or distorts one or both — not whether the binding's broader field-effects are constructive or destructive, not whether the binding sustains itself indefinitely, and not whether the pattern's outcomes are approved. Each of those concerns has its proper analytical home: field-level outcomes belong in value-flow configuration analysis (§4.4), sustainability belongs in durability assessment, and value-evaluations are out of scope for structural assessment (the framework explicitly disclaims moral judgment in its structural vocabulary). The discipline was articulated in v1.3 after corrective work on the migrated cluster's binding-quality assessments surfaced that 'quality: low' had been imported as a value-judgment-via-field-effects rather than describing the binding's structural amplification character. The corrected assessment: when a binding amplifies both patterns' coherent operation — even when the pattern produces massive field-destruction (the Mongol pattern under Temujin conquering most of Eurasia in a generation, Alexander's conquest of the Persian Empire in a decade), even when the configuration cannot sustain without continuous substrate-acquisition or replenishment — that binding is structurally high or conditional, not low. Destructive field-effects belong in the value-flow configuration analysis (extraction-with-substrate-depletion, often paired with expansionist substrate-acquisition); the binding's quality is what it is structurally. The discipline generalises to other structural fields: modulation character, compositional character, and other structural assessments should not import field-level outcomes or sustainability concerns either. The recurring shape of all five disciplines is the same: keep each analytical field carrying the kind of content it is meant to carry; resist the temptation to overload structural fields with field-level outcomes, sustainability concerns, or moral evaluations that have their proper analytical home elsewhere.

### 9.4 What comes next

The framework v1.2 integrates the findings of two stress-tests (power, coercion) completed against framework v1.1. The integration consists of four confirmed refinements (binding_kind, binding-portfolio, companion_relation, maintained-premise as temporal-character) and one explicitly articulated discipline (framework-pattern distinction). One proposed refinement candidate (field-perceptual substrate-type for bindings) was withdrawn after analysis showed it was pattern-level analytical content belonging in bluff and deception entries rather than framework-level vocabulary.

The framework's application requires several subsequent steps:

**Apply v1.2 corrections to existing entries.** The coercion v0.3 records currently include a `substrate_type` field that was added before the framework-pattern distinction discipline was articulated. The field encodes pattern-specific content that should live in bluff and deception entries instead. The records should be revised to remove the field; the analytical content moves to (future) bluff and deception entries.

**Continue stress-testing.** Shame and guilt remain to be tested against the dynamics framework. Shame's stress-test will most rigorously test the cascade primitive (shame's social-to-biological cascade is its defining cross-stratum operation). Guilt's stress-test will test intra-agent dynamics in ways neither power nor coercion stressed. The framework's coverage of these patterns under v1.2 will likely surface further refinements or confirm sufficiency.

**Schema updates.** The schema (currently v0.4) must be updated to incorporate the framework's relation types and the v1.2 field additions. Binding becomes a committed relation type with binding_kind, companion_relation, and the other binding fields. The previously-flagged provisional types in the schema (binding, distinction, cost-source) are resolved: binding is committed; cost-source is a binding_kind value rather than a separate type; distinction remains provisional pending its own resolution.

**Pattern-entry revisions.** Patterns previously migrated acquire dynamics-side content. Power and coercion have already been revised (v0.3); shame and guilt remain. As bluff, deception, and other patterns with characteristic mimicry or perception-dependent operation are added to the atlas, they will provide the analytical depth that the framework-pattern distinction discipline correctly locates at pattern level.

**Cluster expansion.** With binding formally available and binding_kind articulated, additional patterns can be migrated that previously would have required provisional flagging. Patterns whose primary operation involves binding (legitimacy, authority, trust, reciprocity in greater depth, obligation, influence) are natural next entries. Mimicry-patterns (bluff, deception) are also natural next entries given the framework-pattern distinction discipline; these will fill the analytical gap that v1.2 correctly identified as belonging at pattern level rather than framework level.

**Canon promotion.** After stress-testing demonstrates the framework's stability across the migrated cluster and the schema is updated to support its operation, individual elements may be promoted to Canon through the project's governance process. Promotion is not automatic; each element must be evaluated explicitly. The framework as a whole remains Exploratory until promotion criteria are met.

### 9.5 Closing orientation

The dynamics framework is a static-pattern account of value-flow. Its primitives, configurations, and analyses are intellectual-stratum patterns about dynamic processes. The framework does not capture Dynamic Quality; it describes how value moves among existing patterns and how patterns change through value-flow, with the discipline that all such accounts are themselves intellectual work and not the underlying reality.

The framework rests on Circle/Ripple Theory as foundation and adopts selectively from surveyed traditions. It complements the statics framework rather than displacing it. It provides the vocabulary needed to resolve the provisional flags accumulated across the migration work and to articulate the dynamics-side content that the statics framework could only gesture toward.

The construction's success or failure will be determined by whether it survives stress-testing against the migrated patterns, whether the schema update succeeds in formalising it operationally, and whether further pattern migrations using the framework continue to find it adequate to their analytical needs. The same discipline applied to the statics framework applies here: the framework has been constructed; whether it earns persistence remains to be determined.

**v1.1 (2026-05-26)** — Two foundational corrections following user challenge to v1.0:

- *§1.2 rewritten from "Value-flow is not conserved" to "Value-flow has real and tractable quality differences."* The v1.0 framing claimed value is metaphysically created and destroyed in flow, which was not licensed by Circle/Ripple Theory, Pirsig, the survey, or any other source.
