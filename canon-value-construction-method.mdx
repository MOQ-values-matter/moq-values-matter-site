## Purpose

This document defines **how** value patterns are identified, described, evaluated, scored, and structured for Canon promotion within MOQ Values Matter.

Canon Atlas entries are:

- **descriptive**, not prescriptive
- **functional**, not moralised
- **structural**, not ideological
- **revision-disciplined**, not expressive
- **dimensionally scored** against laws and field-bearer combinations, not summarised by a single quality number
- **machine-extractable** through structured frontmatter that constitutes the source of truth for analytical views

This document does not define individual values. It defines the **procedure by which values are defined and analysed**.

For Canon promotion criteria, see `canon-promotion-checklist.mdx`.
For frontmatter schema specification, see `atlas-frontmatter-schema.mdx`.
For AI enforcement rules during construction, see `ai-instructions.mdx` Section 8.

## 0. Foundational Distinction — Eligibility versus Quality

Before stating eligibility criteria, this document makes two foundational distinctions that govern the entire Atlas.

### The First Distinction — Eligibility versus Quality

**Eligibility** is whether a pattern is real enough and structurally observable enough to be analysed by the project's framework. Eligibility is binary — a pattern is admitted to the Atlas or it is not.

**Quality** is what the analysis produces once a pattern is admitted. Quality is dimensional — expressed through the structural axis profile (how the pattern relates to the laws) and the field-bearer matrix (how it ripples value effects across relational contexts and bearers). Quality is not a single number; it is a profile.

**The Atlas catalogues recurrent value patterns at all quality levels.** It includes patterns that satisfy multiple structural axes simultaneously without trade-off (high-quality patterns), patterns that serve some axes at the cost of others (mixed-quality patterns), and patterns that systematically degrade multiple axes while sustaining themselves through enforcement, capture, or coercion (low-quality patterns). All of these are eligible if they are structurally real. Their quality profiles tell the analytical story.

**This distinction is load-bearing.** The project's analytical contribution depends on it. Excluding low-quality patterns from the Atlas because they are low-quality would be a category error — it would prevent the systematic analysis of value patterns that is the project's reason for existence. Pirsig observed that it is least costly at the idea stage to invent patterns that ignore the levels they depend on, and that such patterns can be propagated through institutional power and enforcement. Analysing exactly such patterns is among the most important things the Atlas should be capable of doing.

### The Second Distinction — Field-Experiential Scoring versus Cross-Field Structural Costs

The quality analysis itself contains a further distinction that the construction method must honour rigorously:

**Field-experiential scoring** is what the field-bearer matrix captures. Each cell scores what that field actually experiences from the pattern's typical operation, from the field's own value-experiential perspective. This is field-relative valuation as objective reality — the same pattern produces different real value effects in different fields, and those effects are objective within those fields. The matrix is a snapshot of these field-specific experiences.

**Cross-field structural costs** are what the matrix does not capture. The accumulated asymmetry between bearer-aligned-field benefits and hostile-field costs produces structural volatility, counter-pattern formation, long-term system instability, and the temporal dynamics through which patterns evolve, persist, or collapse. These belong in Section 7 (Persistence Logic) and Section 9 (MOQ Coherence Verdict), where the field-experiential snapshot is integrated with temporal dynamics to produce the verdict on structural quality.

**This second distinction is also load-bearing.** Conflating field-experiential effects with cross-field structural costs produces a different kind of category error: it imports moral aggregates into the matrix where field-relative reality should be captured, and it averages out the structural asymmetries that are themselves the analytical signature of patterns like coercion, deception, and predation. The matrix shows the field-by-field experiential reality; the verdict integrates that reality with the structural costs the matrix snapshot cannot show.

### What These Distinctions Together Produce

The four anchors below test for **structural reality** (eligibility), not for quality. The 12-section structure that follows produces the **quality analysis** for any admitted pattern. Within that quality analysis, the structural axis profile (Section 3) shows the pattern's contribution to the laws as dimensional axes, the field-bearer matrix (encoded in frontmatter and described in Section 6) shows the pattern's field-experiential value-effect profile, and the verdict (Section 9) integrates these with persistence logic (Section 7) to produce the structural quality assessment.

Each of these components answers a different question. Together they constitute the analysis the project exists to produce.

## 1. What Counts as a Canon Value

A Canon value is a **recurrent value pattern** that:

1. Appears across cultures, contexts, or historical periods (recurrence test)
2. Produces observable effects on coordination, selection, recognition, or behaviour (effect test)
3. Operates as a structural pattern rather than a single instance (pattern test)
4. Exhibits identifiable failure modes — conditions under which it weakens, breaks down, or produces specific dysfunction (failure-mode test)
5. Can be located within MOQ levels and scored against the structural axes (analysability test)

These criteria are about whether a pattern is structurally real enough to be admitted to systematic analysis. They are not about whether the pattern operates beneficially or whether members of affected groups would endorse it.

What is **not** Canon-eligible:

- Patterns that exist only as proposals, slogans, or unimplemented ideas with no observable structural effect
- Single instances of behaviour without recurrent pattern across contexts
- Pure rhetoric or sloganry without behaviour-shaping function
- Subjective preferences that have no observable structural reality independent of individual report

What is Canon-eligible regardless of quality:

- Coercive value patterns that propagate through enforcement
- Ideological patterns that override the levels they depend on
- Deceptive patterns that succeed structurally despite (or because of) their dishonesty
- Captured institutional patterns that persist past the point of serving their original function
- Patterns that produce strong negative ripples in hostile and neutral fields while sustaining themselves through internal-field benefits

These are eligible because they are real patterns with observable effects. Their quality is what the Atlas analysis articulates — through the structural axis profile, the field-bearer matrix, and the MOQ Coherence Verdict.

## 2. The Four Canon Anchors

Every Canon value entry must satisfy all four anchors before construction begins. These are pre-conditions for admission to systematic analysis, not quality tests. The quality analysis happens in the entry itself.

<a id="anchor-a"></a>
### Anchor A — Pattern, Not Opinion

The value must describe a **selection or coordination pattern** with observable structural effects, not a belief or preference held in isolation.

A valid Canon value:

- persists as a pattern across multiple instances
- can be observed without endorsement
- influences behaviour through structure, regardless of whether the influence is benign or harmful

If a pattern disappears entirely when no one is talking about it — that is, if it has no behavioural reality independent of its self-description — it is not Canon-eligible. But a pattern enforced through institutional propagation, sustained through coercion, or reproduced through capture is still a pattern; its dependence on enforcement is part of its quality profile, not a reason to exclude it.

<a id="anchor-b"></a>
### Anchor B — MOQ Level Discipline

Each Canon value must declare:

- **one and only one primary MOQ level**
- zero or more secondary levels

The primary level is where the value originates, is first stabilised, and performs its core function.

Secondary levels may constrain, articulate, amplify, or distort the value through legitimate cross-level interaction. They may **not** originate the value pattern as substrate.

"All-level" values are not permitted. A pattern that cannot be located within MOQ levels is not Canon-eligible because the project's analytical framework cannot be applied to it.

<a id="anchor-c"></a>
### Anchor C — Observable Structural Reality

The pattern must produce observable structural effects on selection, coordination, recognition, or behaviour that persist across instances. The pattern must:

- be recurrent across more than one instance, time period, or cultural context
- produce effects observable independently of the pattern's own self-description
- be distinguishable from mere assertion, slogan, or rhetorical proposal

Patterns that exist only as proposals or unimplemented ideas are not yet Canon-eligible. They become eligible when they have been instantiated long enough to produce observable structural effects.

**Critical clarification:** This anchor tests for structural reality, not for non-coercive operation. Patterns that operate through enforcement, coercion, or institutional propagation are eligible if they are observably structural. Their reliance on enforcement appears in the entry's quality analysis (the structural axis profile and field-bearer matrix), not as an eligibility gate. A pattern that exists *only* through coercion may have a low quality profile, but it is not therefore ineligible — it is one of the patterns the Atlas exists to analyse.

What this anchor excludes is not low-quality patterns but **non-patterns**: rhetoric without effect, manifestos without instantiation, slogans without behavioural reality.

<a id="anchor-d"></a>
### Anchor D — Real Failure Modes

Every Canon value must be able to **fail structurally** in identifiable ways.

Failure modes must be:

- repeatable
- observable
- non-moralised
- non-exceptional

Failure modes for high-quality patterns describe how the pattern degrades when its operating conditions are not met. Failure modes for low-quality patterns describe how the pattern degrades the systems it operates within and how the pattern itself collapses when its support mechanisms (enforcement, capture, coercion) are removed. Both kinds of failure modes are admissible; what is required is that some structural failure mode be identifiable.

If a pattern cannot fail in any identifiable way, it is not a value pattern with structural reality — it is a slogan or an ideological assertion masquerading as a pattern.

## 3. Construction Pipeline

Canon entries are constructed using the following enforced sequence. Each step must be completed before proceeding to the next.

<a id="step-1"></a>
### Step 1 — Identify Pre-Justification Recognition

Determine the **earliest MOQ level** at which the pattern is recognised prior to explanation, social validation, rule enforcement, or intellectual justification.

Use these guidelines to test level placement:

- Requires language → not biological
- Requires norms → not biological
- Requires reasoning → not intellectual (note: reasoning *operates within* the intellectual level; if a pattern only manifests through reasoning processes, it may be intellectual; if it requires reasoning to recognise, that is a different test)
- Requires enforcement → not primary social (the pattern *uses* enforcement at higher levels but is recognised structurally before enforcement)

This step determines the **primary level**.

For coercive or captured patterns: identify where the pattern *originates* structurally, not where it is *enforced*. A coercive social pattern still has a primary level — the level at which the pattern's structural function exists, even if its perpetuation depends on enforcement.

<a id="step-2"></a>
### Step 2 — Functional Role per MOQ Level

For each MOQ level, answer only: *What does this pattern do at this level?*

Not what people think it means, what it symbolises, or whether it is good or bad.

Assign level scores:

- **0** — no functional role at this level
- **1** — secondary or supportive role
- **2** — core stabilising or originating role

Scores must reflect **functional role**, not importance, desirability, or moral weight. Exactly one level scores 2 and must match the declared primary level.

For low-quality patterns: the functional role may be *destructive* rather than constructive. The level score reflects the magnitude of the pattern's role at the level, not the direction of effect. A pattern can score 2 at the social level by virtue of being a core social pattern even when its effects are systematically degrading — the level score captures structural centrality, while the field-bearer matrix captures directional effect.

<a id="step-3"></a>
### Step 3 — Structural Axis Profiling

For each level where the pattern scores 1 or 2, score the pattern against each Law within that level. Score range is 0 to 2:

- **0** — does not engage with this structural axis
- **1** — secondary contribution to the axis
- **2** — core contribution to the axis

This step produces the **structural axis profile** — the pattern's position within the dimensional space the laws define. The profile is more granular than the level score and is what makes downstream analytical comparison between patterns possible.

A level's structural axis profile is required if the level score is 1 or 2. A level's profile may be omitted if the level score is 0.

For low-quality patterns: a pattern can score high contribution to one axis while undermining others, producing a distinctive profile that distinguishes structurally degrading patterns from structurally supporting ones. This is part of what the analysis is designed to capture.

<a id="step-4"></a>
### Step 4 — Determine Bearer Applicability

Declare which bearer dimensions are meaningful for the pattern:

- **agent_only** — the pattern is inherently individual; group-carried form is degenerate (e.g. Pride, Lust, Greed)
- **group_only** — the pattern is inherently collective; agent-carried form is degenerate (e.g. Solidarity)
- **both** — the pattern can be carried by individuals or by groups, with different effects (most patterns)

This declaration drives Step 5 (field-bearer matrix scoring) and Section 5 of the entry (Agent vs Group Instantiation).

<a id="step-5"></a>
### Step 5 — Field-Bearer Matrix Scoring

For each meaningful bearer (per Step 4 declaration), score the pattern's value effects in each of the four canonical fields. The scoring follows a specific principle that must be applied consistently across all entries.

#### The Four Canonical Fields

- **Internal** — those whose value experience depends on the bearer (unchosen, hard to exit)
- **Allied** — those with active committed engagement in the bearer's success (chosen, invested)
- **Neutral** — those without active commitment to the bearer (observers judging through reputation)
- **Hostile** — those whose interests run counter to the bearer (regardless of active opposition)

#### The Field-Relative Scoring Principle

Each cell of the matrix scores **what that field actually experiences when the pattern is operating in its typical modes, from that field's own value-experiential perspective.**

This principle is load-bearing. Score interpretation depends entirely on it.

The scoring is *not*:

- A universal moral assessment of the pattern
- A neutral observer's judgment of whether the pattern is good or bad
- A single-application snapshot of one specific use of the pattern
- An aggregate verdict imported from outside the field's own experience

The scoring *is*:

- The field's actual value-experiential position under the pattern's characteristic operation
- Integrated across the typical application modes of the pattern (the pattern as it actually operates, not one extreme or another)
- Scored from inside that field's own value perspective (what does this field experience as enhancement or degradation of its value-field)

This honours the project's foundational principle of field-relative valuation: the same act produces different real value effects in different fields, and those effects are objective within those fields. The scoring captures these field-specific realities rather than averaging them into moral aggregates.

#### Score Range and Meaning

Score range is **-2 to +2**:

- **+2** — strong positive value effect across the pattern's typical operation (the field's value field is significantly enhanced)
- **+1** — positive value effect across the pattern's typical operation
- **0** — see ambivalence semantics below
- **-1** — negative value effect across the pattern's typical operation
- **-2** — strong negative value effect across the pattern's typical operation (the field's value field is significantly degraded)

#### The Meaning of 0

A score of 0 has two distinct possible meanings, both legitimate:

**0 as neutral effect:** The pattern's operation produces no significant value effect in this field across the typical application modes. The field is essentially uninvolved in the pattern's effects.

**0 as structural ambivalence:** The field experiences genuinely mixed effects across the pattern's application modes — substantially positive effects when the pattern is applied in some modes and substantially negative effects when applied in others, with no single direction dominant across the typical operation.

The two meanings are distinguished in the entry's prose (Section 6), not in the score itself. The Relational Impact Map must articulate which kind of 0 applies and why. Structural ambivalence is itself analytically meaningful — it identifies fields whose experience under the pattern depends critically on application mode rather than being characteristic of the pattern as such.

#### Application-Target Sensitivity

Some patterns are highly target-directed (coercion, deception, sanction-as-punishment) — their effects depend strongly on which field the pattern is applied to. For these patterns, the matrix should reflect the field's experience across the pattern's typical operation, including both cases where the pattern is applied to that field and cases where the pattern is applied elsewhere with this field as bystander or beneficiary.

When a target-directed pattern produces strongly opposite effects in a field depending on application target — for example, when a field benefits from the pattern's application to a common enemy but is harmed by the pattern's application to itself — the score of 0 (structural ambivalence) is often the correct score, with prose articulation of the application-mode dependence.

Other patterns are more field-uniform (honour, truth, communication) — their effects are more consistent across application contexts because the pattern operates through reputation, structural conformance, or transmission rather than through targeted application. For these patterns, the matrix scores tend to be more uniform across fields and require less application-mode interpretation in the prose.

The construction method does not require a separate target-directedness declaration in frontmatter. The pattern's character — whether target-directed or field-uniform — emerges from the matrix profile and the prose explanation in Section 6.

#### Cross-Field Structural Costs Live Elsewhere

The field-bearer matrix is a snapshot of field-experiential value effects under the pattern's typical operation. It is not the place to capture:

- Long-term volatility produced by accumulated cross-field asymmetry
- Counter-pattern formation in response to sustained pattern operation
- System-level instability arising from the pattern's structural axis profile
- Temporal dynamics of pattern evolution and decay

These belong in Section 7 (Persistence Logic) and Section 9 (MOQ Coherence Verdict), where the temporal dynamics and structural costs of the pattern are integrated with the field-experiential snapshot to produce the verdict on coherence.

A pattern can score positively across most field-bearer cells in the matrix and still receive a sharp verdict in Section 9 if its cross-field asymmetry produces structural volatility that the matrix snapshot cannot capture. Coercion is a clear example: even when bearer-aligned fields experience genuine ambivalence (0 scores) and only the hostile field shows strong negative effect, the cross-field asymmetry between bearer benefits and target costs is what generates the historical pattern of coercive systems producing violent counter-movements. That cross-field structural cost belongs in the verdict, not in the matrix.

#### Degenerate Cells

Cells may be marked `null` where the bearer-field combination is genuinely degenerate — structurally undefined for the pattern, distinct from neutral effect which scores 0 and from structural ambivalence which also scores 0 but with different prose articulation.

#### What the Matrix Produces

This step produces the **field-bearer matrix** — the pattern's field-experiential value-effect profile across relational contexts and bearer types. Combined with the structural axis profile, this constitutes the entry's complete atomic scoring data.

The matrix's analytical power comes from honouring field-relative valuation rigorously. Patterns that produce broadly positive effects across all fields (some forms of trust, communication, certain forms of cooperation) show one kind of profile. Patterns that produce strongly polarised effects (positive in bearer-aligned fields, negative in hostile fields, with ambivalence in mixed fields) show another. Patterns that produce uniformly negative effects across most fields show a third. Each profile is structurally informative — and these distinctions are only visible because the matrix captures field-specific experiential reality rather than imposing a universal moral aggregate.

#### Bearer Applicability

For low-quality patterns, the matrix is where the pattern's character becomes most visible. A pattern with a polarised profile (strong positive in bearer-aligned fields, strong negative in hostile fields, ambivalence in mixed fields) reveals itself as target-directed and asymmetrically benefiting; the polarisation itself is the analytical signature of patterns like coercion, deception, and predation. A pattern with a uniform profile reveals itself as field-uniform and broadly stabilising or broadly degrading depending on direction.

<a id="step-6"></a>
### Step 6 — Enforce Cross-Level Discipline

Cross-level interaction must follow MOQ directionality without overstating prohibition:

- Lower levels enable higher levels by providing substrate
- Higher levels can stabilise, constrain, formalise, distort, or modulate lower-level patterns — this is legitimate cross-level interaction
- Higher levels cannot **originate** lower-level patterns — they cannot bring into existence what the lower level has not produced as substrate
- Reductionism across levels is prohibited: no level can be explained as merely a configuration of the level it depends on

This rule prevents social constructionism, intellectual reductionism, and metaphysical leakage, while preserving the holistic interaction that makes the MOQ hierarchy a living system rather than a sterile chain.

<a id="step-7"></a>
### Step 7 — Replace Moral Claims with Functional Conditions

Canon language must avoid praise, teleology, moral exhortation, and normative conclusion.

All effects must be framed as conditions and consequences:

- *"When this pattern is active, systems tend to…"*
- *"This pattern degrades when…"*
- *"This pattern persists through…"*
- *"This pattern produces field effects of…"*

Canon entries describe what happens, not what should happen. This applies equally to high-quality patterns (whose effects are described, not praised) and to low-quality patterns (whose effects are described, not condemned). The MOQ Coherence Verdict in Section 9 is where conditions are stated under which the pattern increases or degrades coherence — but this is a structural verdict, not a moral one.

<a id="step-8"></a>
### Step 8 — Empirical Anchoring

Evidence must be framed as observation → recurrence → cross-domain convergence.

Evidence must not justify the pattern morally, prove it correct, or invoke metaphysical authority. All empirical content must remain descriptive.

For low-quality patterns: empirical evidence about coercive, deceptive, or captured patterns is admissible and important. The Atlas's analytical credibility depends on its capacity to describe what these patterns actually do, with the same evidentiary discipline applied to high-quality patterns. Documentation of how patterns of organised deception, propaganda, ideological capture, or institutional coercion actually operate is exactly the kind of empirical anchoring the Atlas should produce.

<a id="step-9"></a>
### Step 9 — Pattern-Specific Boundary Rejection

Each Canon entry must explicitly state what it is not, especially where confusion is likely.

Pattern-specific rejections address conflations and misframings that apply to *this specific pattern*:

- Conflation with neighbouring value patterns where the distinction matters
- Common ideological framings of this specific pattern that the entry rejects
- Reductionist readings that miss the structural function

Atlas-wide boundary rejections (those that apply to all Atlas patterns rather than to the specific pattern) belong in the Atlas-Wide Boundary Rejections registry, not in individual entry sections. Pattern-specific rejections belong in Section 11 of the entry.

Boundary rejection is mandatory Canon hygiene, not optional commentary.

## 4. Canon Entry Structure

Every Canon value entry must follow this 12-section structure exactly. Each section has a specific analytical role and strict constraints. Sections are not interchangeable and content must not leak across them.

This structure prevents poetic drift, moral inflation, level collapse, ideological framing, and repetition disguised as depth. Combined with the structured frontmatter (per `atlas-frontmatter-schema.mdx`), it produces entries that are both readable as analytical prose and machine-extractable as structured data.

The frontmatter is the source of truth for atomic scoring data. Prose sections explain and contextualise the scores; frontmatter contains them. Derived analytical views (heatmaps, quadrant placement, ripple analysis, cluster identification) are computed from frontmatter and never hand-maintained.

<a id="section-1"></a>
### Section 1 — Neutral Definition

**Purpose:** State what the pattern *is*, without argument, praise, condemnation, or justification.

**Must do:**
- Define the pattern as a pattern of selection, recognition, coordination, or operation
- Use descriptive, functional language
- Be readable without MOQ expertise
- Stand alone as a reference definition
- Distinguish the pattern from neighbouring patterns where confusion is common (constitutive distinctions only — broader rejections belong in Section 11)
- Apply equally well to high-quality and low-quality instantiations of the pattern

**Must not:**
- Argue the pattern is good, necessary, superior, evil, or harmful
- Appeal to emotion, spirituality, or moral authority
- Invoke Dynamic Quality causally
- Depend on examples to make sense
- Smuggle quality verdicts into the definition

**Validation question:** Can this definition be quoted out of context without becoming ideological, vague, or evaluatively loaded?

<a id="section-2"></a>
### Section 2 — Multi-Level Evaluation (0–2 per level)

**Purpose:** Assign the pattern's functional role at each MOQ level.

**Must do:**
- Assign a score (0, 1, or 2) for each MOQ level (matching frontmatter `level_scores`)
- Provide a one-paragraph functional rationale per level
- Clearly identify the primary level (score = 2)

**Must not:**
- Score based on importance, desirability, or moral weight
- Score based on whether the pattern's effects are constructive or destructive (that is captured in the field-bearer matrix)
- Assign multiple primary levels
- Use metaphorical or evaluative language
- Drift between this section and the frontmatter; the two must agree

**Validation question:** If the pattern disappeared at this level, would the system still function essentially the same?

<a id="section-3"></a>
### Section 3 — Structural Axis Profile

**Purpose:** Score the pattern's contribution to each Law within each level where it has functional role, locating the pattern within the dimensional space the laws define.

**Must do:**
- For each level scoring 1 or 2 in Section 2, score against each Law in that level (matching frontmatter `structural_axis_profile`)
- Provide a brief functional rationale for each score
- Make explicit which axes the pattern supports strongly, which it engages secondarily, and which it does not engage with
- For low-quality patterns: make explicit which axes the pattern *undermines* even where its level score is 1 or 2 (the structural axis profile uses 0-2 for contribution magnitude; directional degradation appears in the prose rationale and in the field-bearer matrix)
- Use score range 0 to 2 (functional contribution magnitude)

**Must not:**
- Skip levels where the level score is 1 or 2 (partial coverage is not permitted)
- Score against axes outside the level structure (no inventing dimensions)
- Conflate structural contribution with directional value effect (that is Section 6)
- Drift from frontmatter scoring

**Validation question:** Does the axis profile capture how this pattern actually relates to each structural axis, or is it averaging real differences into uniform scores?

<a id="section-4"></a>
### Section 4 — Multi-Level Coherence Analysis

**Purpose:** Explain how the pattern behaves across levels through legitimate cross-level interaction without collapsing levels.

**Must do:**
- Describe how the pattern originates at its primary level
- Describe how it interacts with lower levels (recruiting substrate, modulating, formalising)
- Describe how it is constrained or shaped by higher levels (where applicable)
- Explicitly state conditions of coherence and conditions of degradation
- For low-quality patterns: describe how the pattern's operation degrades systems at multiple levels, identifying the specific cross-level dynamics

**Must not:**
- Introduce new definitions
- Re-score levels or axes
- Claim that higher levels originate lower-level patterns (modulation is allowed; origination is not)
- Reduce a level to a configuration of a lower one
- Invoke Dynamic Quality as a mechanism

**Validation question:** Does this section preserve the holistic, interactive nature of MOQ levels while honouring the prohibition on origination across levels?

<a id="section-5"></a>
### Section 5 — Agent vs Group Instantiation

**Purpose:** Describe how the pattern operates differently when carried by an individual agent versus when carried by a group, where both bearers are meaningful.

**Must do:**
- Match the bearer applicability declared in frontmatter
- Where applicability is `both`: describe agent-carried operation, group-carried operation, and the structural differences between them
- Where applicability is `agent_only`: explain why group-carried operation is degenerate for this pattern
- Where applicability is `group_only`: explain why agent-carried operation is degenerate for this pattern
- Identify which structural axes each bearer most strongly engages
- Identify the failure modes specific to each bearer
- For low-quality patterns: distinguish agent-carried failure modes from group-carried failure modes (these often differ substantially in scale and propagation dynamics)

**Must not:**
- Treat agent and group instantiations as merely different scales of the same operation
- Claim bearer applicability that contradicts frontmatter
- Skip this section for patterns with `bearer_applicability: both`

**Validation question:** Are agent-carried and group-carried operations genuinely structurally different for this pattern, or has the analysis collapsed them?

<a id="section-6"></a>
### Section 6 — Relational Impact Map

**Purpose:** Describe the field-experiential value effects the pattern produces in each of the four canonical fields, from each field's own value perspective, across the pattern's typical operation modes (per Step 5 frontmatter scoring).

**Must do:**
- Describe effects in the **Internal** field for each meaningful bearer, scored from the field's own perspective
- Describe effects in the **Allied** field for each meaningful bearer, scored from the field's own perspective
- Describe effects in the **Neutral** field for each meaningful bearer, scored from the field's own perspective
- Describe effects in the **Hostile** field for each meaningful bearer, scored from the field's own perspective
- Match scores in frontmatter `field_bearer_matrix`
- Note bearer-differentials where they are structurally significant
- Articulate the application-mode context: when the pattern is applied to this field directly, when this field is bystander, when this field is beneficiary of pattern application elsewhere
- Where a cell scores 0, articulate explicitly which kind of 0 applies — neutral effect (no significant effect across operation modes) or structural ambivalence (substantially mixed effects across operation modes with no dominant direction)
- For target-directed patterns (coercion, deception, sanction-as-punishment, predation): make application-mode dependence explicit, including how the field's experience differs when the pattern is applied to it versus when the field is beneficiary of application elsewhere

**Must not:**
- Score from a universal moral perspective rather than from the field's own experience
- Aggregate field experiences into an overall judgment of the pattern
- Moralise actors
- Assume benevolent intent
- Describe outcomes as "deserved" or "earned" in moral terms
- Use ad hoc field names instead of the four canonical fields
- Drift from frontmatter scoring
- Capture cross-field structural costs or long-term volatility in this section (those belong in Section 7 Persistence Logic and Section 9 MOQ Coherence Verdict)

**Validation question:** Does the relational impact map honour field-relative valuation by scoring each field from its own perspective across the pattern's typical operation, including honest 0 scores for genuinely ambivalent fields, without importing moral framing or aggregating into a universal verdict?

<a id="section-7"></a>
### Section 7 — Persistence Logic

**Purpose:** Explain why the pattern recurs and survives over time, and what conditions cause it to weaken or collapse — capturing temporal dynamics that the field-bearer matrix snapshot cannot.

**Must do:**
- Identify the functional or structural problems the pattern solves (or appears to solve)
- For high-quality patterns: explain persistence through structural efficiency at solving coordination problems
- For low-quality patterns: explain persistence through enforcement mechanisms, capture of better patterns, ideological propagation, sunk-cost dynamics, or asymmetric benefit to bearers covering for cross-field costs
- For polarised patterns (strong asymmetry in the field-bearer matrix between bearer-aligned and hostile fields): describe how the polarisation produces characteristic temporal dynamics — counter-pattern formation, eventual destabilisation, cycles of escalation and collapse
- Describe conditions under which the pattern weakens or disappears
- Identify what shifts in field perception, application mode, or system conditions cause the pattern to lose its operating effectiveness

**Must not:**
- Appeal to timelessness, destiny, or virtue
- Argue that persistence proves moral truth or structural soundness
- Confuse popularity with stability
- Confuse enforcement with structural quality
- Treat persistence as evidence of high quality (patterns can persist for many reasons including bearer-asymmetric benefit)

**Validation question:** If the supports of this pattern (whether structural quality, enforcement mechanism, or asymmetric benefit) were removed, would it still re-emerge — and why? What temporal dynamics does the pattern's operation produce that cross-field analysis must capture?

<a id="section-8"></a>
### Section 8 — Empirical Evidence

**Purpose:** Anchor the pattern in observable reality.

**Must do:**
- Use cross-domain evidence (biology, sociology, history, anthropology, behavioural economics, political science, etc.)
- Frame evidence as observation → pattern → interpretation
- Demonstrate recurrence, not proof
- Attribute specific empirical claims to their literature where possible
- For low-quality patterns: cite empirical literature on coercion, propaganda, institutional capture, ideological enforcement, or destructive group dynamics where applicable

**Must not:**
- Use evidence to justify moral claims
- Invoke authority as validation
- Cherry-pick cases that support only one framing
- Present unattributed claims as established findings

**Validation question:** Do these observations remain meaningful even if the reader disagrees with the pattern's content or operation?

<a id="section-9"></a>
### Section 9 — MOQ Coherence Verdict

**Purpose:** State the pattern's systemic role and limits within MOQ, integrating the field-experiential snapshot from the matrix with cross-field structural costs and temporal dynamics that the snapshot cannot capture.

**Must do:**
- State clearly the conditions under which the pattern increases coherence
- State clearly the conditions under which the pattern degrades coherence
- Reaffirm primary level placement
- Where bearer-applicability is `both`, distinguish bearer-specific quality verdicts where they differ structurally
- Connect the verdict to the structural axis profile and field-bearer matrix scores from earlier sections
- Capture cross-field structural costs that the matrix snapshot does not show: long-term volatility, counter-pattern formation, accumulated asymmetric costs across fields, system-level instability arising from the pattern's structural axis profile
- Capture temporal dynamics: how the pattern's operation evolves over sustained use, what conditions cause it to weaken or collapse, what cycles it tends to produce
- For low-quality patterns: state honestly when the pattern systematically degrades coherence rather than producing it conditionally
- For polarised patterns (those with strong asymmetry between bearer-aligned and hostile field experiences in the matrix): articulate how the polarisation produces structural costs beyond what any single field's experience captures

**Must not:**
- Introduce new analysis not grounded in the structural axis profile, field-bearer matrix, or persistence logic
- Re-argue earlier sections
- Declare the pattern supreme or foundational
- Soften verdicts to maintain neutrality where the structural analysis genuinely produces a sharp verdict
- Conflate the field-experiential snapshot with the cross-field structural verdict — these are different analytical levels
- Produce a verdict that does not constrain misuse as much as it highlights function

**Validation question:** Does this verdict integrate the field-experiential snapshot from the matrix with the cross-field structural costs and temporal dynamics, producing an honest assessment of the pattern's systemic role across both dimensions?

<a id="section-10"></a>
### Section 10 — Summary Schema

**Purpose:** Provide a compact reference abstraction scannable without reading the full entry.

**Must do:**
- List: primary level, secondary levels, bearer applicability, level score profile, strongest structural axes, coherence type, persistence drivers, common failure modes, bearer differential (where applicable), field profile summary
- Be scannable and unambiguous
- Be derivable from the frontmatter and earlier sections without introducing new content

**Must not:**
- Introduce nuance not present elsewhere
- Use poetic or metaphorical phrasing
- Replace earlier sections
- Drift from frontmatter scoring

**Validation question:** Could this schema be used accurately without reading the full entry?

<a id="section-11"></a>
### Section 11 — Pattern-Specific Boundary Rejections

**Purpose:** Prevent misinterpretation and ideological capture specific to this pattern.

**Must do:**
- Explicitly reject common incorrect framings that apply specifically to this pattern
- Name competing explanations the entry does not adopt
- Explain why those framings fail structurally
- Distinguish the pattern from neighbouring patterns where conflation is common
- For low-quality patterns: reject framings that justify or sanitise the pattern, *and* reject framings that reduce the pattern to a single dimension when its analysis is more complex

**Must not:**
- Restate Atlas-wide rejections (those belong in the Atlas-Wide Boundary Rejections registry)
- Strawman opposing views
- Engage in debate or rhetoric
- Introduce new positive claims

**Validation question:** Does this section reduce future misuse or overextension specific to this pattern?

- Include the Canon Declaration verbatim when Canon status is granted (within this section)

**Must not:**
- Contain analytical content

**Validation question:** Could this section be removed without changing the meaning of the value entry?

## 5. Frontmatter Conformance

Every Canon entry must include structured frontmatter conforming to the schema specified in `atlas-frontmatter-schema.mdx`. The frontmatter contains the atomic scoring data; the prose sections interpret and explain it.

**Required frontmatter fields (summary):**

- Identity: `title`, `atlas_id`, `status`, `type`, `version`
- Level placement: `primary_level`, `secondary_levels` (optional)
- Bearer applicability: `bearer_applicability`
- Level scores: `level_scores` (all four levels)
- Structural axis profile: `structural_axis_profile` (per level where score is 1 or 2)
- Field-bearer matrix: `field_bearer_matrix` (per declared bearer)
- Lifecycle: `canon_declared` (when Canon), `last_revised`, `revision_history`

**Conformance rule:** When the entry's prose disagrees with the frontmatter, the frontmatter is the source of truth. The prose must be revised to match the frontmatter, not the other way around.

For the complete schema specification, validation rules, and computed fields, see `atlas-frontmatter-schema.mdx`.

## 6. Structural Invariant

A Canon entry is valid **only if**:

- All 12 sections are present in canonical order with exact canonical headings
- Each section answers its own validation question
- No section performs another section's function
- The entry remains intelligible when read non-linearly
- Frontmatter conforms to the schema and matches the prose
- The structural axis profile and field-bearer matrix are populated according to bearer applicability
- Cross-level analysis honours the strengthened framework (origination prohibited, modulation legitimate)
- Eligibility is established by the four anchors; quality is articulated by the structured analysis — the two are not conflated

Violation of structure is grounds for **Canon rejection or demotion**.

## 8. Final Note

The Canon Construction Method exists to protect the Atlas from poetic drift, moral inflation, ideological capture, reductionism, authority substitution, and data-integrity drift between prose and structured scoring.

It also protects the Atlas from a more subtle failure: refusing to analyse the patterns that most need systematic analysis because they are low-quality patterns. The Atlas is the project's empirical observatory of value patterns at all quality levels. Its analytical contribution comes from the systematic application of the framework to whatever patterns are structurally real — including patterns of coercion, deception, capture, and ideological override that operate through the very mechanisms (enforcement, propagation, institutional power) that lower-quality readings of MOQ might wrongly take to disqualify them.

The method enables stable reference, cross-cultural analysis, disciplined disagreement, dimensional comparison across patterns, machine-extractable analytical views, and future expansion.

The method governs **how patterns are described and scored**, not **which patterns matter**. All structurally real patterns matter. Their quality is what the analysis articulates.

> *Canon constrains expression so that meaning can endure. Structure constrains scoring so that meaning can be compared. Eligibility ensures that analysis applies to real patterns; quality scoring ensures that analysis honestly distinguishes patterns that elevate coordination from patterns that degrade it. Field-relative scoring ensures that value effects are captured from each field's own perspective rather than collapsed into universal moral aggregates — because the same pattern produces different real value effects in different fields, and those differences are what the analysis exists to surface.*
